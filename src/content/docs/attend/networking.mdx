**Attend → Networking** turns on messaging, meeting requests, People to meet and contact exchange in the event app, sets when and where meetings happen, and is where you review what attendees report.

<Note>
Messages, meeting requests and People to meet only work between people listed in the attendee directory. Turn the directory on first in [**Mobile App → Sign-in & privacy**](/attend/mobile-app#sign-in-and-privacy). Contact exchange doesn't need the directory. Everything here is off until you turn it on.
</Note>

## Why use it

Meeting people is the reason many attendees come, and sponsors judge an event by the conversations it starts. Networking gives both a safe way to connect without swapping phone numbers: attendees reach only people who chose to be listed, anyone can block or report, and you can pause someone in one click.

## Choose what attendees can do

Under **Networking in the app**, turn on any of:

![Networking in the app with the Messages, Meeting requests, People to meet and Contact exchange switches turned on](/images/kb/attend-networking-features.png)

- **Messages** — one-to-one messages between attendees, and with sponsor and exhibitor teams.
- **Meeting requests** — attendees request a meeting with each other or a team; the other side accepts, declines or proposes another time.
- **People to meet** — suggests people who picked the same sessions, topics, speakers and sponsors, with the reason. Each attendee opts in; nothing is inferred. With [networking goals](#networking-goals), it also suggests people and exhibitors for what each attendee says they are here for.
- **Contact exchange** — attendees scan each other's badge in the app and both save the contact (below).

Attendees can turn off messages or meeting requests for themselves in the app.

## Networking goals

Networking goals let attendees say what they are here for, and the app suggests people and exhibitors to meet for it. You write up to six goals, such as **Buying an EHR**, **Raising a seed round** or **Finding my chapter**, under **Networking goals**. Each goal has a name, an optional emoji and two sets of rules:

- **Who should they meet?** — attendees of a registrant type, attendees whose answer to a registration question matches (an option, or typed text in any case), exhibitors with a showroom or product in a category, and attendees who picked another of your goals. Pair goals that look for each other, like **Buying an EHR** and **Selling an EHR**, by pointing each at the other.
- **Who is this goal for when they skip?** — registrant types and registration answers. Attendees who skip the question get this goal from their registration, so they still see suggestions.

A goal with no rules suggests nobody, and the card says so.

Goals show in the app only while **People to meet** and the attendee directory are both on and there is at least one goal. Then:

- After signing in, each attendee sees **What are you here for?** once and picks one or two goals, or skips. They can change their goals later from **Networking**.
- **Networking** shows a stack of suggested people and exhibitors, each with why: **Matches your goal**, **From your registration** (a goal read off their registration), or **Here for** (someone whose goal is looking for people like them). Each card has **Skip**, **Message** and **Request meeting** (the last two while those are on). A skipped card stays away until the next day of the event.
- Ask answers "Who should I meet?" from the same suggestions.

Suggestions only ever include attendees listed in the directory, never anyone hidden, paused or blocked, and never the attendee's own colleagues (the same company). A reason names a goal, never what someone answered on their registration.

To check your rules, choose one or two goals under **Preview as an attendee**. You see the people and exhibitors an attendee who picked them would get, from your real listed attendees. The card also shows how many attendees picked each goal.

## Set up meetings

- **Meeting length** and the hours meetings run — **Meetings from** and **Meetings until** — on every event day, in the event's time zone. Attendees pick a free slot in that grid.
- **Meeting places** — lounges, meeting rooms or tables attendees can pick. Link one to a session room so it shows on the venue map.
- **Team meetings at the booth** — a meeting with an exhibitor can be at its booth from the venue map.
- **Attendees can name their own place** — lets attendees type a place, like a café nearby.

Confirmed meetings appear in each person's **My schedule** and their subscribed calendar.

## Let attendees swap contacts

With **Contact exchange** on, an attendee opens **Scan a badge** from **People I met** (or the **Scan** tab, where the app has one) and scans the QR code on another attendee's **Me → My badge**. Both of them save each other: the contact appears in each person's **People I met** (in the networking card on **Today** and **Me**), with where they met — the session they had on in **My schedule**, or the booth they were at — filled in. The person scanned gets a notification.

- **No directory needed.** Showing a badge in person is the consent, so contact exchange works even when the attendee directory is off.
- **Each attendee chooses what they share**, under **People you meet** on **My badge**. Name, photo, job title and company always go with the contact, like a printed badge. **Share my email** is off until they turn it on; **Share my LinkedIn profile** is on. Turning one off later hides it from everyone who already saved them. **Let attendees save my contact** off means nobody can save them, and they can't save anyone.
- **People I met** lists everyone they swapped with, with search, tags and their own private notes. From a contact they can **Save to contacts** on the phone, **Connect on LinkedIn**, send an email if it is shared, or message and request a meeting when those are on. They can export the list as a CSV.
- **Scans work without signal** and are sent when the phone reconnects.
- **Block** and **Report** work on contacts too, and a paused attendee can't swap contacts. Turning contact exchange off stops new swaps; contacts already saved stay in People I met.

Contact exchange is for attendees swapping with each other. Exhibitors capture leads with [lead retrieval](/attend/leads), which reads the same badge.

## How sponsor and exhibitor teams take part

Every attendee can message or request a meeting with a sponsor or exhibitor team (except their own). Everyone registered on that team reads and answers for it, and whoever accepts a meeting hosts it. Teams come from the contacts on each account in **CRM**.

## Who can reach whom

- Only listed attendees of this event can be reached, and you must be listed yourself to start a conversation or a meeting.
- Blocking works both ways and the blocked person isn't told.
- Messages are plain text. There are limits on how many messages and new conversations one person can send in a day, which stop spam without getting in the way of real conversation.
- Push alerts for new messages and meeting requests never include a name or the message text.

## Review reports

When an attendee reports someone or a message, it appears under **Reports** with the reason — **Spam or selling**, **Harassment**, **Inappropriate**, **Fake profile** or **Other** — and any note. Filter by **Open**, **Acted on**, **Dismissed** or **All**, then:

- **Dismiss** the report,
- **Remove message** to take the reported message down, or
- **Pause** the person's networking, so they can't start new conversations or meetings.

**Paused attendees** lists everyone paused; **Resume networking** lifts it.

<Warning>
You never see message text here, only counts and reports. Attendee conversations stay between the people in them.
</Warning>

## Numbers

The tiles at the top show **Messages** (and how many in the last day), **Conversations**, **Meetings confirmed** (and how many are waiting for an answer), how many attendees are **In People to meet**, **Contacts exchanged** (and how many people are scanning), and **Open reports** with the number of blocks.
