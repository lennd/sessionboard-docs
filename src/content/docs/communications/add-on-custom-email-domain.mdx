## What the add-on changes

Out of the box, every email Sessionboard sends for you — a send from Contacts, a campaign, a portal digest, a submission confirmation — comes from `no-reply@notify.sessionboard.com`, with your event name as the sender name. A custom email domain replaces that address with one on a domain you own, such as `speakers@yourconference.org`.

Once it is set up:

- A **Send From** field appears on your [email templates](/settings/email-templates) and a **Send from:** field in the [send composer](/communications/create-send-emails), listing addresses on your authorized domain.
- [Automated emails](/communications/automated-emails) — confirmations, portal notifications, evaluator reminders — go out from your address too.
- Replies still go wherever **Replies sent to** points, so a shared inbox keeps working as before.

Recipients see mail from a domain they recognize, which reads as legitimate and is less likely to be filtered. Authentication proves to receiving servers that Sessionboard is allowed to send as you; it does not change the sending infrastructure — see [Deliverability & allowlisting](#deliverability--allowlisting) below.

## Email Domain Requirements

Below, are the requirements for an email domain before it can be utilized within Sessionboard:
- No typos
- The domain must be corporate and not Outlook, Yahoo, or Gmail.
- Once a domain has been set up within Sessionboard, the email utilized can not be changed.

<Note>
The process to set up your domain with your event can take about a week.
</Note>

## Set-Up/Authentication Process
1. Event Admin sends an email to support@sessionboard.com with the email address they are requesting to use.
2. Sessionboard provides the event admin with DNS (Domain Name Resolution) records to configure.
3. From your domain provider's website, find the page or area that has your domain or domain records (below are some instructions for the most common domain providers).
   1. [Google Domains](https://support.google.com/domains/answer/3290350#zippy=%2Cadd-a-resource-record)
   2. [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/Route53/latest/DeveloperGuide/resource-record-sets-creating.html)
   3. [Azure](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/a-records-cname-and-using-dns-with-windows-azure-web-sites-waws/)
   4. [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/manage-dns-records/how-to/create-dns-records)
   5. [GoDaddy](https://ie.godaddy.com/help/add-a-cname-record-19236)
4. Once you’ve found this area you are ready for the next step, adding the DNS records to your domain.
   1. [Troubleshooting Sender Authentication](https://docs.sendgrid.com/ui/account-and-settings/troubleshooting-sender-authentication)
5. Sessionboard verifies that the DNS records were set up correctly and adds the preferred event email to the event.

## Check your domain's authentication status

DNS records can be edited, expire, or be replaced long after your domain was first authenticated, and when that happens every send from your address starts being rejected. Sessionboard rechecks each custom sending domain on a schedule rather than trusting the answer it was given at setup.

The status view sits on the organization's **Settings → Admin** page, which only Sessionboard staff can open — ask your Customer Success Manager or support@sessionboard.com to check it for you. What they see under the system organization email address:

- **&lt;your domain> is authenticated. Checked &lt;when>.** — everything is in order. The timestamp matters as much as the result: a pass from months ago is not evidence that the domain works today.
- **&lt;your domain> is not authenticated with SendGrid** — mail from this domain is being rejected right now. The failing DNS records are listed underneath, each with the host and the value it should have, so you can hand them straight to whoever manages your DNS.
- **Last checked &lt;when> — that is too long ago to rely on**, or **has not been checked yet** — the status is unknown rather than good. Treat it as unverified.

Sessionboard also alerts us when a working domain stops validating, once per breakage, so a domain that fails overnight is noticed rather than discovered by a customer mid-campaign.

Organizations sending from the default `notify.sessionboard.com` address have no domain of their own to break and see nothing here.

## Deliverability & Allowlisting

Authenticating your domain tells receiving servers that Sessionboard is allowed to send as your address. It does not override filtering on the recipient's side. Organizations with strict filters, commonly universities, controlled work networks, government agencies, and hospitals, may occasionally filter your email as potentially malicious, even if it's not.

A custom email domain changes the sending address, not the sending infrastructure. All Sessionboard email is sent through SendGrid from the same Sessionboard sending IP addresses.

**If anyone reports missing email, ask their IT team to allowlist**:

- `159.183.10.230`
- `159.183.207.219`
- `159.183.47.254`

Also have them allowlist your custom domain sending address: example@yourcompany.com

Allowlisting is per recipient organization. Setting up your custom domain does not allowlist you with any third party, and allowlisting done by one organization does not carry over to another.

For recipient-side troubleshooting, see: [Why am I not receiving emails?](/faq/why-am-i-not-receiving-emails)

## Frequently Asked Questions
1. **Why do I need to configure the DNS records?**
   1. The DNS records are used to map the alias (custom email domain) to the Sessionboard domain. Once this is completed on your end, we then ensure the alias is authenticated with our email provider to ensure deliverability. If the DNS records are not added to your domain and we try to send emails from your domain, the email will be flagged as spam and affect both of our deliverability scores. Verifying the domain creates a connection that indicates trust between both parties that we will not try to impersonate you or send unauthorized communications. Because we don't have access to your domain we ask you to add the records we provide to your domain to show that we are authenticated. Once that is done we have to verify it on our end. Here is a reference link that explains the process: https://docs.sendgrid.com/ui/account-and-settings/how-to-set-up-domain-authentication#key-terminology. ​
2. **How can I retrieve the SPF record entries?**
   1. Sessionboard uses automated security which handles the SPF/DKIM so they stay automatically updated: https://docs.sendgrid.com/ui/account-and-settings/spf-records#spf-and-sender-authentication
3. **We set up our custom domain but some recipients still are not receiving emails. Why?**
   1. Domain authentication proves you authorized Sessionboard to send as your address. It does not stop the recipient's mail server from filtering the message. If a specific organization is blocking your email, their IT team needs to allowlist your sending address and include:sendgrid.net. Allowlisting applies only to the organization that does it, so it does not extend to other recipients or organizations.
