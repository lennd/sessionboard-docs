A **disclosure** is a dated statement from a person about their financial relationships and conflicts of interest — the form accredited CE providers collect from every faculty member, and the shorter conflict-of-interest check many associations ask of speakers, abstract reviewers and award judges. Disclosures lets you build that form once, send it to people as a private link they complete without logging in, and see on every contact's profile whether their disclosure is current, expiring, expired or missing.

<Note>
Disclosures is an Early Access **Preview** you can turn on yourself from [Early Access](/get-started/early-access). It is **org-wide**: turn it on from **Preview** at the bottom of the organization sidebar and every event inherits it. It does not need Lessons & Credits.

Once it is on, **Disclosures** appears under **CRM** in the organization sidebar, and every contact profile — organization and event — gains a **Disclosure** tab.
</Note>

## Why use it

- **One record per person, not per event.** A disclosure lives on the contact, with a rolling window (24 months by default). A speaker who disclosed for your spring meeting is still current for the autumn one — Sessionboard tells you, and you do not ask twice.
- **A link, not a login.** The request email carries a private, single-use link. The person opens it, answers, attests and is done. They are never asked to create an account.
- **Nothing on file is revealed.** The form shows questions only. It never prefills what the person said last time and never shows what staff recorded, so a forwarded email cannot leak anyone's relationships.
- **The status follows the person everywhere.** Contact lists get a **Disclosure** column, session participant lists get a chip, and the contact profile shows the full history — so the question "who still owes us a disclosure?" is a glance, not a spreadsheet.

## Set up a form

Forms live on the **Forms** tab of **Disclosures**, at the organization level, where every event can use them.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Disclosures">
In the organization sidebar, open **CRM → Disclosures**.
</Step>
<Step title="Create from a template">
Click **New form** and pick a template. Each one is a complete, working form you can edit:

| Template | Window | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ACCME standard financial disclosure** (recommended) | 24 months | Accredited CE: relationships with ineligible companies over the past 24 months, off-label discussion, attestation. |
| **Simple conflict of interest** | 12 months | Speakers at non-accredited events: do you have a conflict, tell us about it, attest. |
| **Reviewer / committee conflict of interest** | 12 months | Abstract reviewers, judges and committee members: relationships with applicants or institutions, and what they should be recused from. |
| **Start from scratch** | — | Just the attestation. Add your own questions. |

A form created from a template is active right away; **Start from scratch** opens as a draft until you publish it.
</Step>
<Step title="Edit the questions">
The editor works like the [submission form](/sessions/submission-forms) and [survey](/evaluations/surveys) builders: add, reorder and remove questions, set each one required or optional, and show a question only when an earlier answer matches (for example, the relationships table only when **Any relationships to disclose?** is **Yes**).

Two question types are specific to disclosures:

- **Financial relationships** — a table where the person lists each company, the nature of the relationship (consultant, speaker's bureau, research grant, stock, employment and so on), an optional amount range and dates. It can offer an **I have no financial relationships to disclose** checkbox instead of rows.
- **Attestation** — the statement the person must tick before submitting. Every form has exactly one; its wording is yours.

The **Introduction** at the top explains why you are asking, **Attestation text** is the statement they tick, and the **Look-back window (months)** sets how long a submission stays current. A live preview on the right shows what the person will see.
</Step>
<Step title="Set a default">
On the Forms list, choose **Make default** on the form you send most. The default is what a request uses when nobody picks a form, and what the **Send disclosure request** dialog preselects.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Duplicate** makes a copy to edit; **Archive** retires a form without touching the disclosures already collected with it.

## Send a request

A request is an email with a private link to one form. Send one from a single contact or to many at once.

**From a contact.** Open the contact's profile and the **Disclosure** tab, then **Send request** (it reads **Send again** while a request is already out). In the event contact sidebar, **Send Disclosure Request** is also in the actions menu.

**In bulk.** Select contacts in the event **Contacts** list or the organization directory, then choose **Send Disclosure Request** from the bulk actions (in the organization directory the button is **Send disclosure**). The dialog shows how many people it will go to.

**From the Requests tab.** On **Disclosures → Requests**, **Send request** opens a picker: search people by name or email, tick the recipients, and **Continue** to the same dialog. People without an email address are listed but cannot be selected — the request is an emailed link.

In the dialog:

- **Form** — the organization default is preselected. Change it to any other active form.
- **Note to include** — optional text added to the email, such as a deadline.
- **Skip anyone whose disclosure is current** — shown when you selected more than one person. Leave it on and only people with no disclosure, or an expired one, get the email; the confirmation tells you how many were sent and how many skipped.

Each link is **single-use** and expires after the period in **Settings** (30 days by default). The **Requests** tab lists every request with its status — pending, completed, expired, revoked — and lets you **Resend** (which issues a fresh link and retires the old one) or **Revoke** a link you no longer want to work.

<Warning>
Anyone holding the link can complete the form, which is why it never shows what is already on file. If a request email went to the wrong address, **Revoke** it from the Requests tab and send a new one.
</Warning>

## What the person sees

The email, branded for your organization, explains who is asking and carries one button. The link opens a page with your introduction, the questions, and the attestation. Conditional questions appear as the person answers. When they submit, the page confirms it and the link stops working.

Opening a link that has already been used shows **Thanks — your disclosure has been received**; an expired one shows **This link has expired** and to ask the organizer for a new request; a revoked one shows **This link isn't valid**.

## Read a contact's status

Every contact in an organization with Disclosures on has one of four states, computed from their most recent attestation and the form's window:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Current** | Attested within the window. |
| **Expiring soon** | Current, but the window ends within 60 days. |
| **Expired** | The window has passed. |
| **No disclosure** | Never attested. |

A **Requested** marker on top of any state means a request is out and unanswered.

**On the profile.** The **Disclosure** tab shows the state, the open request (with **Resend** and **Revoke**), the relationships currently on file, and a **History** of every submission with its date, form and how it was made — by link, from the portal, inside a submission form, or recorded by staff.

**In lists.** The event **Contacts** list and the organization directory show a **Disclosure** column with a **Disclosure** filter above the table — **Needed**, **Expiring soon**, **Current**, **None on file** — so "who still owes us one" is a filter, not a scroll; the participants list on a session shows a chip beside each name. Use the **Requests** tab's status filter to find everyone with an open or expired request.

**Record one yourself.** When a disclosure arrives on paper or by email, click **Record** on the Disclosure tab, pick the form and enter the answers. It is stored as a submission made by staff, with your name on it, and counts toward the contact's state like any other.

## When a disclosure is needed

An expired or missing disclosure is not, by itself, a problem — a contact you never put on a program does not owe you one. The badge reads **Needed** only when the disclosure is stale **and** something currently asks for it. Hover the badge (or read the **Disclosure** tab) and it says why:

| Reason | Who it covers |
| --- | --- |
| On a session that offers credit | Any participant on a session with a credit designation (Lessons & Credits). |
| All participants must disclose | Every participant on every session, when **Require a disclosure from every session participant** is on in Settings. |
| Submitted to a Call for Papers that collects disclosures | Submitters and participants on a [submission form](/sessions/submission-forms) with **Collect disclosures** on. |
| Reviews on a plan that requires one | Reviewers on an [evaluation plan](/evaluations/evaluation-plans#create-a-plan) with **Require a disclosure from reviewers** on. |
| Judges a round that requires one | Judges on an [Awards round](/awards/managing-submissions-reviewers-rounds#understanding-evaluation-rounds) with **Require a disclosure from judges** on. |
| A request is open | Anyone you have sent a request to and who has not answered. |

A contact whose disclosure has lapsed but who none of these apply to shows **Expired** or **No disclosure** with the note *Not required right now* — nobody is chased, nothing is flagged.

<Note>
Everything that happens on its own — the **Needed** badge, the portal task and banner, automatic requests and reminders, agenda warnings, public display — applies only while **Disclosures are on for this organization** at the top of Settings. Turn it off and all of it pauses; forms, past submissions and requests you send by hand are kept. The switch stays after Disclosures leaves Early Access.
</Note>

## Automatic requests and reminders

Once you say who needs one, Sessionboard can do the asking.

- **Send a request automatically when someone starts needing one** — every Monday, anyone newly flagged **Needed** with no open request gets a private link to your default form by email.
- **Send a request automatically when a disclosure is about to expire** — inside the last 60 days of the window, anyone without an open request gets a link to renew.
- **Remind every (days)** — an open request gets a fresh link on this cadence, up to three times, then is left alone. Leave it blank to send no reminders.
- **Message in automatic emails** — plain text shown above the link in every automatic request and reminder, such as who to contact with questions. Requests you send by hand keep their own note.

Automatic requests appear on the **Requests** tab with the source **Automatic**, and the **Reminders** column shows how many went out and when the last one did. Nobody is asked twice for the same reason inside 30 days, and nobody with a current disclosure is asked at all.

The **Disclosures** tile on the organization and event dashboards keeps the running count — **Needed** first, then **Expiring soon**, **Open requests** and **Completed (30 days)** — with the time of the last check. It appears only while Disclosures is on.

## In the speaker portal

A participant who needs a disclosure sees it in their [portal](/portals/portals-101): a banner on **Home** — *A disclosure is needed* or *Your disclosure is about to expire* — and a **Disclosure** task alongside their other tasks. **Complete disclosure** opens the form in a panel; they answer, attest and submit without leaving the portal, and the task clears. The participant-side walkthrough is [Complete a disclosure](/portals/complete-a-disclosure).

The portal never shows what is already on file: a renewal is a fresh form, the same as a link. A submission from the portal shows on the contact's **History** as made *from the portal*.

## Reviewers and judges

Reviewers and judges can owe a conflict-of-interest disclosure too.

- Evaluation plans — under **Conflict moderation**, turn on **Require a disclosure from reviewers**. Every reviewer on the plan is flagged **Needed** until they have a current one.
- Awards rounds — on the round's settings, the **Disclosures** card has **Require a disclosure from judges**.

Both switches appear only when Disclosures is on. Flagged reviewers and judges are asked automatically like everyone else when automatic requests are on, and the **Reviewer / committee conflict of interest** template is the form made for them.

## Warnings on the agenda

A session with a participant who still needs a disclosure shows an orange **warning** in [the agenda's Conflicts tab](/sessions/agenda#conflicts), tagged **Disclosure**, naming the participant and the reason. It is a warning, never a conflict: it does not stop you publishing the agenda, and it clears on its own once the disclosure comes in.

## Mitigations

When a participant on a credit-designated session **does** report financial relationships, accreditors expect you to record how the conflict was handled. The **Mitigations** tab on **Disclosures** (organization and event) lists every such participant, those still needing a decision first, with the number of relationships on file and the decision recorded for that session.

**Record decision** opens a dialog with the decisions accreditors recognise — **Not relevant to content**, **Divested**, **Recused from topic**, **Peer review of content**, **Attested no influence**, **Other** (which needs notes) — and a notes field that goes into the accreditor export. **Revise** re-opens a recorded decision; the export shows who last changed it and when.

If you add a participant with relationships and no decision to a credit-designated session, the add is refused with **Mitigation needed first** and a link to the tab; a participant with no current disclosure gets **Disclosure needed first** and a link to Requests. Record the decision or send the request, then add them.

## Export for your accreditor

**Export** on the **Requests** and **Mitigations** tabs produces one file — CSV or Excel — for the organization or for the current event: every participant on a credit-designated session with their role, disclosure state and why it is needed, the date attested and when it expires, each current relationship (company and nature, never amounts), the mitigation decision with notes and who recorded it, and any open request with its reminder count. It runs as an export job and lands in the task dock like every other export.

## Show disclosures on your website

Turn on **Show current disclosures on your website and widgets** under **Public display** in Settings and the session pages in your [embeds](/site/publishing-surfaces#embeds) list each participant's disclosed relationships beneath their name — the company and the nature of the relationship only, never an amount range or free text. It reads **No financial relationships disclosed** for a participant with a current disclosure and nothing to report, and shows nothing at all for anyone without a current disclosure, so nobody is singled out. It is off until you turn it on.

## Collect disclosures in a submission form

A [submission form](/sessions/submission-forms) can collect disclosures from the people it adds to a session, so a speaker discloses as part of proposing a talk.

On the form's **Participants** step, turn on **Collect disclosures** and pick the form. Two things then happen:

- **The submitter completes their own disclosure inline**, as a section at the end of the Participants step — when they are listed as a participant themselves. Leave **Ask the submitter inline when they are a participant** off to send them a request like everyone else instead.
- **Every other participant gets a request email** after the submission goes in, with the link to the same form.

Anyone whose disclosure is already current is not asked again.

## Settings

The **Settings** tab (organization level) starts with the master switch — **Disclosures are on for this organization** — and groups everything else beneath it. While the switch is off the groups are greyed out and nothing automatic runs.

**Who needs a disclosure**

- **Require a disclosure from every session participant** — flag every participant on every session, not only those on sessions that offer credit. Participants on credit-designated sessions, people with an open request, Call for Papers submitters on forms that collect disclosures, and reviewers or judges on plans and rounds that require one are always flagged.

**Automatic requests and reminders**

- **Send a request automatically when someone starts needing one** and **Send a request automatically when a disclosure is about to expire** — the weekly sweep described in [Automatic requests and reminders](#automatic-requests-and-reminders).
- **Message in automatic emails** — the plain-text note above the link in automatic requests and reminders.
- **Remind every (days)** — the reminder cadence for open requests, up to three reminders; blank for none.

**Requests you send**

- **Default form** — used by automatic requests, by requests sent without picking a form, and by Call for Papers forms that collect disclosures without naming one.
- **Disclosure valid for (months)** — how long a disclosure stays current on forms that do not set their own window.
- **Links expire after (days)** — how long a request link works (30 by default).
- **Skip people whose disclosure is current** — the default position of the bulk-send toggle, also applied to submission-form requests.

**Public display**

- **Show current disclosures on your website and widgets** — see [Show disclosures on your website](#show-disclosures-on-your-website).

## Permissions

Seeing and sending disclosures follows the **Contacts** permissions: anyone who can view contacts sees the Disclosure tab, status column and Requests tab; creating and editing forms, sending requests and recording disclosures need the matching **Disclosures** permissions on the organization role. Turning the Preview on or off needs **Manage Early Access**.
