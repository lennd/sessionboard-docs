A **disclosure** is a dated statement from a person about their financial relationships and conflicts of interest — the form accredited CE providers collect from every faculty member, and the shorter conflict-of-interest check many associations ask of speakers, abstract reviewers and award judges. Disclosures lets you build that form once, send it to people as a private link they complete without logging in, and see on every contact's profile whether their disclosure is current, expiring, expired or missing.

<Note>
Disclosures is an Early Access **Preview** you can turn on yourself from [Early Access](/get-started/early-access). It is **org-wide**: turn it on from **Preview** at the bottom of the organization sidebar and every event inherits it. It does not need Lessons & Credits.

Once it is on, **Disclosures** appears under **CRM** in the organization sidebar and under **Contacts** in each event, and every contact profile gains a **Disclosure** tab.
</Note>

## Why use it

- **One record per person, not per event.** A disclosure lives on the contact, with a rolling window (24 months by default). A speaker who disclosed for your spring meeting is still current for the autumn one — Sessionboard tells you, and you do not ask twice.
- **A link, not a login.** The request email carries a private, single-use link. The person opens it, answers, attests and is done. They are never asked to create an account.
- **Nothing on file is revealed.** The form shows questions only. It never prefills what the person said last time and never shows what staff recorded, so a forwarded email cannot leak anyone's relationships.
- **The status follows the person everywhere.** Contact lists get a **Disclosure** column, session participant lists get a chip, and the contact profile shows the full history — so the question "who still owes us a disclosure?" is a glance, not a spreadsheet.

## Set up a form

Forms live on the **Forms** tab of **Disclosures**. You can keep them at the organization level, where every event can use them, or create event-specific ones inside an event.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Disclosures">
In the organization sidebar, open **CRM → Disclosures**. (Inside an event it is **Contacts → Disclosures**; forms you create there belong to that event only.)
</Step>
<Step title="Create from a template">
Click **New form** and pick a template. Each one is a complete, working form you can edit:

| Template | Window | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ACCME standard financial disclosure** (recommended) | 24 months | Accredited CE: relationships with ineligible companies over the past 24 months, off-label discussion, attestation. |
| **Simple conflict of interest** | 12 months | Speakers at non-accredited events: do you have a conflict, tell us about it, attest. |
| **Reviewer / committee conflict of interest** | 12 months | Abstract reviewers, judges and committee members: relationships with applicants or institutions, and what they should be recused from. |
| **Start from scratch** | — | Just the attestation. Add your own questions. |

A form created from a template is active right away; **Start from scratch** opens as a draft until you publish it.
</Step>
<Step title="Edit the questions">
The editor works like the [submission form](/sessions/submission-forms) and [survey](/evaluations/surveys) builders: add, reorder and remove questions, set each one required or optional, and show a question only when an earlier answer matches (for example, the relationships table only when **Any relationships to disclose?** is **Yes**).

Two question types are specific to disclosures:

- **Financial relationships** — a table where the person lists each company, the nature of the relationship (consultant, speaker's bureau, research grant, stock, employment and so on), an optional amount range and dates. It can offer an **I have no financial relationships to disclose** checkbox instead of rows.
- **Attestation** — the statement the person must tick before submitting. Every form has exactly one; its wording is yours.

The **Introduction** at the top explains why you are asking, **Attestation text** is the statement they tick, and the **Look-back window (months)** sets how long a submission stays current. A live preview on the right shows what the person will see.
</Step>
<Step title="Set a default">
On the Forms list, choose **Make default** on the form you send most. The default is what a request uses when nobody picks a form, and what the **Send disclosure request** dialog preselects.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
Inside an event, organization forms appear read-only with a **Copy to this event** action. Copy one when a single event needs different wording; leave it alone when the organization form is fine.
</Tip>

**Duplicate** makes a copy to edit; **Archive** retires a form without touching the disclosures already collected with it.

## Send a request

A request is an email with a private link to one form. Send one from a single contact or to many at once.

**From a contact.** Open the contact's profile and the **Disclosure** tab, then **Send request** (it reads **Send again** while a request is already out). In the event contact sidebar, **Send Disclosure Request** is also in the actions menu.

**In bulk.** Select contacts in the event **Contacts** list or the organization directory, then choose **Send Disclosure Request** from the bulk actions (in the organization directory the button is **Send disclosure**). The dialog shows how many people it will go to.

In the dialog:

- **Form** — the organization default is preselected. Change it to any active form at this event or the organization.
- **Note to include** — optional text added to the email, such as a deadline.
- **Skip anyone whose disclosure is current** — shown when you selected more than one person. Leave it on and only people with no disclosure, or an expired one, get the email; the confirmation tells you how many were sent and how many skipped.

Each link is **single-use** and expires after the period in **Settings** (30 days by default). The **Requests** tab lists every request with its status — pending, completed, expired, revoked — and lets you **Resend** (which issues a fresh link and retires the old one) or **Revoke** a link you no longer want to work.

<Warning>
Anyone holding the link can complete the form, which is why it never shows what is already on file. If a request email went to the wrong address, **Revoke** it from the Requests tab and send a new one.
</Warning>

## What the person sees

The email, branded for your organization, explains who is asking and carries one button. The link opens a page with your introduction, the questions, and the attestation. Conditional questions appear as the person answers. When they submit, the page confirms it and the link stops working.

Opening a link that has already been used shows **Thanks — your disclosure has been received**; an expired one shows **This link has expired** and to ask the organizer for a new request; a revoked one shows **This link isn't valid**.

## Read a contact's status

Every contact in an organization with Disclosures on has one of four states, computed from their most recent attestation and the form's window:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Current** | Attested within the window. |
| **Expiring** | Current, but the window ends within 60 days. |
| **Expired** | The window has passed. |
| **Missing** | Never attested. |

A **Pending** marker on top of any state means a request is out and unanswered.

**On the profile.** The **Disclosure** tab shows the state, the open request (with **Resend** and **Revoke**), the relationships currently on file, and a **History** of every submission with its date, form and how it was made — by link, inside a submission form, or recorded by staff.

**In lists.** The event **Contacts** list and the organization directory show a **Disclosure** column; the participants list on a session shows a chip beside each name. Use the **Requests** tab's status filter to find everyone with a pending or expired request.

**Record one yourself.** When a disclosure arrives on paper or by email, click **Record** on the Disclosure tab, pick the form and enter the answers. It is stored as a submission made by staff, with your name on it, and counts toward the contact's state like any other.

## Collect disclosures in a submission form

A [submission form](/sessions/submission-forms) can collect disclosures from the people it adds to a session, so a speaker discloses as part of proposing a talk.

On the form's **Participants** step, turn on **Collect disclosures** and pick the form. Two things then happen:

- **The submitter completes their own disclosure inline**, as a section at the end of the Participants step — when they are listed as a participant themselves. Leave **Ask the submitter inline when they are a participant** off to send them a request like everyone else instead.
- **Every other participant gets a request email** after the submission goes in, with the link to the same form.

Anyone whose disclosure is already current is not asked again.

## Settings

The **Settings** tab (organization level) holds the defaults every request and form inherits:

- **Default form** — used when a request does not name one.
- **Disclosure valid for (months)** — how long a disclosure stays current on forms that do not set their own window.
- **Links expire after (days)** — how long a request link works (30 by default).
- **Skip people whose disclosure is current** — the default position of the bulk-send toggle, also applied to submission-form requests.

## Permissions

Seeing and sending disclosures follows the **Contacts** permissions: anyone who can view contacts sees the Disclosure tab, status column and Requests tab; creating and editing forms, sending requests and recording disclosures need the matching **Disclosures** permissions on the organization role. Turning the Preview on or off needs **Manage Early Access**.
