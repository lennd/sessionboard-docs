A skill can never do more than you could do in Sessionboard yourself. That is not a promise the skill makes: it is enforced by Sessionboard on every call, whichever assistant sends it.

## Every action runs as you

- **You sign in, not a key.** The assistant connects with OAuth: you sign in to Sessionboard, pick the organization and choose **Authorize**. There is no shared API token.
- **Your role applies.** Each call is checked against your Sessionboard role and permissions, the same as the screens you use: what you can see in Contacts, the Awards programs you manage, the Learning permissions you hold, whether you can change an event. A skill that reaches something your role cannot gets the same refusal you would.
- **Your scopes apply.** At sign-in you choose what the assistant may do. Without write access it can read but not change anything, whatever the skill says.
- **One organization per connection.** The assistant works in the organization you authorized and nowhere else. Regions are separate: a connection to the US server cannot see a European organization.

## It asks before it changes anything

Every skill reads first. Before it creates, changes, publishes, sends or deletes anything, it shows you exactly what will change (names, counts, recipients, dates) and waits for your yes in the conversation. It never takes an earlier answer or a plan as that yes.

Assistants add their own check: tools that change data are marked as such, so Claude and ChatGPT ask you before they run one.

## Some actions wait for a person in Sessionboard

Anything that reaches people outside your team, or goes live, is not done from the chat even after you say yes. Sessionboard answers that it needs approval and gives a link. A person with the right permission opens it in Sessionboard and approves it, and it then runs as them. This covers, among others:

- opening a call for speakers and publishing an agenda
- opening Awards nominations, inviting Reviewers, reminders and announcing Winners
- publishing courses to learners, changing what learners pay, and sending credit to CE Broker
- launching campaigns, sending presenter kits, publishing pages and Sites, approving ad spend and sharing results with a sponsor

Choosing Awards Winners is never done by an assistant: a person does it in Sessionboard.

## What administrators control

- **AI features for the organization.** When AI is off, no assistant can connect. An organization admin can check under **Organization Settings → API Tokens**: a banner reads *AI features are disabled for this organization* when it is off. Contact your account manager to change it.
- **Who can connect.** Only roles that include **Connect AI assistants** can authorize an assistant. Organization administrators have it; grant it to other roles in your organization's role settings.
- **What each person can do.** Roles and permissions in Sessionboard decide what a connected assistant can read and change, so tightening a role tightens the assistant too.
- **Attendees.** The attendee connection, which the [attendee skill](/skills/my-event-day) uses, is off for every event until you switch it on, and it can only read the attendee's own event.
- **Claude for your whole team.** On Claude Team and Enterprise plans, an Owner decides whether skills are allowed and can add Sessionboard skills for everyone (see [Install skills](/skills/install)).

## What is recorded

Changes an assistant makes through the Marketing, Awards, Learning and CRM tools, calls for speakers and agendas, and event pages and emails are recorded in Sessionboard with the assistant's name and the person who asked, beside changes made on screen.

<Warning>
What the assistant reads can end up in its conversation history. Results are not masked: an assistant that reads contact details gets them, as you would. Connect only assistants your organization trusts, and grant write access only to people who need it.
</Warning>
