A sponsor in Sessionboard is a **group** — the company record, with its tier, logo, website and description — and the **contacts** attached to it, each with their own portal login. Most sponsors arrive through the [sponsor intake form](/sponsors-exhibitors/sponsor-intake-form), which creates the group and its first contact in one go. This article covers doing it yourself: adding a group, attaching people, and letting them in.

<Note>
Sponsors is part of the Sponsors & Exhibitors module. If **CRM → Sponsors** is missing from your sidebar, ask [support@sessionboard.com](mailto:support@sessionboard.com) to enable it for your event.
</Note>

Exhibitors and sponsors. Speakers are people. Exhibitors and sponsors are companies, and a company brings a booth, a logo, a contract, and usually more than one person who needs to log in. So Sessionboard treats them as groups. A group is the company record, and contacts attach to it. Both live in the CRM section of the sidebar, exhibitors and sponsors each with their own list and their own intake form.

All exhibitors. One row per company. Name, the primary contact, which portal the group has been assigned, then the profile fields, website, address, phone, description, logo, and social links. Same grid as everywhere else, so saved views, columns, sort and filter all work the way you'd expect. The second tab is the useful one, groups with no primary contact yet, which is your list of companies nobody can log in for.

Sponsors is the same list with one extra column, tier. Platinum, gold, silver, whatever your prospectus calls them. Filter by tier and save the view, and you've got all gold sponsors as a one-click list you can email, text, or assign tasks to. The pencil at the start of a row opens the profile.

The sponsor profile. Logo, name, and which portal they're on across the top, and six tabs. Profile is the company itself, tier, description, contact information, social links, and any custom fields you've added, here a booth number and whether the contract is signed. Every value is editable in place. Notes and activity are your team's history with them, messages is what's been sent, and program is the events they're part of.

CRM is the people. This company has one contact so far, marked primary, and add attaches more, either someone already in your contacts or a new person. Everyone attached shares the group. Each contact gets their own login, and they all see the same tasks, files and information, so a marketing manager and a booth lead can split the work without sharing a password.

Actions is where you act on the group. Send an email or a text to its people, copy the portal link, view the portal as they'd see it, and manage portal access. That dialog lists everyone attached who can't log in yet, and give portal access sends them their invitation. When a sponsor writes to say a colleague needs to get in, this is the whole job, add the contact, give access, done.

Tiers. Rename these to match your levels or add new ones, and the count shows how many sponsors sit in each. Tiers matter beyond the label. A portal can be filtered to a tier, so gold sponsors see a task to upload their sponsored session slides and silver sponsors never do. Emails can go to one tier. And if levels change after a sale, an import updates a hundred sponsors at once.

The intake form is how sponsors put themselves in. Four steps to set up. Welcome and terms is what they see first. Group information is the company fields you want filled in. Contact information is the person filling it in, who becomes the primary contact. And notifications decides who on your team hears about it and what the confirmation email says. Copy link gives you the address to put in your sponsor contract or confirmation email. When a sponsor completes it, the group and the contact are created and they're in the portal.

## The sponsor list

**CRM → Sponsors** shows one row per company: **Name**, **Tier**, **Primary Contact**, **Assigned Portal**, then the profile fields — description, logo, website, address, phone and social links. It is the same grid as your other lists, so **Saved Views**, **Columns**, **Sort** and **Filter** all apply, and selecting rows opens the bar with **Edit**, **Send**, **Assign**, **More** and **Delete**. The **No Primary Contacts** tab lists the companies nobody can log in for yet.

![The Sponsors list with Name, Tier, Primary Contact, Assigned Portal and profile columns, and the All Sponsors and No Primary Contacts tabs](/images/kb/sponsors-list.png)

## Add a sponsor group

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add Sponsor">
    Click **Add Sponsor** at the top right of the list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Search first">
    The panel starts with a search across your organization, so a company that already exists from another event is attached rather than duplicated. Type the name; matches are grouped into sponsors already in this event and sponsors elsewhere in the organization. Tick one or more and click **Add to Event**.

    ![The Add Sponsor panel with its organization-wide search and the Add new sponsor button](/images/kb/sponsors-add-search.png)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add new sponsor">
    If it is a new company, click **Add new sponsor**. **New Sponsor Details** asks for the **Name**, an optional **Tier**, **Website** and **Description**, plus a logo. Which fields appear here is the **Group Fields** layout under **Settings → Record Settings → Layouts**.

    ![The New Sponsor Details panel with logo, Name, Tier, Website and Description fields](/images/kb/sponsors-add-new.png)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create">
    Click **Create Sponsor** to save the group, or **Create & Add Contacts** to save it and go straight on to attaching people.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Add a contact to a sponsor

Open the sponsor with the pencil on its row and select the **CRM** tab. **Contacts** lists everyone attached, with the **Primary** badge on the person who receives the group's communications.

![The Sponsor Profile CRM tab listing the group's contacts with the Primary badge](/images/kb/sponsor-profile-crm.png)

1. Click **Add**. The search lists contacts already in the event — pick one to attach them.
2. For someone new, click **Create new contact**, then **Add new contact** on the **Add Contacts to …** panel.
3. Fill in **First Name**, **Last Name** and **Email** (mobile phone and biography are optional), tick **Set as primary contact for …** if this person should receive the group's emails, and click **Create Contact**.

![The New Contact Details panel opened from a sponsor's CRM tab, with the Set as primary contact checkbox](/images/kb/sponsor-add-contact.png)

The contact is created as a sponsor contact, attached to the group, and appears in **CRM → All Contacts** like anyone else. Every contact attached to a group shares its tasks, files and information in the portal, so a marketing manager and a booth lead can each log in without sharing a password.

## Give the contacts portal access

Creating a contact does not send anything. When they should be able to sign in, open the sponsor, click **Actions → Manage Portal Access**. **People without access** lists the attached contacts who cannot log in yet; select them and click **Give Portal Access**, then choose **Send Email** — with an optional personal message — or **Get Link** to copy the invitation link and send it yourself.

![The Manage Portal Access dialog for a sponsor, listing people without access and the Give Portal Access button](/images/kb/sponsor-portal-access.png)

The same **Actions** menu has **Send Email**, **Send SMS**, **Copy Portal Link** and **View Portal**, which opens the portal as the sponsor sees it. Which portal that is shows under the group's name as **Portal:** and in the **Assigned Portal** column — see [Creating custom portals](/portals/creating-custom-portals) to point sponsors at a portal of their own.

<Tip>
For contacts you already have in the event, [Invite people to the event portal](/portals/inviting-users-to-the-event-portal) covers granting access in bulk from **All Contacts**.
</Tip>
