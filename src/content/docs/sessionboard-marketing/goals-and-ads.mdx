A goal is a number of registrations for one event by a date. You approve one plan to get there, and **Goals** shows every day whether you're on pace and the one thing to do next.

Each event has one goal. Setting a new number for an event changes its goal; it doesn't add a second one.

## Set a goal

<Steps>
<Step title="Name the number">
Go to **Goals**. Under **What's your number?**, pick the **Event**, how many **Registrations** and **By** when. We fill in last time's number and the day before the event starts; change anything. Add an **Ad budget** if you want ads, or leave it empty.

You can also start from one of the example sentences, or select **Let Ask fill this in**.
</Step>
<Step title="Make the plan">
Select **Make the plan**. The plan splits your number across six lanes: **Speakers and guests sharing**, **Email to past attendees**, **Your newsletter**, **Automatic emails**, **Ads on your own accounts** and **Your event page**.
</Step>
<Step title="Approve it">
Under **The next move**, select **Approve the plan**. Approving starts tracking only: each lane still asks before it sends an email or spends anything.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Follow the pace

The top of the goal says where you stand in one sentence, such as "On track for ~520 of 500" or "Behind by 60", with a chart of the target, registrations so far and the forecast. Before anyone registers, the chart shows the plan's forecast. Select **Show as a table** to read the numbers.

**The next move** is the one thing that helps most right now, worked out from your numbers: for example, how many speakers haven't shared yet and what sharing has brought per speaker. Its button does it or opens the approval.

## The plan, lane by lane

Each lane shows its status (**Not started**, **Waiting for your OK** or **Running**), what it's planned to bring and what it has brought, and links to the real campaign, ads or page. Campaigns started from a lane show **Part of:** and the goal's name in **Campaigns**.

The email part of the plan is shown across **Email to past attendees**, **Your newsletter** and **Automatic emails**, by how many people each can still reach. Email can only bring people it can reach, so it is planned for at most about a third of the people on your lists; the rest moves to sharing and your event page, and the email lane says why (for example "No one to email yet: your list is empty"). The total doesn't change.

**Your newsletter**: select **Draft the newsletter** for an issue that announces the event to your subscribers, with a **Register** button. It stays a draft until you open it with **Approve the newsletter** and schedule it.

**Automatic emails** are the reminder to invited guests and the welcome to new newsletter subscribers. Select **Turn them on** to see each email and who gets it, then confirm. The welcome goes to new subscribers for every event, not just this one.

**Ads on your own accounts** holds the ad setup: connecting your ad account in **Apps**, the spend cap and the audience. Ads spend nothing until you select **Approve the spend** for the amount shown.

## What brought registrations

**What brought registrations** shows the same numbers as **Results**, by lane. In **Results**, the Email row lists what sent the email: campaigns, your newsletter or automatic emails. Select **Open in Results** for the full report. Sponsored reach is on **Results** too.

## Change or stop a goal

Select **Change the number** to change the target or the date. **Pause the goal** and **Mark it done** are under the goal.

<Note>
On **Home** and on an event's page, **How's it doing?** opens that event's goal.
</Note>
