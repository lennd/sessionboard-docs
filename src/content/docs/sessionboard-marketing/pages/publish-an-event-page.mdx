**Pages** holds your event pages, tour pages, on-demand library and snippets for your website, all in your brand. Nothing goes live until you publish it.

## Why use it

Sessionboard Marketing Pages let you publish event landing pages, sign-up forms and on-demand libraries without a separate website tool. You control the layout with blocks, apply your brand kit, and connect registration directly to the page. Use it to launch a campaign page, collect email sign-ups before tickets go on sale, or host a post-event on-demand library for attendees.

## Make and publish an event page

<Steps>
<Step title="Describe the event">
Under **Event and webinar pages**, say what the event is about and who it is for, then select **Make the page in your brand**.
</Step>
<Step title="Change it in plain words">
Under **Change the page**, describe what to change and select **Change it**. Use **Pick a brand kit** or **Refresh from the brand kit** to restyle it.
</Step>
<Step title="Publish">
Select **Publish**, then **Publish** again to confirm. The page goes live at its public address.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Where people register

- **In-person events and roadshows** take registrations on the page itself: people save a seat, and you approve them under the event's **Guests**.
- **Events that register somewhere else** (Cvent, Eventbrite and the like): give Ask the registration link during setup, under **Where do people register?** The page's **Register** button then opens it.
- **No registration yet?** The page says registration opens soon. Add a sign-up form (below) and the Register card points visitors to it, so you still collect their emails.

## Collect emails

After you publish, the **Collect emails from visitors?** card adds a sign-up form to your event page; select **Publish changes** to put it live. You can also ask: "Add a sign-up form to the Boston dinner page". People who sign up and confirm their email become subscribers for your newsletter. See [Sign-ups and email preferences](/sessionboard-marketing/sign-ups-and-email-preferences).

## Agenda, Speakers and Sponsors pages

Beyond the main page, an event makes **Agenda**, **Speakers** and **Sponsors** pages from its own program. **Agenda** appears once the event has sessions, **Speakers** once a speaker is public, and **Sponsors** once the event has a sponsor. Webinars and get-togethers keep one page. Under **Agenda, Speakers and Sponsors pages** you can **Show** or hide each, move it up or down, and rename how it reads in the menu, then **Save** and **Publish the page**. Publishing also puts any other unpublished edits live.

## Track visitors

See [Track visitors on your own website](/sessionboard-marketing/pages/website-tag) to see the pages people read before they sign up.

## Launch an on-demand library

Name the library and select **Make the library**. Visitors can ask the library questions about its sessions. Publish it when it is ready.

## Use your own domain

Every Marketing plan includes your own domain. Under **Your own domain**, enter an address such as `events.yourcompany.com` and select **Connect**. Add the DNS record it shows you; the status reads **Waiting for DNS** until it is in place.

A subdomain such as `events.yourcompany.com` is the easiest: a root domain such as `yourcompany.com` usually cannot take the record we ask for unless your DNS provider offers an ALIAS or CNAME-flattening record. One domain serves one page or library.

### Do it from Claude or ChatGPT

Your assistant can do the same steps: list the domains you have, connect a new one, give you the exact DNS records to add (a CNAME and a TXT, each with type, name, value and TTL, for any DNS provider), check them when you say they are in, move a domain to another page, or remove it. It never changes your DNS for you; you or your IT team add the records. Removing a domain takes the page back to its Sessionboard address, so your assistant asks first.

## Delete a page you no longer need

Ask Claude or ChatGPT to delete it, for example a test page or a duplicate. Your assistant asks you first. If the page is live, it also asks whether to take it offline, because visitors lose it. The page's address and your own domain are freed at once, and your domain stays under **Your own domain** for another page. If you deleted a page by mistake, contact Sessionboard support to bring it back.

Pages of archived events are left out when your assistant lists your pages, unless you ask for them.

## On your own website

Select **Get the snippets** to put your event and library on your own site. To see who from your audience visits your website, use **Track visitors on your own website** and paste the one line it gives you into your site.
