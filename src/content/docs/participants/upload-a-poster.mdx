If your event publishes an online poster hall, the poster you upload in your portal is the one visitors see. This page covers everything you can do with it: upload it, replace it, record a narration, answer the organizer, and share it once it is live.

## Why use it

Your poster is read for an afternoon at the board and for months afterwards in the online hall, by people who were never in the room. The few minutes you spend here — uploading the corrected version rather than the first one, recording two minutes of narration, answering the organizer where they asked — are what those later readers get instead of a PDF with no voice attached to it.

## Find your poster card

<Steps>

<Step title="Open My Participation">

Sign in to your portal and open **My Participation**. Your poster appears under its poster session — the session in capitals is the parent; yours is the row beneath it.

![A poster listed under its parent poster session in My Participation, with its format and accepted status](/images/kb/portal-poster-in-participation.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Open the session, then the Files tab">

Click your poster's row and choose **Files**. The **Poster** card is the organizer's ask: what they want, what they accept, and when it is due.

![The Poster card on the Files tab, showing the organizer's instructions and the accepted file types](/images/kb/portal-poster-slot-card.png)

</Step>

</Steps>

<Note>
No Poster card? Your event may collect one file for every session rather than a separate poster, in which case there is a single upload area instead — see [Upload files & make comments](/participants/upload-files). If the deadline has passed, the card says so in place of the drop zone.
</Note>

## Upload your poster

Drag your file onto **Upload to Poster**, or click it to browse.

A file of a type the organizer does not accept is refused with the reason, so you find out immediately rather than after the deadline.

Once it is uploaded, the card shows the file, when it arrived, and that it is the latest version.

![The uploaded poster showing its name, upload date, Latest Version marker, file type, size and a revisions control](/images/kb/portal-poster-versions.png)

### Replacing it

Upload again and the new file becomes the current poster. The old one is not deleted — it becomes a revision, and the control on the file row opens the history. The poster hall, your organizer's coverage view and this card always agree on which version is current.

<Tip>
Upload the corrected version rather than asking an organizer to delete the old one. Replacing keeps the history, and the hall switches over on its own.
</Tip>

## Read and answer the organizer's comments

Comments on the poster are a conversation with the event team, kept with the file rather than in your inbox.

![A comment thread on a poster, with messages from an admin and from the author, each labelled and dated](/images/kb/portal-poster-comments.png)

Write back with **Post comment**. If the organizer asks for a change, reply there and upload the new version — they will see both.

## Record a narration

A narration is a short walkthrough visitors hear next to your poster. You can record one in the browser; no software, no file to export.

<Steps>

<Step title="Open Narrate your poster">

The card sits under your poster on the same Files tab.

![The Narrate your poster card explaining that visitors hear the recording next to the poster, with a Record narration button](/images/kb/portal-poster-narration-card.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Record">

Click **Record narration** and allow your browser to use the microphone. The timer runs while you talk, up to five minutes.

![The narration card while recording, showing the elapsed time against the five-minute limit and a Stop recording button](/images/kb/portal-poster-recording.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Listen back, then save or start again">

**Stop recording** gives you the take to play back. **Save narration** publishes it; **Discard and record again** throws it away and starts over.

![The narration card after recording, offering playback with Save narration and Discard and record again](/images/kb/portal-poster-recording-review.png)

</Step>

</Steps>

Recording again later replaces the previous narration — visitors only ever hear the newest one. You can also upload an audio file instead; it appears on the card the same way.

If your narration is being held back, the card says why: the hall has not opened yet (with the date it opens), it is waiting for your organizer's approval, or it was declined. Only a declined narration needs recording again.

<Tip>
Two or three minutes is plenty. Say what the question was, what you found, and what you would tell someone standing at the board — visitors who want the detail have the poster in front of them.
</Tip>

## Open your poster

Once your poster is uploaded, **Open poster** opens it in one place:

- **On the left**, what you can do with this file: its versions, **upload a new version**, download it, the organizer's template if they shared one, and the narration. A video poster brings its own sound, so it is not offered a narration.
- **In the middle**, your poster exactly as visitors will see it in the gallery.
- **On the right**, the comment thread with your organizer.

A new version replaces the current poster straight away; the earlier ones stay in the version list.

## Share it once it is live

When your poster is in the hall, the card says so and gives you what you need to point people at it.

![The "Your poster is live" card offering View in gallery, Copy link and QR code](/images/kb/portal-poster-share.png)

- **View in gallery** opens your poster as a visitor sees it.
- **Copy link** is the direct URL to your poster, for email and social posts.
- **QR code** is yours to put on a slide, a handout or a business card.

<Note>
If the poster hall has not opened yet, or your organizer asked for publication consent you have not given yet, your poster will not be visible to the public until the hall opens or you accept the consent statement when you upload. The card tells you which applies.
</Note>
