Integrations. This is the chapter where the work you've done so far leaves the building. Registrations coming in, and accepted sessions and speakers going out to your registration platform, your website and your mobile app. Three ways to do it, and most teams use two.

One rule to hold onto first. Almost everything that goes out of Sessionboard is the accepted set. Accepted sessions, and the speakers on them. Pending and declined never leave. So your review process, the Review and evaluation chapter, is also your publishing control. Accept a session and it flows to the website and the app on the next sync. Un-accept it, and many of the connectors can pull it back too.

Apps. This is the marketplace, and it's organised by what you're trying to do. Media, for bringing webinar recordings in. Engagement, that's the networking and matchmaking platforms, Swapcard, Grip and the like, plus the audience advocacy tools. Program and Scheduling is the one most of you came for, Cvent, Swoogo, Bizzabo, Stova, ASP, Swapcard. Operations is Slack and Teams. Some apps connect once for the whole organisation, some per event, the badge on each card tells you which.

Let's open one. Cvent. Every app page opens with what syncs and which way. Sessions and speakers, Sessionboard to Cvent. Exhibitors and events, two ways. Down the side is the setup order, connections, map events, configure, map session fields, map speaker fields. Those stay greyed out until you've connected. And there's a read the setup guide link before you connect, please use it, the field mapping is where every integration call we get comes from.

Now the way that needs no connector and no developer. Program, Embeds. An embed is a live feed of your agenda and speakers that you paste onto your website as one line of code. It updates itself every hour, no sync, no push. Five widgets to pick from, a schedule itinerary with search and filters, an agenda grid, a session list, a speaker gallery and a speaker list. And if you'd rather feed an app, the same thing comes out as JSON, XML or an iCal calendar.

Add Embed. Name it for where it lives, agenda page, speakers page. The Enabled switch is your kill switch, turn it off and the widget goes blank on your site without touching the code. Then the format. Styled HTML is the interactive one you drop in as is. Plain HTML is for a developer who wants to style it. And then the data feeds. Style options next, theme, your primary colour, date format, language, and which participant roles show, speakers, chairs, moderators.

Filters does two different jobs, and this is the one people mix up. Attendee filters are the dropdowns your visitors get, format, track, location, level. Included sessions is what's in the feed at all. Status is Accepted is the default rule, and you can add a track, so the pre-conference page only ever shows pre-conference sessions. Watch the count at the bottom, twelve sessions and fifteen speakers match. That's your sanity check.

Field Options is what shows on each card. Title and times are required. Everything else, speakers, description, track, format, location, tags, is your choice, and you can rename a field for this embed only. Sort Order at the bottom, new embeds follow the speaker order on the session, or you can go alphabetical. Save, and you get your code.

Here's a saved one. Get Code, and you get a snippet for each of the five widgets, schedule itinerary, speaker gallery, agenda, sessions, speaker list. Copy, and those two lines go to whoever runs your website. Preview shows the widget before it goes anywhere. Two things to remember. It refreshes every sixty minutes, and if you need it faster there's Refresh Cache in the card menu. And the format is locked once saved, you can change style and fields, but a JSON feed can't become a widget, make a new one.

The bottom of the marketplace is for when someone technical joins the call. Automation and Developer has Webhooks and Zapier, and under For developers, the API reference, webhooks, which is how your systems hear about changes in real time, the MCP server, which is how you plug Claude or ChatGPT into your event data, and the SDKs and command line. You don't need any of this to run an event. It's there when your IT team asks.

Last one, at the organisation level. Settings, Single Sign On. If your staff sign in everywhere with Okta or Microsoft Entra, they can sign in here the same way, SAML or OIDC, and it can cover portal users and submitters too. It's an add-on, so this page is where you ask, and we set it up with your identity team.

So, three ways out. Connect an app, paste an embed, or hand a token to a developer. All of them run on the accepted set. The Sessions and agenda chapter is how sessions get made, subsessions, and building the schedule those embeds will show. The Help Center home for all of this is App marketplace, with a what each integration syncs table one click in.

Find it in the admin nav under **Apps**. The marketplace has four areas: **Marketplace** (browse & connect apps), **Skills**, **Workflows**, and **Developers** (API, webhooks, MCP).

<Note>
Apps connect at the **org level** or **event level** depending on the app. Some surface capabilities like **Discover**, **Send to**, **Clips**, **Webinars**, or contribute **signals** to the graph.
</Note>
