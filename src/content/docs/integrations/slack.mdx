You connect Slack once for your organization. After that, anyone on your Sessionboard team who is in the Slack workspace can use it, and every answer runs under that person's own Sessionboard permissions.

## Why use it

Your team already lives in Slack. When someone asks "how many sessions are confirmed?" in a channel, they can ask Sessionboard directly and get the number in seconds, instead of waiting for whoever has Sessionboard open. Program managers check speaker counts between meetings, and notifications about new submissions and speaker updates land where people will see them, with a link back to the record.

## What you can do

| Where in Slack | How | Who sees the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Sessionboard app's chat** | Open **Sessionboard** under **Apps** in Slack (or from **Agents & tools**) and type your question on its **Messages** tab. | Only you. |
| **`/sessionboard`** | Type `/sessionboard` followed by your question in any channel or direct message. | Only you. |
| **@mention** | Type `@Sessionboard` and your question in a channel the app has been added to. | Everyone in the channel. The answer is posted as a thread reply. |
| **Notifications** | Turn on the **Slack** column in your notification preferences. | Only you, as a direct message from the app. |

Each answer ends with a line such as *via Sessionboard MCP · find_event, get_event_overview*, which names the Sessionboard tools used to find it.

## Before you start

- **AI features are enabled for your organization.** The Slack app answers with the same AI that powers [AI assistants](/integrations/connect-ai-assistants#prerequisites). If AI is off, Slack replies that AI features are not enabled for your organization.
- **Everyone who asks has a Sessionboard user with the same email as their Slack account**, and is an active member of the organization. Sessionboard matches each person by their Slack email. Someone with no matching user gets a message asking an admin to invite them.
- **They are full members of your Slack workspace.** Slack guests, and people from another organization in a shared channel, can see the app but can't ask it questions.
- **To ask questions from Slack, your organization is hosted in the United States** — you sign in at `appv2.sessionboard.com`. Organizations in other regions can connect Slack and receive Slack notifications.
- **You can install apps in your Slack workspace.** If your workspace requires admin approval for apps, Slack asks a Slack admin to approve Sessionboard first.

## Connect Slack

Slack is set up at the **organization**, from **Apps** in the left sidebar. It is listed under **Operations**.

![The Slack page in Apps, showing what the app does and that Slack is connected](/images/kb/apps/slack-app-page-connected.png)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Slack in Apps">
    In your organization, open **Apps** from the left sidebar and click **Slack**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a connection">
    Click **Set up Slack**, or **Manage connection** and then **Add a connection** if your organization already has one. Optionally enter a **Name** for the connection; if you leave it blank, the connection is named after your Slack workspace. Click **Connect with Slack**.

    ![Add a connection form with an optional Name field and the Connect with Slack button](/images/kb/apps/slack-add-connection.png)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow access in Slack">
    Slack opens and asks you to allow Sessionboard to access your workspace. Check that the workspace in the top-right menu is the right one, and click **Allow**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    You see **Connected**. Close the window or click **Return to Sessionboard**. The Slack page now says **Slack is connected**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Connect each Slack workspace to one Sessionboard organization. If the same workspace is connected to two organizations, Sessionboard can't tell which one to search and asks you to contact support.

### Install from Slack

You can also start in Slack: click **Add to Slack** on the Sessionboard app's page in Slack, and click **Allow**. Slack sends you to Sessionboard, where you sign in if asked. Choose the **Organization** to connect the workspace to, and click **Connect Slack**. You land on **Apps > Slack** with the connection in place.

Finish within 30 minutes of clicking **Allow**. After that, Sessionboard says **This Slack install has expired**, and you add the app from Slack again. If the workspace is already connected to another Sessionboard organization, disconnect it there first.

## Ask a question

### In the app's chat

Open **Sessionboard** from the **Apps** section of Slack's sidebar, or from **Agents & tools**, and type your question on its **Messages** tab.

Sessionboard replies in a thread under your question. Ask follow-up questions in that thread and it keeps the context, so you can ask "and how many of those are keynotes?" without repeating the event name. Start a new message for a new topic.

![A question about upcoming events and Sessionboard's answer in a thread on the app's Messages tab](/images/kb/apps/slack-agent-thread-answer.png)

### With /sessionboard

Type `/sessionboard` and your question in any channel or direct message, for example `/sessionboard How many sessions does Annual Meeting 2027 have?`. The answer is visible only to you, even in a busy channel. Sessionboard first shows *Looking that up…*, then replaces it with the answer.

![A /sessionboard answer, visible only to the person who asked, listing total sessions, accepted sessions and speakers](/images/kb/apps/slack-slash-command-answer.png)

`/sessionboard` works in channels and direct messages, not inside a thread or the app's own chat. In the app's chat, type the question without the command.

### By @mentioning Sessionboard in a channel

Add the app to a channel first: type `/invite @Sessionboard` in the channel. Then mention it with your question — `@Sessionboard which speakers haven't uploaded a headshot for Annual Meeting 2027?`. Sessionboard replies in a thread under your message, and **everyone in the channel can read the answer**.

<Warning>
An @mention answer is posted for the whole channel, and it contains whatever your Sessionboard permissions allow you to see. Use `/sessionboard` or the app's chat when the answer should stay with you.
</Warning>

### What you can ask

Sessionboard picks the right lookup for your question, so phrase it the way you would ask a colleague:

- "List my events by date."
- "How many sessions are confirmed in Annual Meeting 2027?"
- "Which speakers need attention in Annual Meeting 2027?"
- "List speakers added in the last 7 days."
- "What are the top-rated sessions?"

If you don't name an event, Sessionboard uses your most recent active event and says which one it used. Name the event to switch. It knows today's date, so "which events start next?" and "this week" mean what you expect.

Sessionboard can also create or update records when you ask it to in so many words — "add a session called Opening Keynote to Annual Meeting 2027". It can only change what your role lets you change in Sessionboard, asks you to confirm before deleting anything or changing many records at once, and tells you exactly what it changed.

## Get notifications in Slack

Once Slack is connected, each person can choose which Sessionboard notifications also arrive as a Slack message. Open your notification preferences and turn on the **Slack** column for the notification types you want. See [Notifications](/communications/notifications#slack-delivery).

## Who can see what

- Every answer runs as the person who asked. Sessionboard checks their role, event access and field permissions, exactly as if they were signed in to Sessionboard. A team member who can't see an event in Sessionboard can't see it from Slack.
- Answers in the app's chat and from `/sessionboard` are visible only to the person who asked. Answers to an @mention are visible to everyone in the channel.
- The app reads a channel's messages only when it is mentioned there, to follow the thread it was asked in.

## Disconnect Slack

Open **Apps > Slack**, click **Manage connection**, and delete the connection with the trash icon. Questions in Slack then get a reply that the workspace isn't connected, and Slack notifications stop. In-product and email notifications keep arriving. To remove the app from Slack as well, a Slack admin can remove it from your workspace's app settings.

Removing the app in Slack also disconnects it in Sessionboard automatically, so you don't need to delete the connection by hand. To use it again, connect Slack again from Sessionboard or from Slack.

## Troubleshooting

| Slack says | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "This Slack workspace isn't connected to a Sessionboard organization yet." | An organization admin connects it from **Apps > Slack**. |
| "I couldn't find a Sessionboard user with your Slack email." | Invite the person to your organization with the same email they use in Slack. |
| "Your Sessionboard account isn't a member of the organization linked to this workspace." | Add the person to the organization connected to this workspace, or reactivate them if they were deactivated. |
| "Slack guests can't ask Sessionboard." | Ask from a full member account in the workspace. |
| "Sessionboard only answers members of the workspace it's connected to…" | People from another organization in a shared channel can't ask. They can use their own organization's Sessionboard connection. |
| "You're sending requests a bit too fast…" | Wait a minute and ask again. Each person can ask about 15 questions a minute. |
| "AI features aren't enabled for your Sessionboard organization yet." | Contact [support@sessionboard.com](mailto:support@sessionboard.com) or your account manager to have AI turned on. |
| "That took too long to answer and timed out." | Ask again, or narrow the question to one event. |
| "/sessionboard is not supported in threads." | Use the command outside the thread, or ask in the app's chat. |
| Nothing happens after an @mention | Add the app to the channel with `/invite @Sessionboard`. |

## Still having issues?

Email [support@sessionboard.com](mailto:support@sessionboard.com) with your organization name, the Slack workspace, the question you asked, and roughly when you asked it.
