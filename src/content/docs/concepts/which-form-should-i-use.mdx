Sessionboard has several kinds of forms, and each one is a different product with its own module, settings, and reporting. They are not interchangeable: a submission form cannot track a per-applicant decision, and an application form cannot collect a session abstract. Choose by answering three questions — **what** are you collecting, **from whom**, and **for what decision**?

## Quick answer

| You want to… | Use | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collect session proposals, abstracts, or posters for review | **Submission form** | **Program > Forms** |
| Take applications from people or companies and track each one to a decision | **Application form** | **Program > Applications** |
| Bring a new or returning sponsor or exhibitor company into your event | **Intake form** | **CRM > Sponsors** / **CRM > Exhibitors** |
| Collect details from participants who are already in the event | **Portal form** | **Program > Portals** |
| Source speakers year-round, outside any one event | **Interest form** | **CRM > Interest Forms** (organization level) |
| Accept entries or nominations for an awards program | **Awards submission form** | **Program > Programs > Awards** |
| Score submissions or rate sessions | **Evaluation** or **Survey** | **Program > Evaluation** / **Surveys** |

## Submission forms

A submission form is your call for papers. It collects a speaker *and* the content they propose — a session, an abstract, a poster — in one flow. Submissions land in **Program > Submissions**, go through **Evaluation**, and the accepted ones become sessions on your agenda.

Use a submission form when the thing you are deciding on is **content**. It is the only form type that captures session information, and it is the one Sessionboard's submission pipeline, evaluation plans, and agenda are built around.

An event can run several submission forms at once — for example one for general sessions and one for posters, each with its own open and close dates.

[Session submission forms](/sessions/submission-forms) covers building one.

## Application forms

An application form collects a request to take part and gives you a **status per applicant** — pending, accepted, declined, or a custom status you define. Applications are attached to the person (or company), not to a session, so the form has no session or abstract fields.

Use an application form when the decision is a **yes or no about a person or company**: exhibitor applications, scholarship or travel-grant requests, volunteer sign-ups, invited-speaker confirmations. It is not a substitute for a call for papers — if you need to review proposed content, use a submission form.

[Create applications](/applications/create-applications) walks through setting one up.

## Intake forms

Intake forms belong to the sponsors and exhibitors modules (**CRM > Sponsors** and **CRM > Exhibitors**). You send one to a sponsor or exhibitor company to bring the group and its primary contact into your database. Once the group exists, you keep collecting what you need from it through its group portal — tasks, file requests, and portal forms.

Use an intake form when the goal is to **get a company into the event**. Everything after that (booth details, logos, marketing copy) is portal work.

## Portal forms

A portal form is a set of structured questions you assign as a **task** to contacts or groups who are already in the event. They complete it inside their portal, and the answers are stored on their record. Typical uses: headshot and bio collection, AV requirements, dietary needs, booth details, session logistics.

Use a portal form when you are **collecting from people you already have**, not recruiting new ones. Because it is a task, you can set a due date, see who has and has not completed it, and send reminders.

[Create & assign portal forms](/portals/create-assign-forms) has the steps.

## Interest forms

Interest forms live at the **organization** level in Speaker CRM, outside any single event. Every submission lands in your Speaker CRM Pipeline, where you can qualify people and add them to an event later.

Use an interest form when you want a **year-round "speak with us" page** — a standing intake that keeps sourcing speakers between events. Interest forms are part of Speaker CRM; if your organization does not have it, contact [support@sessionboard.com](mailto:support@sessionboard.com).

[Speaker CRM: interest forms](/speaker-crm/speaker-crm-interest-forms) explains how the routing works.

## Awards submission forms

The Awards module has its own submission form for entries and nominations. It carries the pieces an awards program needs and a session call for papers does not: categories, pricing and payment, and judging rounds with winner tags.

Use an awards submission form when you are running an **awards program**. Do not try to run awards through a session submission form — you would lose categories, pricing, and the judging workflow.

[Building your awards submission form](/awards/awards-submission-form) covers configuration.

## Evaluations and surveys

Evaluations and surveys collect **feedback**, not intake. Evaluation plans let reviewers score submissions before the event; surveys let attendees rate sessions during and after it. Neither creates a new contact or a new session.

If you are trying to decide whether something is "a form" in Sessionboard, ask whether it brings a new record in. Evaluations and surveys do not — they attach opinions to records that already exist.

<Note>
The names above are the ones you will see in the product. If a colleague says "the CFP" they mean a submission form; "the speaker form" on a portal is a portal form.
</Note>

## Choosing between two that seem close

**Submission form vs. application form.** Do you need to review *what* they propose, or just decide *whether* they take part? Content → submission form. Person or company → application form. A speaker who submits an abstract uses a submission form; an exhibitor who wants a booth uses an application or an intake form.

**Application form vs. intake form.** Intake forms exist to create a sponsor or exhibitor group and its portal. Application forms track a decision and do not create a group. If the answer to "will they exhibit?" is already yes, use an intake form; if you are still deciding, use an application form.

**Portal form vs. any of the above.** Portal forms only reach people who are already in the event. If the person is not yet a contact, you need a submission, application, intake, or interest form first.

<Tip>
Many events use several of these together: a submission form for proposals, an application form for exhibitor requests, an intake form once a sponsor signs, and portal forms to collect headshots and AV needs from everyone who is accepted.
</Tip>
