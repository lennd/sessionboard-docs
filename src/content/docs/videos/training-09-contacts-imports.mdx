Contacts and imports. Everything in Sessionboard hangs off a contact. A speaker is a contact on a session, a sponsor's primary contact is a contact, an evaluator is a contact with a login. So getting people in cleanly, and keeping them clean, is worth ten minutes. It all lives in CRM, in the left nav, under All Contacts.

All Contacts is everyone attached to this event, whatever their role. The sub items are views of the same people, speakers, chairpersons, moderators, submitters, and additional contacts, the assistants and PR people who aren't on stage but need the emails. Contacts are shared across your organisation, so a speaker from last year is the same record this year. That Speaker CRM badge is the organisation-wide side of it.

Add Contact. Notice it doesn't open a form first, it opens a search. Type a name or email and if the person already exists anywhere in your organisation, you attach that record instead of making a new one, which is how most duplicates start. Only if nobody comes back do you go to add new contact. First name, last name and email are required, the rest can come later. Which fields show up here, and in what order, is a layout setting under record settings. I'll cancel, we have plenty of people.

The table itself. Columns opens preferences, and this is every field on the contact, standard and custom, tick what you want to see. Sort and filter are the other two tabs. Once you've built a view you like, say accepted speakers missing a headshot, saved views keeps it so you're not rebuilding the filters every morning. The search box up top is for the quick lookups.

Now bulk. Options, import. Two choices. Generate template downloads a CSV with a column for every field in your current view, so add your custom fields to the view first and they'll be in the file. Upload file takes a CSV or Excel, up to a thousand rows at a time. Column headers stay in English, that's how Sessionboard matches them. I'll close this rather than run an import on a demo event.

The steps once you upload. Tell it whether the first row is headers, then map your columns to Sessionboard fields, ignore the ones you don't want to touch. Then validation, any cell it can't accept goes red, click it for the reason, usually a phone number without a country code, a date Excel reformatted, or a multi select without the pipe separator. Fix, submit, and you get a confirmation once the rows are in.

The trick people miss is that import also updates. Export first, with email in the view, because email is how a contact row finds its existing record. Change what you need, add a column called update record if already exists, set it to true, and re import. Same for sessions, matched on session friendly ID, and groups, matched on name. Mapped blank columns overwrite, so choose ignore this column for anything you're not changing.

Order matters. People first. The session import's speakers, moderators and chairpersons columns take email addresses, and a row fails if that email isn't already a contact on the event. So contacts, then sessions with their participants, then sponsors and exhibitors. And if last year's event is in the same organisation, skip the spreadsheet, options, copy from another event, over in sessions.

Taking someone out. Tick the row and the bulk bar appears, edit, send, assign, and delete. Delete asks the question that matters, remove from this event, the record stays for your other events, or remove from all events, which is permanent. Nine times out of ten you want the first one. I'll cancel.

Duplicates still happen, two emails for one person, a name typed twice. Open a contact and, when the platform has spotted lookalikes, a banner at the top of the profile says we found potential duplicates, merge duplicates. That takes you to a two step wizard, pick up to three records and which one is primary, then compare the fields side by side and choose the values to keep. Sessions, events and content come from all of them, notes and event level fields from the primary. It can't be undone, so export first.

That's contacts. They are the foundation, search before you add, import people before sessions, and merge rather than delete when two records are one person. The chapters together cover everything the onboarding calls do, and the Help Center article for this one is Importing data, with common CSV issues linked from the bottom.
