Sessions and the agenda. Most of your sessions arrive through the form we built two chapters ago. This chapter is about the rest, the ones you add yourself, the keynote, the welcome reception, the sessions that need a subsession underneath, and then turning all of it into a schedule.

Program, Sessions. Every session in the event, one row each. The tabs across the top are the statuses, all, accepted, the accept queue, pending, the decline queue, declined, withdrawn and drafts. Same statuses we set up in Program Settings, and the Source column tells you whether a session came in through a form or was added by hand.

Add Session, top right. Two tabs. Details is the session itself, title and status are the only two you must fill, then description, start and end, capacity, credits, and the vocabulary, format, language, level, track, tags, and a location. Participants is who's on it, speakers, chairpersons, moderators, the submitter, sponsors and exhibitors. One thing to know, if you search a speaker here and they don't come up, they don't exist yet. Create them in Contacts first, then come back.

Let's open one that exists. The pencil on any row opens the session in a drawer, and the tabs across the top are everything about it. Details, notes, participants, files, media, connections, reviews, messages, activity. Search fields at the top finds any field on the page, and your custom fields sit at the bottom of Details. Click a value to edit it in place.

Connections is where subsessions live. A parent session is the block on your agenda, a subsession is a segment inside it, three papers in one poster session, five talks in a lightning round. Create Subsession makes one from scratch, and it has to fall inside the parent's time. Convert Submissions turns a batch of accepted abstracts into subsessions in one step, which is how the medical and scientific programs do it. Speakers on a subsession are added to the parent too, so the parent always knows who's in the room.

Before we look at the agenda, its settings. Program, Settings, Agenda. Day start, day end and the interval shape the grid. Agenda content decides whether sessions, abstracts, or both show up. And Program Statuses is the one people miss, by default only accepted sessions appear on the agenda. Add pending here if you want to schedule before you've decided.

Program, Agenda. Five ways to look at the same schedule. List is the table. Day and week are the calendar, and the colour of each block is its track, so you can see at a glance whether Tuesday morning is all research. Month is the overview, no colours there. And Rooms puts every room side by side so you can spot an empty slot in Room A.

The right-hand panel is your to-do list, scheduled and unscheduled. Drag a session from unscheduled onto the grid and it's scheduled, drag it again to move it, drag it into a room in the rooms view to assign the room. Each drag saves, and if submission change notifications are on for the speaker's portal, the speaker hears about it. Turn that off while you're still shuffling, and back on when the schedule is close to final.

Conflicts. Two rules are always on and can't be turned off, two sessions in the same room at the same time, and the same speaker, chair or moderator in overlapping sessions. Below those you write your own rules in plain English, no two keynotes on the same day, no workshop before ten, and mark each one enforced, which blocks the agenda, or warning, which just flags it. Anything that breaks a rule is listed here, with a red dot on the agenda block.

That's the program, made, connected and scheduled. The part that comes before all of this in real life, deciding what gets accepted, is the Review and evaluation chapter. Accept and decline queues, evaluation plans, and what your reviewers see. The Help Center article for this one is Agenda, with Create a session and Create a subsession alongside it.
