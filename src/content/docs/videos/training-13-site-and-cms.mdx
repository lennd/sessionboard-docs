Site and CMS. Everything you've built, the sessions, the speakers, the forms, needs somewhere to be seen. CMS is that somewhere. It's one group in the sidebar with four ways to publish, and the whole trick is picking the right one for the job, so that's where I'll start.

The CMS overview lays the four out. A Site is your program's own home page and the portal participants sign in to. Embeds put live agenda and speaker data inside a website you already run. A Poster Gallery is a searchable e-poster hall. And a Content Gallery is the evergreen home for recordings, decks and lessons after the event. Two of these are Early Access, so they may show request access until they're switched on for your organization.

If you're not sure, this button is the answer. It starts from what you want to do. Somewhere for speakers to sign in, that's a Site. The agenda on your marketing site, that's an Embed. Recordings after the event, a Content Gallery. Underneath, the four compared side by side, what you get, who can see it, and what each one is not for. Pick a row and it creates the right template for you.

Every surface starts from a template. For a Site, a call for papers page, a participant hub, an evaluators page or an awards portal. For Embeds, a styled agenda widget, a speaker gallery, a JSON feed for your app, or an iCal feed. Choosing one opens the builder with that structure already in place, so you're editing, not starting from nothing.

This event's Site. The overview is a status page. The public address, whether it's live, how people sign in, and what the signed-in side looks like. Preview Site opens it as a visitor would see it, and the quick actions at the bottom jump straight to login options, branding, pages or single sign-on. Settings opens the builder.

The builder. Sitemap on the left, a live preview in the middle, and the inspector for whatever's selected on the right. The rule that matters most is in the header. Everything you change saves to a draft and shows in the preview right away, but nothing reaches the live site until you press Publish. Right now there's nothing waiting, so it says live and Publish is resting. Undo and Redo step through your edits.

The sitemap has two tabs. Public is what anyone sees before signing in, the settings, the pages, and any custom pages you add. Each page shows live or off, so you can tell at a glance what visitors can open. This event has its landing page on, and the speakers, agenda and exhibitors pages off until you want them. Custom pages you add yourself, or copy across from another event.

Site is the basics. Ready to share is a checklist, branding, a live program, a login method, a privacy notice. The site URL is a slug on sites dot sessionboard dot com, unique across the platform. And branding, logo, gradient and font for the public landing, then logo, background, primary and accent colors for the signed-in portal. Generate scans your website and fills these in as a starting brand kit.

Layout decides what visitors meet before they sign in. Portal is the classic landing, gradient, sign-in card, program cards. Marketing site turns the same URL into a public event website, with its own top menu, a hero banner, blocks and a footer. Switching never deletes anything. On the marketing layout, templates set the structure, speakers first, partners first, program first, and color schemes set the palette and fonts without touching that structure. Every scheme is checked for contrast, and your brand, saved, brings your own colors back.

Login and SSO is how people get in. Standard login is always on, that's the magic link or password flow speakers and reviewers already know. Turn on SSO login when your members should sign in through your association's identity provider. Programs and CFP is the set of forms that appear as cards on the signed-in home, tick a form here and it's on the site. The preview switches to a submitter's view so you can see exactly what they'd get.

The Portal tab is the other half, the pages people see after they sign in. Welcome, home, the review workspace for evaluators, and my profile. Each has its own inspector, so the home page text your speakers read is edited right here, with the same draft and publish rule as everything else.

The rest of the group. Embeds is where the agenda and speaker widgets live, and they refresh on their own as the program changes, no login involved, that's covered in the Integrations video. Poster gallery and content galleries are Early Access, so until they're on you'll see this, and the link takes you straight to the request.

If you take one thing from this, it's the draft. Change anything you like, check it in the preview, and it only goes live when you say so. The Help Center's site builder article walks through every block and the public program pages in detail. The Call for Papers video shows the forms that end up as cards here, and Event setup covers the branding these colors come from.
