Hi. This is a short tour of Sessionboard before you go into any of the training chapters. Sessionboard runs your speaker and session program end to end, the call for papers, the review, the agenda, the speakers' portal, the emails, and the content that comes out the other side. This is where you land when you sign in, your organisation.

Two levels to keep in your head. The organisation is the container, your events live inside it, and so do the things you share between them, contacts, custom fields, email templates, your team. An event is one edition of one conference. Most of your day is inside an event, so let's open one.

The event dashboard. The header at the top of the sidebar says which event you're in, with its dates, and clicking it takes you back to the organisation or across to another event. The sidebar is the whole product, and it only shows what your plan includes and your role allows, so yours may be shorter than mine. Dashboard and Training are always the first two rows, then the modules, then the setup rows underneath.

Program is the content, and it's the module you'll live in. Submissions is what came in, the abstracts, the sessions, the files that arrived with them. Collect and review is how it came in and what happens to it. Forms is the call for papers. Send is the emails that go with it. Evaluation is where your committee scores. Agenda is where accepted sessions get a room and a time, with conflict checks. The Call for papers, Sessions and agenda, and Review and evaluation chapters each take one of these.

Still inside Program, the Portals section is the speaker's side of the product. Each portal is a branded page where a speaker, sponsor or exhibitor signs in, sees their tasks, bio, headshot, agreement, and completes them. You build the tasks here, they see the checklist there. That's the Speaker portal and tasks chapter, and the Communications chapter for the emails around it.

CRM is the people. Contacts is everyone attached to the event, one row per role you use, speakers, chairpersons, moderators, submitters. Exhibitors and sponsors are groups, companies with their own people and their own portal. Contacts are shared across the organisation, so the same speaker is one record in every event. The Contacts and imports chapter goes deep on this.

Two more modules you may or may not have. Marketing is what you make from the program, advocacy for your speakers to share, transcripts, media and slides. CMS is where the event shows up in public, embeds for your website and the event site. Both light up as your plan adds them.

Then the rows under the modules. Automations and reports do what they say. History is every email ever sent, with its delivery status. Event team is who can sign in to this event and with which role, that's the Users and permissions chapter. And settings, at the bottom, is the event itself, name, dates, branding, program vocabulary, integrations, and the library, where your custom fields live. Event setup and Integrations cover those.

Two habits worth picking up on day one. Hover any row and pin it, and it sits at the top of the sidebar for you, on every event. And the search box at the top finds any page by name, so you never have to remember which module something lives in.

And Training, right under dashboard, is where these videos live inside the product, with a checklist of what's set up and what isn't, so you can watch a chapter and do the step in the same window.

That's the shape of it. Content in Program, people in CRM, and the setup rows underneath. If you're starting from nothing, go in the onboarding order, event setup first, then team, then the form, and the rest follows. The Event setup chapter is the place to start.
