Let's go back to the start. Before anyone imports a spreadsheet or opens a form, there's a short list of settings that everything else reads from. Get these right once and the rest of the event behaves. Four stops. Event details, record settings, custom fields, and your team.

Settings, bottom of the navigation. The Overview groups everything into event setup, library, communications and configuration. Today we're in the first group.

Event Details is the basics. The name and the slug, that's the address in every portal link. Event type, website, location, and the theme, a sentence about who the event is for. Those four aren't decoration, the AI features read them to understand your event, so a real sentence here makes the agents noticeably better.

Timezone is the one to double check. It's the clock every participant sees. Session times and any date field in a portal render in this zone, with its abbreviation, not the reader's own. Leave it blank and they fall back to UTC, and a speaker in Denver sees a keynote at the wrong hour. Then start and end, and your logo and background image under Image Settings.

Record Settings, General. This page shapes how submissions and contacts behave across the whole event. Submission limit caps how many sessions one person can submit, counted across every form, not per form. Subsession limits do the same for the sessions under a parent, and you can override it by filter, say a higher cap for anything in the expo format.

The Portal and Contact group is where most onboarding questions land. Automatically provision portal access means anyone you add or import can sign into the portal without another step. Collect Additional Contacts adds an assistant or co-author section to the submission form, and those people get copied on emails. Enable Primary Speakers lets you mark who the point of contact is on a session with several people.

Speaker Headshot Limitations is worth turning on before the first upload. Set accepted formats, an optional file size cap, and maximum dimensions. Dimensions and size behave differently. An oversized image is resized for you, an oversized file is refused. If you want consistent headshots for the website, set the dimensions.

Down at the bottom, Record IDs. The prefix goes in front of every submission, contact and group number, so this is where SESS or ABST comes from. It's applied when an ID is displayed, not when the record is made, which means changing it renames every existing record at once. Fine before launch, disruptive after you've sent IDs to reviewers.

Custom fields. Settings, then Library, then Fields, inside the event. Three tabs, Contact, Group and Submission, and each has the fields that already exist, with the system ones locked. Two reasons this comes before anything else. If you import, the column has to have a field to land in. And on a submission form, a field becomes a question, so building the field first is building the form.

Add Field, then Add Field. A name. The type, and this is the one decision you can't undo, the type is locked once you save. Text, dropdown, date, file, formula, and so on. For a dropdown, the options go here, and you can let submitters add their own if you want the list to grow. Field Level, event or global, decides whether this field exists on every event in the organization or just this one.

Contains Personal Data is easy to miss. Turn it on for anything sensitive and the value is masked in AI queries and in reports you share outside the team. Save Changes, and it's on the list and available to forms, columns and filters.

When a field stops being useful, archive it rather than delete it. Archived fields disappear from forms and pickers but keep their data, and you can restore them from the Hidden Fields tab. Delete is for a field made by mistake, because it takes the data with it.

Last, your team. Event Team, then Add, Invite User. Email and name, then the type of user, and that's the real question. Admin User sees everything. Admin Lite and the manager roles each cover one area, sessions, content or communications. Reporting Only is for the person who needs the numbers and nothing else. Session Managers can be given a filter so they only see their track. Tags here are for evaluation plans later on.

That's the foundation. Details, the record settings that shape every form, fields before data, and the right role for each person. Roles and field-level permissions get their own chapter, Users and permissions. The submission form itself is the Call for papers chapter. The Help Center article is Event details and record settings, and it's worth reading once with your event open beside it.
