Automations. Every event has a handful of moments that should trigger a message without anyone remembering to send it. A session gets accepted, a speaker's status changes, a submission lands. An automation is a short recipe for one of those moments, when this happens, then do this, and it emails the people on that record, or puts the session in front of reviewers.

Automations, in the sidebar footer. Every recipe on this event, with Enabled and Disabled filters and, on each row, what it watches and when it last ran, never run, last ran, didn't run, failed. Anyone on the event team can open this and build one. The Delivery history tab we'll come back to at the end.

Add. Two tabs. The gallery is six ready recipes with the When already filled in. Session accepted and Session declined email the speakers the first time the status lands. Email session participants fires on any field you pick. New submission received emails the submitter the moment a form is completed. Assign reviewers drops a session onto evaluation rounds. Email a person watches a contact's own fields. From scratch is a blank When card for sessions or people. I'll take Session accepted.

This is the builder. The name at the top is what shows in the list, and next to it, saved, and disabled, because a new recipe is always off until you attach an action and flip Enable. The left rail is the recipe in three parts, the trigger, then, and activity. The canvas reads top to bottom, when this happens, status becomes accepted, then the actions, and the plus is where you add one.

The When card. For sessions there are two triggers. A field changes, which is the default, or a new submission on a form, any form or one you choose, and that one fires exactly once per submission. Then the field, status here, and optionally the values it has to change to, accepted, and you can tick more than one. Only enroll when is extra filters on the same record, the same filter builder as embeds and evaluation plans. Leave it empty and every matching change enrolls.

Then. Send email, and Configure email opens the sheet. Three ways to build it. Compose new, right here, with merge tags. Start from a template, which copies one of your event templates. Or reuse an existing campaign from Send, as is. The recipient isn't a list you pick, it's the enrolled record, so a session automation emails that session's speakers, chairs and moderators, or the submitter, or both, and a people automation emails that contact. Send test puts it in your own inbox first.

The other action is Assign reviewers, sessions only. You pick the plan rounds, plan name and round name, and then the evaluators, people already on the plan or a reviewer tag. As submissions come in and hit your condition, they're on a reviewer's desk without anyone assigning them. The rounds have to exist first, so build the evaluation plan before the recipe.

Re-enroll decides how often. Every time this happens runs on each matching change, and quick edits inside forty five seconds are grouped so it fires once. Only the first time is one run per record, which is what you want for an acceptance email. Test runs the recipe now, with no record for a dry run, or against a real session, and that one sends live email. Save, then Enable, and Sessionboard won't let you enable until there's at least one action.

Back in the directory, Delivery history. Activity on a recipe says what it did. This tab answers the question you get after the fact, was this person emailed, and by what. Every record any automation enrolled, newest first, with Email sent backed by the real email, or skipped and why. Emails only narrows it, and Deleted automations only shows records handled by recipes that no longer exist, because deleting a recipe never deletes its history.

Two things this isn't. It isn't the automated emails Sessionboard already sends, submission confirmations and portal invites, those are in Communications. And it isn't a campaign to a segment, that's Send. An automation always goes to the record that changed. The Help Center article is Automations.
