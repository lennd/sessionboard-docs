Review and evaluation. The submissions are in, and now somebody has to decide. This chapter is the two halves of that. The status on every session, which is your decision, and evaluation plans, which is how you get twelve committee members to score two hundred abstracts without giving them the keys to the event.

Program, Sessions. The tabs are the five status categories, and the words matter. Pending is where everything lands. Accept Queue and Decline Queue are holding areas, you've leaned one way but haven't told anyone, and the speaker still sees pending in their portal. Accepted and Declined are the real decisions, and those are the two the speaker can see. Accepted also puts the session on the agenda and into the accepted set that flows to your website. Your custom statuses, In Review, Needs Revisions, sit under one of these five.

Changing one is a click on the status itself. Every status, standard and custom, pick one, save. Select a batch of rows and you can do it in bulk. And here's the thing people get caught by, changing a status sends no email. Nothing. The speaker finds out when you send the accept and decline emails from the Communications chapter, which means you can decide on Friday and tell everyone on Monday.

Program, Evaluation. The summary shows how far along you are, total evaluations, how many submissions have been scored, plans, evaluators. An evaluation plan is a set of submissions, a set of reviewers, and a way of scoring. You can run more than one, a first-pass plan for the whole committee and a shortlist plan for the chairs, and each one has its own instructions and its own due date.

Add Plan. The first screen shapes the whole review. A name. Whether it opens right away, closed plans are view-only for evaluators, so you can set up in peace. Scoring, percentage or points. Instructions, the criteria your reviewers follow, and a review period with an open and a close. Then the switches. Anonymized review hides speaker names. Weekly reminders nudge reviewers every Monday while they have work left. Include uploaded files lets them open attachments. The steps after it are rounds, where the rubric lives, evaluators, and assignments, who evaluates what.

About the reviewers themselves. An evaluator is a user on your event with one job, complete the plans assigned to them. They don't see the sessions module, contacts, emails or settings. They log in and see their queue. So invite your committee as evaluators, not admins, and use tags, poster committee, education track, so you can assign a whole group to a plan in one pass. A plan has to be closed to change who's on it.

Here's a plan that's running. Open, with a due date, and a progress bar, fifteen of twenty-four evaluations in. Submissions is each session with how many of its reviews are done and the average score so far. Evaluators is the same thing by person, so you can see who's behind before the deadline, not after. Close plan stops the scoring, and Plan Options has the export.

Review is where you read the results. Pick a session on the left. Average score, how many evaluations, how many rated, how many abstained. Then each reviewer's answers, the rubric criteria and their comments for the committee. And below it, the committee decision, Accept or Decline, written straight to the session's status. One decision, no re-keying into a spreadsheet and back.

Once you've decided, the accepted sessions are on the agenda waiting to be scheduled, that was the last chapter. The speakers still don't know. Send the accept and decline emails from Contacts or Sessions, and if you've turned on speaker acceptance in settings, each speaker confirms from their portal and you watch the confirmation column fill in.

That's review. Statuses are your decision, plans are how your committee informs it, and neither one emails anybody until you do. Next is users and permissions, the roles on your team and what each of them can see, including those evaluators. The Help Center articles are Accept or decline submissions and Evaluation plans.
