Reports, views and exports. Three ways to get your data out of Sessionboard and in front of the people who need it. A report you build once and share, a table view your team comes back to every day, and a plain spreadsheet when someone just wants the file.

Reports, in the sidebar footer. Two cards at the top, and both of them open the builder. Browse the gallery is a ready-made report you run in one click. Describe it with AI is a question in plain English. Below that, your custom reports, anything you've saved, then Scheduled Reports, and then the pre-built ones, session reports, contact reports, group reports for sponsors and exhibitors, and evaluation plan reports.

Let's start from the gallery. Ninety-odd examples, and the chips across the top filter them, sessions, subsessions, speakers, submissions, evaluation, agenda, tasks and compliance, contacts, sponsors, exhibitors, audit. Every card lists how many columns it comes with. I'll take Sessions by track, because that's the question every program chair asks in week one.

This is the Report Builder, and it's three parts. Fields on the left, every field you can report on, grouped by the session itself and then by what's connected to it, speakers, sponsors, tasks, ratings. Preview in the middle, a live table that reruns as you change things, with the row count and the query time at the bottom. And the report shape on the right, filters, group by, nested rows, pivot, sort and columns.

Add a filter picks a field, and the operators fit it, a status gives you is and is not with your real statuses. Group by is what makes this a summary, a count of sessions per track. Nested rows and pivot are the alternatives, speakers stacked under each session, or spread across it as speaker one and speaker two, one of the three at a time. Sort, with a row limit for a top ten. And columns can be dragged, renamed, and shown differently, a date by month, a list as a count.

Describe it with AI. Type what you want, all accepted speakers with their sessions, and the builder writes it. And because it works on the report that's open, you can keep going, group it by track, only sessions with more than two speakers. If the answer isn't right, say it more precisely, or fix the column yourself and carry on.

Save asks for a name, and that's what puts it under Custom Reports. Once it's saved, the tabs above the shape rail do everything around it. Scheduled emails it daily, weekly or monthly to up to five people. Links makes a share link anyone can open without logging in, with a password, an expiry or a view limit if you want them, and the same link comes as CSV, Excel, JSON, or a Google Sheets formula that stays live. Download, above the preview, is the quick version, Excel or CSV, right now.

Back to Reports. The pre-built ones are the same in every event, and you can't break them. Run Report on Sessions with Speaker Details and you've got the export most teams asked support for. Contact reports for speakers, chairpersons, moderators and submitters, group reports for exhibitors and sponsors, and the evaluation plan reports, evaluator progress, every individual rating, and the aggregated scores per session. Custom reports, once you have them, are also where Schedule and Share live, on the card's menu.

One more thing before we leave reports. The same page exists at the organization level, reports in the org sidebar. Same builder, same gallery, but the data is every event you run, so a report on speakers who've spoken more than once is built here, not inside one event.

Now the tables themselves, because most days you don't need a report, you need the table to show the right things. Any module table, sessions here, has Columns, Sort and Filter on the right, and they all open the same Preferences drawer. Columns is a checklist on the left and the selected set on the right, drag to reorder, up to twenty five, with Reset to Default if you get lost. Filter gives you contains, is, is empty and so on, per field. Apply Changes puts it on the table.

Applied isn't saved. Once you've applied a change, Saved Views lights up and offers Save as View, give it a name, and now it's a view every admin on the event can pick from this same menu. Come back later, change a filter, and you get Save Changes, Save as New View, or Reset Changes. Rename and delete are in here too. A view per committee, a view for the AV team, a view for the printer.

And the plain file. Options, top right of any module, then Export CSV or Export Excel, and the export uses the columns of the view you're looking at, so shape the view first. Download files bundle pulls the files attached to these records into one zip. Two things to know about the file. Any file field comes through as a public link, and every date and time is written in the event's timezone with the zone named next to it, not in yours.

Who can do all this is set on the role, under Data and Insights, view reports, create, share, schedule, manage dashboards. The Help Center articles are Build a report, Module table views, and Exporting data.
