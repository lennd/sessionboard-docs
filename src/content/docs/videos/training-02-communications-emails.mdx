Now the emails. Everything you built in the portal only matters if speakers know it's there, so this chapter is about getting the right message to the right people, and knowing it arrived. Three parts. Templates you write once, the send itself, and the emails the platform sends without you.

Templates live under Settings, Email Templates. Four come with every event, Accept, Decline, and the one-day and five-day reminders for a closing form. You can edit them, you can't delete them. Everything else here is yours, and the list is alphabetical, so a naming habit like "Speaker, dash, something" keeps them together.

Let's make one for the portal link. Add Template, then Add Template again from the menu. A name, this is internal, your speakers never see it. Then the type, and this is the decision that matters. Contacts is for people. Exhibitors and Sponsors is for companies. Sessions is for the session itself, so that's where accept and decline live.

The type decides which merge tags you get. If you change it after you've written the body, the tags you already placed no longer resolve and you'll have to put them back. So pick the type first, write second.

Reply To is where answers land, and it takes one address, so use a shared inbox rather than someone's personal one. Sender Name is what shows in the inbox, your event name is usually right. CC and BCC take up to five each. Then the subject.

The body is where merge tags earn their keep. Merge Tags, top right of the editor. Search for portal, and there's the Portal Login Link, as a hyperlink or the raw address. One click drops it at the cursor, and every speaker gets their own link. First Name works the same way. One template, a personal email for each person. Add Template, and it's on the list.

Sending happens where the people are. In Contacts, the trick is to filter first and select second. Filter to accepted speakers, or one track, or whoever hasn't finished a task, then tick the rows, or the box at the top for the whole page. Send, then Send Emails. Up to a hundred per send, so a bigger list goes out in pages.

The left side is the envelope. Who should receive this email, and whether to copy their additional contacts, that's the assistant or the co-author on their record. Replies sent to, Sender Name, CC and BCC, same as the template. On the right, Template pulls in what we just built, and Merge Tags are here too if you want to tweak one send.

Review, and this is the step I'd never skip. Every recipient is listed. Click a name and you see their actual email, subject, their name, their portal link, filled in. If a tag is empty for someone, you'll see it here rather than hearing about it later. Send Now goes out, and we'll leave that for the real thing today.

For sends you repeat, or an audience over a hundred, Email Campaigns under Send filter by role, status and custom fields, and can leave out people who already got it. Same templates, same tags.

Now the emails you don't send. Back in Email Templates, the System tab lists every one of them, grouped by area. Account emails, invites and sign-in codes, are always on. Submission confirmations and closing reminders come from your forms. Evaluation notices go to your reviewers. And the Portal group is the one to know.

Two of these are switches on each portal, under Portal Notifications. Email Notifications tells a speaker the moment a new task or form is assigned to them. The Weekly Digest goes out Monday morning with whatever is still open. Both are per portal, so your sponsor portal can be quieter than your speaker portal. Accept and decline are not on this list, those stay yours to send.

Last thing, proof. History, Emails. Sent Emails is every message, yours and the system's, with a delivery status per recipient. When a speaker says they never got it, this is where you look first, then their address on the contact, then their spam folder. The Errors tab is for the ones that bounced.

That's communications. One template, one filtered send, one preview, and a portal that reminds people for you. The Event setup chapter covers what sits underneath all of this, the details, record settings, custom fields and how your team gets in. The Help Center article is Create and send emails, and your CSM can review your templates before the first send.
