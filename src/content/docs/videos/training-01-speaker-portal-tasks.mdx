Let's talk about the portal. This is where your speakers land once they're in, so everything we set up here is what they'll see. Think of it as their to-do list for your event, and your view of who has done what.

Portals live here in the left navigation, under Portals. Every event starts with a Default People Portal, and every contact in your event lands there unless a custom portal's filters pick them up first. This event has a Speaker Portal for accepted speakers, and that's the one we'll build on.

Under Portals you'll see the things you can put in one. A task is a plain action item, register for the event, book your hotel, that kind of thing. A file request is a task that needs an upload, like a signed agreement. And a form collects structured answers, so a headshot and a bio in one go. Today we'll build a task.

On the Tasks page I'll open Add and choose Add Task. First a name. Keep it in the speaker's words, so "Confirm your travel dates" rather than "Travel confirmation". Then the type. Contacts means people, your speakers, moderators, chairs.

Groups is for sponsor and exhibitor companies, so one task lands on the company and anyone on their team can complete it.

And Submissions attaches the task to each session instead of each person, which is what you want for something like uploading slides. Ours is about the person, so Contacts it is.

The description is where you tell them what good looks like. You can type one description for everyone, or choose Use Field to pull it from a contact field, so each speaker sees their own. Same idea for the task link. If every speaker has a personal registration link stored on their record, Use Field opens theirs. Links need the https prefix or they won't resolve. Create Task, and it's saved.

A task on its own doesn't show up anywhere yet. It has to be assigned to a portal. Back on the portal list, the three dots next to the Speaker Portal, then Edit Tasks. That opens the portal's Assign Items step. In the Tasks section I'll click Add, tick our task, and Add Selected. A task can sit in more than one portal, so you build it once.

Now the pencil under Actions, because this is where the settings that matter live. Alias lets the portal show a different name. Due Date is a real deadline, once it passes they can't mark it complete, they see a message with the date instead. If you know people will be late, set the Extended Due Date now, before the deadline, and they get a grace window. Required puts it at the top of their list. And Make Completed Tasks View-Only stops someone unticking a task by accident.

For a Submissions task there's one more control in here, Assign By Filter. Say the task is "Upload your slides" and only breakout sessions need it. Add a filter, format is Breakout, and speakers on a keynote never see it. Up to three filters per task. Ours is a contact task, so we don't see it today. Update, and we're set.

One setting trips people up, so let's look at it. Under Configuration, Always Show Tasks. When it's off, tasks only appear for speakers whose session is accepted. That's usually what you want during review, nobody gets homework before they're in. When it's on, everyone in the portal sees every task, including moderators and anyone still pending.

Before you send anything, look at it as a speaker would. View Portal, top right, then View portal as, and pick one of your real contacts. This is the same screen they'll get, same task order, same due dates. I do this every time I change a portal, it catches a lot.

Back in Contacts, tracking is a column, not a report. Open Columns, find the task, and it joins the table. Each row tells you where that person is. Green check, done. Blue circle, assigned through the portal and still open. Orange checklist means someone on your team assigned it by hand. Save the view and it's there every morning.

If a handful of people need something extra, you don't need another portal. Select them here, Assign, then Tasks, pick it, set the due date, and it lands only on those records. Anything assigned this way can be unassigned from the same column. Tasks that came through the portal can't, which is a good reason to keep portal tasks to the ones everyone needs.

That's the portal. Speakers get a clear list, you get a clear column. Next we'll set up the emails that point them to it, the templates, the merge tag for the portal link, and the reminders the portal sends on its own. The full write-up is in the Help Center under Portals 101, and your CSM is happy to walk your team through your own setup.
