Interest forms. Between calls for papers, the good speakers still turn up. Someone emails a chair, someone hands over a card at a booth, someone fills in a web form that goes to a spreadsheet nobody owns. An interest form is a standing "speak with us" page for the whole organization, open all year, and every submission lands in your Speaker CRM Pipeline as a card, with the person saved as a contact. Nothing to import, nothing to chase.

It lives at the organization level, under CRM in the sidebar, because it belongs to no single event. Interest forms is part of Speaker CRM. Four tabs across the top, Forms, Submissions across every form, Speakers, and Evaluations. The counters are the whole picture, how many forms, how many are open, and how many submissions and people have come in. This organization has one open form, with twelve submissions from twelve people.

How is this different from a call for papers? A call for papers is an event's form, with a deadline, and its submissions live inside that event. An interest form sits above the events. It can point at one upcoming event, several, or none yet, and what comes in waits in the CRM until you decide where it belongs. Use the call for papers when a program is open. Use the interest form for everything that arrives in between.

Open the form and you get its dashboard. Status and the total up top, Settings for duplicate, archive and delete, and Edit, which is the same wizard you built it with. Submissions and speakers with a View All each, and a setup checklist that ticks itself off, fields, notifications, events, and whether the form is on a site yet. If a form is not collecting anything, this checklist is the first place to look.

The Submissions tab is one row per proposal. Its ID, the title, who submitted it, the events they picked, when, and the pipeline stage it is in right now, which you can change from here. Search and filters up top, export, and Add Submission for the ones that arrive by phone or in a hallway, so they get the same treatment. Select a few rows and the bar offers Add To Event, Send Email and Download Files. Open in Pipeline takes the whole list to the board.

One thing worth knowing if your form collects headshots or abstracts as files. Select the rows and Download Files packages every file from those submissions into one zip, sorted by submission and named after the question each one answered. It builds in the background and you collect it from History and Exports.

Speakers is the same data by person. Name, email, company, and the submission they came in on. Everyone here was created as a contact in the CRM the moment they submitted, so they show up in your directory, in segments and in emails like any other contact. One person can submit more than once, and this is where you see that.

Edit opens the wizard, five steps. Details first. The internal name is for you, the public title and description are what speakers read, so this is the place to say what you cover, travel, hotel, a fee, before anyone asks. Opens and closes at, and the form closes itself on that date. Then the decision that shapes the whole form. Sessions and speakers, a session proposal with the people on it, or speakers only, just the person. Below that, how many speakers a session may have, and the pipeline stage new submissions land in.

Events is which upcoming events a speaker can express interest in. Only future events are offered. And you can skip it. If the event is not created yet, publish the form anyway and add the event later. People will have interest before you have a program, and that is the whole point.

Fields is the form itself, in two sections. Session information, where the title is required, and speaker information, where first name, last name and email are. Add Field pulls from your global session and contact field libraries, the same fields your events use, so nothing you collect here needs mapping later. A field you create here is added to that library for every program. Preview form shows it the way a speaker will see it.

Managers are co-admins for this one form. They can work the submissions, change its settings and run its review, without being given anything else in the organization. Add an existing admin, or invite someone new.

Notifications. A confirmation to the submitter is on by default, and Customize lets you change the sender, the subject and the body. Admin notifications tell your team when something new arrives. Save Form finishes the wizard. I will go back rather than save, since this form is already live.

A form is not reachable until it is on a site. Sites, under Program, and this organization has a global site for speakers. In the builder, Programs and CFP lists every form you could offer, tick the interest form, and the canvas switches to what a signed in speaker sees. Open programs, the form, its window, and Start Submission. Publish makes it live. That is the link you put on your website and in your speaker emails.

From the speaker's side it is short. They sign in to the site, pick the events they are interested in, fill in the session and their own details, and submit. They get a confirmation on screen and by email, and My Submissions in the portal sidebar shows them what they sent, so nobody has to email you to ask whether it arrived.

And here is where all of it lands. Pipeline, under CRM. Every submission became a card in the Identified column, tagged with the form it came through. Drag a card to move it, Researching, Approved, Contacted, and so on, or open it for Move to, its stage history and activity, with a link back to the submission it came from. When you are ready, Assign to event moves the person and the proposal into the event you are programming.

If a committee should score these before you commit, the form's Evaluation tab holds one evaluation plan, with rounds, scorecards and assigned evaluators, the same model as your event evaluations, run on the submissions while they are still in the CRM. The Evaluations tab on the Interest Forms page lists every such plan across the organization.

So, one form, on for the whole organization, published on your site, and then you work the Pipeline. A speakers only form is a plain contact form that lands in your CRM, so it can double as an intake for anyone you want to hear from, sponsors included. Set it up before the event exists, and say what you cover in the description. The Help Center article on interest forms has the full wizard and the FAQ, and the Pipeline article covers stages and what happens to the people once they are in.
