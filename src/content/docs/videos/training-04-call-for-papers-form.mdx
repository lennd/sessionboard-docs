Now the form. For most of your speakers the submission form is the first time they meet your event on a screen, so it's worth building carefully. Two halves today. The program vocabulary the form draws from, tracks, formats, roles, statuses. Then the form builder itself.

Program, then Settings. This page is the vocabulary. Anything you define here shows up as a dropdown on the form, a filter on the sessions list, and a color on the agenda. So a track you add here is a question your submitters answer and a column your reviewers sort by, all at once.

Tracks are the broad themes attendees filter by. Formats are the shape of the session, keynote, workshop, panel, poster. Levels and languages work the same way, and tags are the loose keywords for things like sponsored or live-streamed. Fill these in before the form goes live. Adding a format after fifty submissions means fifty sessions with a blank.

Roles, and this one answers a question we get on nearly every call. The submitter is the person who fills in the form, and they may never set foot on stage. Speakers present. A chairperson oversees the session, a moderator runs it, introductions, timing, questions. Only admins can add chairs and moderators, submitters can't. Manage means the role shows up as a Contacts view. Portal Access means they can sign in. And you can rename any of these to your own terms.

Statuses. Pending, accepted, rejected and the queues are built in, you can reorder them but not rename them. If your committee thinks in stages like shortlisted or needs revision, add a custom status here. Any status, standard or custom, can be the one a form drops new submissions into, and we'll see that setting in a minute.

Program, Forms. Every event starts with one form, and you can have several, an abstract call and a session call, say. Add, Create Form starts a new one. The three dots on a form give you Edit, the submissions and drafts that came through it, the public link, and duplicate, which is how most people build next year's form. Let's open this one.

The builder walks down the left. Submission Setup first. Abstracts or Sessions decides what the form collects, and the Participants switch decides whether there's a step for speaker details at all. Then the Welcome Screen, the message and terms people see before they start. Say what you're looking for and when it closes, that's the whole job of this screen.

Session Information is the heart of it. A section title and a short page heading, that's the tab name submitters see. Then the questions. Title is locked, every session needs one. Everything else you control. Required is a switch on each row, and the padlock makes a field view-only, useful when you're pre-filling something and don't want it edited.

Add Field. Search the fields that already exist, this is why we built fields in the last chapter, they're waiting here. Create Field makes a new one without leaving the form, event or global, same choice as before. And Add Section Element is for a header, a divider or a block of text between questions. One thing to know, evaluators will see these field names when they review, so name them the way you'd want a reviewer to read them.

The three dots on a question. Customize question changes the label and help text your submitters see, and sets a word or character limit with a live counter. Edit field changes the field itself, everywhere it's used. And Use question rules is how one form handles several session types. Format is Workshop shows duration and room setup, Format is Poster hides them. Build every field first, save, then add the rules.

Participant Information. Up top, the roles this form lets a submitter add, and a minimum and maximum for each. Total across all roles caps the whole session. Below, the questions per person. First name, last name and email are locked, that's how a speaker becomes a contact. Biography and mobile are optional until you switch Required on. Ten speakers is the most the public form will take, whatever you set here.

Form Settings. Open and close dates, and once there's a close date, draft reminders go out five days and one day before. Advanced gives certain answers a different deadline, posters get an extra month. Set Submission Limit caps how many one person can submit through this form, and it counts drafts. Default status for new submissions is the setting I mentioned, lightning talks can land straight in the accept queue. Auto-redirect sends them to their portal after the confirmation page. And Notifications, the next step, is who on your team hears about each new submission.

View Form, top right, opens what your submitters get. Welcome, account, submission, participant, review. Same headings you just wrote. Walk through it once as a submitter before you send the link, and read every help text out loud. If a question needs explaining on a call, it needs better help text.

That's the form. Vocabulary first, then one form with rules instead of five forms. Next is integrations, getting registrations in, and sessions and speakers out to your website and your app. The Help Center article is Session submission forms, and Copy Link on the form is what goes in your call for papers email.
