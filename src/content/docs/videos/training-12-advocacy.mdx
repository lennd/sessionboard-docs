Advocacy. Your speakers already tell people they're speaking. The trouble is you never see it, half of them post a screenshot of the agenda, and you can't tell which post sent anyone to registration. Advocacy gives every speaker, sponsor and exhibitor their own branded share card and their own tracked link, ready to post in one tap. And it shows you who shared, what got clicked, and who drove the traffic. No chasing anyone for a screenshot.

It lives under Marketing in the event sidebar, and it's on for every event, you don't need any extra module for it. Campaigns are always built inside an event. Advocacy in the organization sidebar is the read-only roll-up of every event's campaigns, handy for reporting across a season.

Three things to have ready. The link you want people to land on, usually registration, and if your event website is set under Settings and Event Details it's already filled in. The people, speakers on the event, or sponsor and exhibitor groups, with email addresses if you want Sessionboard to notify them. And your event branding, logo and colours, because the cards build themselves from it.

Advocacy opens on Campaigns. Three tabs, the campaigns themselves, analytics for the whole event, and activity, the raw feed. Each row is one campaign, its status, who it's for, how many recipients, and shares and clicks so far. This one is live, with speakers, and it's already moving. New campaign is how you start another. Let me open the live one first.

A campaign page. Up top, the destination every share points at, preview as any recipient, edit the campaign, and the actions menu, notify recipients, export assets, pause, end, delete. Then the numbers. Recipients assigned, shares across channels, clicks on the destination, and clicks per share. Top advocates ranks people by clicks and then shares, and channel mix shows where the shares happened. This is the view you'd otherwise be assembling from screenshots.

Recipients. One row per person, whether they've opened their share page yet, their shares, clicks, and last activity. Every recipient has a magic link to their own share page, no login, no account, and you can copy it or view the campaign exactly as they see it. Message all emails everyone at once, and Add recipient brings in a speaker who joined the program late.

Activity is the same story as a feed, opens, shares and clicks in order, with the country on each click. It answers did anything happen since yesterday, and the search box narrows it to one person.

The Analytics tab rolls every campaign on the event together. At a glance, then the funnel, assigned, opened, shared, clicked, so you can see where people drop. Channel mix, top advocates across campaigns, geography, and by campaign at the bottom. Both tables export to CSV, and Export assets, back in the actions menu, hands your marketing team every rendered card and tracked link in one file.

New campaign opens Quick Launch, five steps. Build is where you decide who's sharing. Speakers, which is everyone in a speaker, moderator or chair role. Groups, your sponsor and exhibitor companies, which needs Sponsor Pro or Exhibitor Pro. Or attendees. Then a format, a static image or an animated video card. The designer pulls colour, font, logo and background from your event, and the strip along the bottom is every card style, previewed as a real speaker. Session cards are on the other tab. Nothing here needs a designer.

Recipients is who, exactly. All speakers, whoever a portal targets, or select and pick people by hand with a filter. All speakers is the usual answer, and you can add a late one from the campaign later.

Share console is the important one, because it's what your speakers will see. On the left, the default message, with short, long, professional and casual variants and a generate button, and the link placeholder that becomes each person's tracked link. The register link, a promo code if you have one, and the channels they can share to. On the right, a live simulation of one speaker's page. Their card, their headshot, their message, and one tap to post. Change anything on the left and the preview updates.

Landing is the branded page people see for a moment before they're forwarded on. You can turn it off and send them straight through, or pick a layout, spotlight, banner, editorial or minimal, with the font and colour inherited from the card. Content is the copy, tracking is the UTM parameters, and there's a mobile and desktop preview.

Review and launch. The cards going out, as one speaker will see them, the recipient count, templates, channel, destination and landing page. Activate campaign makes it live. And the step people miss, activating tells no one. Notify, on the success screen and in the actions menu afterwards, emails each person their personal link. Until you send it, the funnel sits at assigned. I'll go back rather than activate, since this event already has its campaign.

How the links work, since it's what makes the numbers trustworthy. Every recipient gets a short link, sessionboard dot com slash r and an eight character code, theirs alone. A click on it is recorded, your UTM parameters and promo code are applied, and the visitor is forwarded to your destination, through the landing page if it's on. Because the link is per person, every share and click attributes to the advocate, not just the campaign.

If you take one thing from this, it's that a campaign is three decisions, who shares, what the card looks like, and where the link goes, and then one email to notify. The Help Center article on Advocacy campaigns has the export formats and the FAQ. Sessions and the agenda is where the session cards get their content, and Exhibitors and sponsors covers the groups you can invite to share.
