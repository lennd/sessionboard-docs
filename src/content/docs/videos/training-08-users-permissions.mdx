Users and permissions. Short chapter, but it's the one that decides whether your track chairs, your AV vendor and your reviewers see the right amount of the event, and not more. Event Team, bottom of the left nav.

Everyone with a login to this event, with their role, last login and tags. The chips split it, event admins, and portal only access, which we'll come back to. Add, Invite User is how someone joins, that was chapter three, and the menu on each row is where you edit their role, resend an invite or remove them from the event. A locked account, five wrong passwords, is unlocked from here too.

Permissions is the tab that matters. Down the left, the default roles, admin user, admin lite, session manager, evaluator session manager, evaluator, the content, communication and reporting roles, sponsor and exhibitor managers, AV manager, and portal user. Pick one and the grid on the right is exactly what it can do. Admin user is everything, sessions six of six, submission forms four of four, and so on down. Default roles can't be edited or deleted, which is a feature, you always know what admin means.

Now evaluator. Zero of six on sessions, zero on forms, zero on people. The one thing it can do is view assigned evaluations, my evaluations. That's why we said invite your committee as evaluators. They log in, they see their queue, and none of the rest of this exists for them.

Session manager is the track chair role. Three of six on sessions, view, update and export, no adding, no deleting, no changing the fields. And when you invite one, there's an extra step, a filter, status, track, format, source, so a track chair sees only their track's sessions and speakers. Evaluator session manager is the same idea for someone who both runs a track and scores it.

If none of those fit, create new role. Name it, describe it, and then tick the grid yourself, view, add, update, delete, per module. Faster is to duplicate a default role from its menu and adjust, a session manager who can also export, say. Custom roles can be edited and deleted, and deleting one asks where its users should go.

Fields is the second layer. Contact fields, session fields, group fields, and for each one, this role can view it, lock it, see but not change, or hide it. Bulk actions do a whole list at once. This is how you hide phone numbers and compensation from the volunteers, or lock the accepted status from anyone who isn't on the committee. Admins always see everything, and changes here save as you go.

One distinction that clears up a lot of tickets. Team members are people you invite here, they log into this admin app with a role. Speakers, sponsors and exhibitors are contacts, they use the portal, and they hold the portal user role automatically. They never see this side. So if a speaker asks for a login, the answer is their portal link, not a seat on the team.

Event roles are per event. Someone who should be in every event goes on the organisation team instead, back to organisation, settings, team. Organisation access reaches every event as an admin, events-only access is the people who've been invited to specific events. When you clone an event next year, the event team comes with it.

That's access. Default roles first, a custom role when you must, field permissions for the sensitive columns, and speakers never need a seat. Next is the last of the nine, contacts and imports, getting people and sessions into Sessionboard in bulk and keeping duplicates out. The Help Center article is Invite and manage event team members.
