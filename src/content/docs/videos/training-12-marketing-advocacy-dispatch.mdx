Advocacy and Dispatch. Your speakers already tell people they're speaking, the trouble is you never see it, and you can't tell which post sent anyone to registration. Advocacy gives every speaker, sponsor and exhibitor their own branded share card and their own tracked link, and shows you who shared, what got clicked, and who drove the traffic. Dispatch is the same machinery pointed inward, at your own sales, membership and marketing teams.

Marketing is its own group in the event sidebar. The overview is the whole journey on one page, source and produce on top, distribute underneath. Every event gets the overview, Advocacy, Transcribe, Media, Create and Slides. When your organization has the full Marketing module, the group grows, Dispatch, Plan, Voices, Posts, Quotes, Clips, and Brand, so don't worry if yours is shorter than mine.

Advocacy opens on Campaigns. Three tabs, campaigns, analytics for the whole event, and activity, the raw feed. Each row is one campaign, its status, who it's for, how many recipients, and the shares and clicks so far. This one is live, ten speakers, and it's already moving. New campaign is how you start another. Let me open the live one.

A campaign page. Up top, the destination every share points at, preview as any recipient, edit the campaign, and the actions menu, notify recipients, export assets, pause, end, delete. Then the numbers. Recipients assigned, shares across channels, clicks on the destination, and click rate. Top advocates ranks people by clicks then shares, and channel mix shows where the shares happened. This is the screenshot nobody has to chase.

Recipients. One row per person, whether they've opened their share page yet, their shares, clicks, and last activity. Every recipient has a magic link to their own creator console, no login, and you can copy it or view the campaign as them. Message all emails everyone at once, and Add recipient brings in someone who joined the program late.

Activity is the same story as a feed, opens, shares and clicks in order, with the country on each click. It's the answer to did anything happen since yesterday, and the search box up top narrows it to one person.

The Analytics tab rolls every campaign on the event together. At a glance, then the advocacy funnel, assigned, opened, shared, clicked, with the drop at each step. Channel mix, top advocates across campaigns, geography, and by campaign at the bottom. Both tables export to CSV, and the organization has the same view across all your events under Advocacy in the org sidebar.

New campaign opens Quick Launch. Five steps, build, recipients, share console, landing, launch. Build is where you decide who's sharing, speakers, or sponsor and exhibitor groups, and pick a format, a static image or an animated video card. The card builds itself from your event branding, and the strip along the bottom is every card style, previewed as a real speaker, with session cards on the other tab. Nothing here needs a designer. I'll go back rather than continue, since this event already has its campaign.

One thing to remember on any new campaign. Activating it doesn't tell anyone. Notify recipients, in the actions menu, sends each person their personal link, and until you send it your funnel stays at assigned. Sending it is the moment the campaign starts.

Dispatch, right under Advocacy. Same tracked links and leaderboard, different audience. You package event content into a kit, posts, quote cards, clips, pick a goal like registrations or attendance, and deliver it to your own team through Slack and email. Tracked mode gives each teammate a personal link, so you see who posted and what it drove. It's in Early Access, so ask for it from the Preview page if the group doesn't show it yet.

If you take one thing from this, it's that a campaign is only three decisions, who shares, what the card looks like, and where the link goes. The Help Center article on Advocacy has the landing page and export options in detail. Sessions and the agenda is where the cards get their content, and Communications and emails covers the notify message itself.
