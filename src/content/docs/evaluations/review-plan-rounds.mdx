A **round** is one pass of review with its own dates, scorecard and reviewers, for example *Initial screen → Peer review → Committee decision*. A plan starts with Round 1; add more when submissions need more than one look.

<Note>
Part of [Set up a review plan](/evaluations/setting-up-round-based-evaluations). Wizard steps: [Type](/evaluations/setting-up-round-based-evaluations#choose-what-to-review) → [Overview](/evaluations/review-plan-overview) → **Rounds** → [Scorecard and reviewer view](/evaluations/review-plan-scorecard) → [Reviewers](/evaluations/review-plan-reviewers) → [Assignments](/evaluations/review-plan-assignments) → [Ballot](/evaluations/review-plan-ballot).
</Note>


The Rounds step (labeled "Review rounds" in the sidebar) is where you configure review rounds (similar to award program rounds). A default Round 1 is created automatically. Click + Add round to add additional stages.

To remove a round, open it, expand **Dangerous** at the bottom of its settings and click **Delete round**. Its submissions move back to the previous round, its reviews and assignments drop out of reports, and you can restore it from Event History → Trash. Round 1 can't be deleted, so for a single-round review simply don't add more rounds.

![The Rounds step with the Add round button and Basic information showing Round name and a locked Round type of Review only](/images/kb/review-rounds-basic-information.png)

## Why use it

Rounds let one plan mirror how your committee actually works: a quick triage pass, then a deeper read of what survived, then a final decision, each with its own dates, scorecard and reviewers. Without rounds, every reviewer scores everything the same way at the same time.

## Basic Information
- **Round Name:** A descriptive label (e.g., "Round 1," "Peer Review," "Final Selection").
- **Round Type:** Select the round type (e.g., "Review Only"). This setting cannot be changed after creation.

## Voting
- **Who reviews this round:** Choose **Reviewers** or **Public voting** for each round (one audience per round; a plan can mix both across rounds).
  - **Reviewers:** Invited reviewers score an assigned queue in the reviewer portal.
  - **Public voting:** Anyone with the Site link scores the same scorecard. Whether they sign in first depends on the round's **Voter sign-in** setting (below). Public voters are not added as invited reviewers — they join when they save a score.

![Reviewers and Public voting tiles, with Public voting marked SELECTED](/images/kb/public-voting-audience.png)

- **Voting Type:** Choose the voting method (e.g., "Score Voting"). Review plans use scorecard scoring.

### Voter sign-in and vote limits

Selecting **Public voting** reveals two more settings under the audience picker:

- **Voter sign-in** — what voters must do before their vote counts:
  - **Inherit Site settings** (default): Voters sign in however your Site allows (magic link and/or SSO).
  - **Magic link**: Voters must confirm their email with a one-time sign-in link before voting, even if the Site also allows SSO.
  - **IP address only**: No sign-in — anyone with the link votes immediately. Each vote records the voter's IP address.
- **Votes per voter** — the maximum number of submissions each voter can vote for in this round. Leave blank for no limit. Voters see their budget on the ballot (for example, "2 of 2 votes used"), and revising a score on a submission they already voted for does not use another vote.

![Voter sign-in options and the Votes per voter limit on a public voting round](/images/kb/public-vote-signin-settings.png)

<Note>
With **IP address only**, votes are not tied to a confirmed email. Sessionboard limits repeat voting by IP address on a best-effort basis, but shared networks and VPNs can defeat it. Choose **Magic link** when vote integrity matters more than voter friction.
</Note>

### Ballot: show all sessions or a random subset

Under **Ballot**, choose which sessions each voter is asked to score:

- **Show all eligible sessions** (default): every voter can browse and score every session in the round.
- **Show a random subset per voter**: each voter sees a fixed number of sessions (**Sessions per ballot**, up to 10,000), assigned the first time they open the ballot and kept across visits. Sessionboard spreads sessions evenly, so with 50 sessions and 20 per ballot each session is shown to roughly the same number of voters. If the round has fewer sessions than the ballot size, voters see all of them.

**Votes per voter** still applies inside the subset and cannot exceed the ballot size.

Which sessions are on the ballot at all is set on the wizard's [Ballot step](/evaluations/review-plan-ballot). A voter's random subset is dealt from that list the first time they open a round that has sessions on it; a round with nothing on the ballot deals no ballots, so voters are not locked into an empty page.

When you add sessions to the round later, voters whose subset is still short of **Sessions per ballot** are dealt the new sessions on their next visit — including after voting has started. Voters who already have a full subset keep it. Sessions removed from the round disappear from every ballot.

**Reset ballots** (in the round's Ballot settings) re-deals everyone the next time they open the ballot. It is only available before the first vote is saved; once a vote exists the button is replaced by a notice showing how many ballots have been dealt and how many votes have been cast.

### Who can vote: eligible-voter lists

**Who can vote** limits the ballot to a list you upload, or lets anyone who signs in take part:

- **Anyone who can sign in** (default): every signed-in voter counts once, with the same weight.
- **Only people on the eligible-voter list**: under **Eligible voters**, click **Upload list** and choose a CSV or paste rows of `email, group`, where the group is **Ticket holder** or **Community member**. Uploading replaces the previous list; someone on both lists is kept as a ticket holder. **Download list** exports what is currently loaded.

Voters confirm their email with a magic link. If that email is not on the list they see *Voting is limited to current event ticket holders and Pro community members.* — on the ballot, on direct links to a submission, and when saving a vote.

Random ballots and eligible-voter lists both need a signed-in voter, so **IP address only** is not available while either is on.

![Ballot set to Show a random subset per voter with Sessions per ballot 10, and Who can vote set to Only people on the eligible-voter list, showing Ticket holders ×2 and Community members ×1](/images/kb/eval-public-vote-ballot-eligibility.png)

### Weighted results

On rounds with an eligible-voter list, ticket-holder votes count twice (×2) and community-member votes once (×1). A voter on both lists counts ×2, not ×3. The weight is recorded with a voter's first vote and kept for the round.

The round's results table adds **Weighted avg** and **Distinct voters**, with a filter for **All voters / Ticket holders / Community**. Participation counts are always distinct people. The cumulative export adds Submitter, Weighted average, Distinct voters and per-group voter counts and averages; the individual export adds Voter email, Voter group and Vote weight.

When the round is set to Public voting, a **Public voting** banner on the round includes **Copy link** and **Open**. Share that Site URL. If the event does not have a Site yet, **Create a Site to share** creates one. Voters land on the Program Site ballot — not on **My Reviews**. See [Program site](/site/program-site).

![Public voting banner on the review Rounds tab with Copy link and Open](/images/kb/eval-public-voting-copy-link.png)

Pasting the vote URL into Slack, LinkedIn, or similar shows a branded preview of the round. A link to one submission shows that item. Blind review rounds never put a name or headshot in the preview. Search engines and AI assistants do not index these URLs.

![Voting settings of a Public voting round, with Voter sign-in options, Votes per voter, Ballot choices and Who can vote](/images/kb/review-rounds-public-voting-settings.png)

## Round Mode: Funnel vs. Parallel

When configuring your review plan, you can now choose how submissions move between rounds:
- **Funnel (default):** Submissions must be explicitly promoted from one round to the next. Only promoted submissions appear in subsequent rounds. This is the traditional peer-review model where you triage first and advance only qualifying submissions.

Parallel: Submissions are reviewed across all rounds simultaneously — no promotion required. This is useful when every submission should go through every review stage regardless of earlier scores.

![The How should these rounds run? choice with Funnel selected beside Parallel, each with a diagram of how submissions move](/images/kb/review-rounds-funnel-parallel.png)

<Warning>
Choose your round mode before creating rounds — it changes how submissions move through the plan, so switching later is disruptive even where it is possible.
</Warning>

<Warning>
Round Type cannot be changed once the round is created. Make sure you select the correct type before proceeding.
</Warning>

Round mode also decides how you move submissions between rounds. **Promote** and **Demote** in **Sessions → Submissions** work on funnel plans only. For parallel plans, open the review plan's **Rounds** tab and move submissions between round buckets there.

Promoting needs the **Can promote sessions** permission (Event Team → Permissions → Review plans). Admins have it by default; give it to a custom role to let a session manager advance submissions. A team member with a session filter on their role can only promote sessions inside that filter. Demoting is not gated by this permission. See [Invite and manage event team members](/event-team/invite-manage-event-team-members).

## Abstaining

Reviewers can mark a submission as abstained when they have a conflict of interest or cannot review it. Configure this per round, under **Abstaining**:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Allow reviewers to abstain** | On by default. When off, reviewers cannot mark submissions as abstained in the review portal. |
| **Require a reason when abstaining** | Prompts the reviewer for a reason whenever they abstain. They cannot save the abstention without one. |

When a reason is required, choose how reviewers give it:

- **Free text** — the reviewer types their own reason.
- **Select from a list of options** — the reviewer picks from **Reason options** you define.

<Tip>
A fixed list of reasons is easier to report on than free text. Use it when you need to show a committee how many abstentions were conflicts of interest as opposed to reviewers simply lacking the expertise.
</Tip>

The same settings exist on Awards round settings, and behave identically there.

## Reviewer recommendations and session builds are not round-based only

The two settings below live on a round, but they are not limited to plans with several rounds. A plan with a single round has them too, in the same place — open the round and they are in its settings alongside abstaining.

Everything described in the next two sections therefore applies whether your plan runs one round or five.

## Reviewer recommendations

Turn on **Enable Reviewer Recommendations** to add a **Recommend** row to every submission card in the reviewer portal: reviewers mark each submission **Accept**, **Decline**, or **Alternate** alongside their scores.

The field picker under the toggle controls what **Alternate** means. Check the submission fields a reviewer may propose a different value for — Format and Track are typical — and the Alternate form in the portal shows exactly those fields, plus an optional note. Only dropdown-type fields are eligible.

![The Enable Reviewer Recommendations toggle with the alternate-fields picker](/images/kb/eval-round-recommendation-build-settings.png)

Recommendations are advisory. They never change the submission — they roll up in a **Recommendations** column on the plan's Submissions tab (accept / alternate / decline counts per submission, with each reviewer's suggested values in the tooltip) so your committee can act on them.

![A submission row with the Recommendations rollup column](/images/kb/eval-recommendations-rollup.png)

### Applying recommendations to submission statuses

When the committee is ready to act, you can turn recommendations into statuses in bulk. Two optional settings under **Enable Reviewer Recommendations** control this:

- **When applied, set status to** — pick the submission status that **Accept**, **Decline**, and **Alternate** each map to, or leave one on **Don't change status**. Nothing changes until you apply.
- **Acceptance targets** — how many sessions of each type you want accepted. Add a row per **Session type**, optionally narrowed to one **Track** (or **Any track**), with a **Target** and a **Weight**. Set **Group total cap (weighted, optional)** to limit the weighted total across all rows. Targets are guidance only: progress shows on the Submissions tab, and they never block a recommendation.

On the plan's Submissions tab, click **Apply recommendations** to open the apply sheet:

1. Each submission starts on its reviewers' majority recommendation, and ties go to **Alternate**. Change any row before you apply.
2. Check **Also apply alternate session type/track** to write the values reviewers proposed with their Alternate recommendations onto the submission.
3. Optionally add **Proposed tags**, choosing **Add to existing tags** or **Replace existing tags**.
4. Under **Set status to**, choose the status each decision sets — **Accept**, **Decline** and **Alternate** each take any status in the event, including your custom ones (for example *Preliminarily Accepted*), or **Don't change status**. It starts from the round's recommendation settings and applies to this run only; the round's saved settings are not changed. A warning shows when your choices would not change any status.
5. Click **Preview** to see every status change, then **Apply to N** to commit them. Changing a status choice clears the preview, so preview again before applying.

![The Set status to section of the Apply recommendations sheet, with Accept set to Accepted, Decline set to Declined, and the Alternate menu open on Don't change status and every event status, including custom ones such as Legal Review and VIP Meeting](/images/kb/eval-apply-recommendations-set-status.png)

When acceptance targets are set, an **Acceptance targets** panel on the Submissions tab shows accepted counts against each target. Reviewers see the same progress in an **Acceptance progress** strip in the portal.

## Reviewer session builds

Turn on **Enable Reviewer Session Builds** in the round's settings (the **Rounds** step) to let reviewers propose combining their assigned abstracts into full sessions — for example, grouping three related abstracts into one panel. A **Build sessions** button appears in their round view.

Reviewers who build sessions are called **team leads**, and a team lead is simply a reviewer on the round who has been given a session-build quota. Because team leads are picked from the round's reviewers, you choose them on the **Reviewers** step — after the reviewers are enrolled — not on the Rounds step. Under the toggle, the round shows how many team leads and pre-created sessions it has so far, and **Choose team leads in the Reviewers step** takes you straight there.

![The Enable Reviewer Session Builds toggle, with the team lead count and the Choose team leads in the Reviewers step button beneath it](/images/kb/eval-round-session-build-toggle-pointer.png)

On the **Reviewers** step, every round with session builds turned on gets a **Team leads (session builds)** section. Pick the round's tab (or stay on **All rounds** to see every such round), enroll the reviewers first, then:

- Under **Allocations**, click **Add allocation** and add a row per team lead and session type: which reviewer, which session type they may build, how many **Sessions**, how many presenting **Abstracts** fit in each, and how many **Alternates** each session may hold. Only reviewers enrolled on that round are listed. Click **Save allocations**.
- Under **Pre-created sessions**, you can add program sessions you have already scheduled for team leads to fill. Click **Add pre-created session**, pick the session, and either pin it to one team lead or leave it on **Any team lead** — an unpinned session is open to every team lead with an allocation for that session type. Each pre-created session counts as one session of that type's allocation.

![The Team leads section on the Reviewers step, with one allocation row for an enrolled reviewer and the Pre-created sessions list beneath it](/images/kb/eval-reviewers-team-leads.png)

If the round has no reviewers yet, the section says **Enroll reviewers on this round first** — add them from the list above it and the pickers appear. You can also reach this section later from the plan's **Builds** tab with **Manage team leads**.

Submitted builds land on the plan's **Builds** tab, where you approve, decline, or return them — nothing is created until you approve. See [Managing your review plans](/evaluations/setting-up-round-based-evaluations#deep-dive-view) below.

<Tip>
Tip: You can add as many rounds as you need. A typical 3-stage process might look like:
- Round 1: Triage (quick pass/fail by committee chairs)
- Round 2: Peer Review (detailed scoring by subject-matter experts)
- Round 3: Final Selection (committee vote on top-scored submissions)
</Tip>
