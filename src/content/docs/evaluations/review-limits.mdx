Review limits are a **round setting**, not an assignment setting. Open the round's settings panel and find **Review limits**. Each limit is a checkbox with a number, and both are optional.

![The Review limits group in a round's settings, with Close a submission after 3 completed reviews and Each reviewer can complete up to 10 reviews both ticked](/images/kb/review-limits-round-settings.png)

- **Close a submission after N completed reviews** (1 to 500). Every reviewer assigned a submission sees it until it has N completed reviews. After that it disappears for reviewers who haven't started it. Reviewers who already started can finish, so a submission can end with one or two more than N.
- **Each reviewer can complete up to M reviews** (1 to 5000). Reviewers see everything assigned to them and choose what to review. Once a reviewer has completed M, their remaining unstarted submissions disappear. Drafts they already started can be finished.

Rounds that leave both off work exactly as before. Review limits are not available for public (Community Polling) rounds.

## What counts and when a submission reopens

- Abstains count as completed reviews when **Count abstains as completed reviews** is on in [Program settings → Reviews](/sessions/program-settings#reviews).
- AI reviewers don't count toward M and aren't limited by it.
- If a completed review is removed, for example a reviewer is removed or an abstain is undone, the submission reopens automatically.
- Changing or turning off a limit mid-round applies immediately. Turning both off brings back everything that was closed.

## What reviewers and admins see

Reviewers see a banner, "You can complete up to M reviews in this round. You've completed X.", and a note that submissions close once they have N reviews. If a reviewer saves onto a closed submission, they see "This submission already has enough reviews." or "You've reached your review limit for this round."

In the admin progress view, a submission shows its completed reviews out of N. A submission that can't reach N because its reviewers all hit their own limit (M) shows **Closed: x of N reviews** instead of Complete, so you can see which ones need a follow-up.

## Review limits or up-front caps?

[Reviewers per submission](/evaluations/review-plan-assignments#reviewers-per-submission), [Max submissions per reviewer](/evaluations/review-plan-assignments#max-submissions-per-reviewer) and the [plan-wide limit](/evaluations/review-plan-assignments#max-submissions-per-reviewer-whole-plan) split submissions among reviewers in advance, so each reviewer sees only their share. Review limits let reviewers choose, and stop when enough reviews are in.

The recommended setup is to assign every reviewer all submissions, or all of a track's submissions, **without** the up-front caps, and let the review limits do the capping. The Assignments step warns when both are combined.

<Note>
When reviewers choose what to review, a submission nobody picks can finish below N. Watch progress and follow up with reviewers.
</Note>
