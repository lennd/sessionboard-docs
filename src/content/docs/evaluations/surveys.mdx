Surveys collect structured scores and comments **while the event is running** — floor reviewers walking rooms, committee members with an assigned station, or attendees scanning a QR code on their way out of a talk. Every response lands on the session, speaker, poster, or subsession it is about, and the results page turns into a committee packet you can print or export.

Surveys live in **Sessions → Evaluation → Surveys**.

<Note>
**Surveys** is an Early Access **Beta**. Open the **Surveys** tab and click **Request access** — our team reaches out to set it up with you. The [Early Access](/get-started/early-access) article covers how Beta requests work.
</Note>

## Surveys vs. evaluation plans

Both live under **Evaluation**, but they answer different questions:

| | Evaluation plans | Surveys |
|---|---|---|
| When | Before the event — reviewing **submissions** | During the event — scoring the **live program** |
| Who | Assigned evaluators signed into a portal | Named reviewers, walk-in attendees, or anyone with the link |
| About | Submitted sessions | Sessions, speakers, posters, or subsessions on the agenda |

If you are reviewing submissions to decide what makes the program, you want [evaluation plans](/evaluations/evaluation-plans). If people are scoring what is actually happening on-site, you want Surveys.

## Create a survey

<Steps>

<Step title="Name it and pick what people score">

Click **Add survey**, name it, and choose what each response is about:

- **Sessions** — one card per session.
- **Sessions + speakers** — session comments, then a card for each speaker on that session.
- **Speakers only** — one card per speaker, across every session they appear on.
- **Posters** — one card per poster (sessions with attached poster files).
- **Sessions + subsessions** — parent session comments, then a card for each subsession.

For the nested modes you also pick a **headline score** recipe: the session's own score only, an equal blend of session and speaker/subsession scores, or a weighted blend you control.

**Identity fields** decide what respondents are asked before they start. Leave them all optional for anonymous walk-ins; make any of them required to collect names on the open link.

</Step>

<Step title="Build the questions">

Add questions from the builder. Scored types roll up into each target's score; the rest collect context:

| Type | Scored | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 / 1–5 / 1–10 scale | Yes | Quick tap-to-rate scoring |
| Numeric | Yes | Custom min/max ranges |
| Dropdown | Yes | Custom options, each with its own point value |
| Checkboxes | No | "Pick all that apply" — topics covered, formats used |
| Yes / No | No | One-tap questions — "Was commercial bias present?" |
| Free text | No | The comments committees actually read |
| File upload | No | Photos or documents from the floor |
| Separator | — | Section headings inside the form |

Scored questions carry a **weight** (in percentage mode) so a headline criterion can count more than a nice-to-have. In nested modes there are two question lists — one about the session, one about each speaker or subsession.

You can also **reuse questions from another survey**: pick the source survey and its questions are appended as an independent copy.

</Step>

<Step title="Show a question conditionally (optional)">

Click the branch icon on any question to show it only when an earlier answer matches — for example, ask **"Describe the bias"** only when **"Was commercial bias present?"** is **Yes**.

Conditions key off a **dropdown, checkboxes, or yes/no** question. A required question that is hidden by its condition never blocks someone from finishing — it only becomes required when it appears.

</Step>

<Step title="Pick the agenda">

Choose which sessions the survey covers, or **sync from the agenda** to pull everything in. Speaker, poster, and subsession cards are derived automatically from the sessions you select.

</Step>

<Step title="Add reviewers (optional)">

For committee or floor-review workflows, add **named recipients** — one at a time or via CSV import. Each person gets a personal fill link tied to their name.

A recipient can also be assigned a **station**: a room plus an AM/PM slot and a role (floor reviewer, moderator, or attendee). Stationed reviewers open their link and see exactly the sessions running in their room during their slot — the list follows the agenda, including late room changes.

Skipping this step is fine: the open link and QR codes work without any recipient setup.

</Step>

<Step title="Distribute">

Open the survey (set status to **Open**) and share it any of three ways:

- **Open survey link** — one URL for the whole survey; respondents pick sessions from a searchable hitlist grouped by day or room.
- **Per-session links and QR codes** — each session card has its own link, so a QR by the door opens straight to that session's questions.
- **Personal links** — each named recipient's own URL, tied to their identity and station.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Filling a survey

Respondents see a mobile-friendly hitlist: search, filter by room, sort by time or room. Completed cards get a checkmark, and after marking one done the next unfinished card opens automatically. **Save draft** keeps partial answers; **Mark done** submits. Required questions are enforced on submit.

On the open link, respondents fill the identity fields you marked required (or none, for anonymous collection) — answers resume on the same device if they come back later.

## Results

The **Results** step is live as responses arrive:

- **Scores table** — every session with response count, its own mean score, and (in nested modes) the speaker/subsession mean plus the headline blend you configured.
- **By question** — each scored question's mean and response count; choice questions (dropdown, checkboxes, yes/no) show a bar chart of how often each option was picked.
- **Committee packet** — comments grouped by session, then by speaker, with the reviewer's name, role, and room on each line. **Print packet** turns it into a hand-out PDF.
- **Export CSV** — one row per response with reviewer, station, status, score, and every answer in its own column, ready for a spreadsheet.

<Tip>
Scores are a percentage of the maximum by default. If your committee thinks in raw points instead, switch the survey to **Points** scoring in the Basics step — weights are hidden and answers are summed as-is.
</Tip>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Do checkboxes and yes/no questions affect the score?">

No. Scores come only from scales, numeric questions, and dropdowns with point values. Checkbox and yes/no answers are reported as option counts in the results — they never raise or lower a session's score.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens to a required question that is hidden by a condition?">

Nothing — it only counts when it is visible. If "Describe the bias" appears only when bias is **Yes**, a reviewer who answered **No** can finish without it.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can the same person score more than one session?">

Yes — that is the default. The open link and personal links both show the full hitlist (or the stationed room list), and each session is scored on its own card.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do anonymous surveys work?">

Leave every identity field optional in the Basics step. Respondents on the open link go straight to the hitlist, and their rows show as **Anonymous** in the packet. Make fields required when you need to know who said what.

</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
