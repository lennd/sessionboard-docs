A scenario puts the learner in a situation: someone speaks, the learner chooses what to say or do, and their choice leads to the next scene. Some endings are the outcome you want; reaching one of those can complete the lesson.

![A scenario on a Learning Site where Dana, the customer, says her order is late and the learner chooses between two replies](/images/suites/learning/learning-scenario-site.png)

## Write a scenario

<Steps>
<Step title="Add the block">
In a lesson, type **/** and pick **Scenario**. It starts with two scenes: an opening scene with one choice, and an ending.
</Step>
<Step title="Write each scene">
For each scene, fill in **Who speaks** (for example, `Dana, the customer`), **What happens or is said**, and a picture if you want one. **Name (only you see it)** helps you find the scene when you link choices.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the choices">
Select **Add a choice**, write **What the learner says or does**, and choose where it leads: an existing scene, or **A new scene**. **Feedback after this choice** shows before the next scene, if you write some.
</Step>
<Step title="Mark the endings">
A scene with no choices is an ending. Tick **Reaching this ending completes the scenario** on the endings you want learners to reach.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide whether it's required">
Tick **Learners must reach an ending that completes it to finish the lesson** if the lesson should wait for it.
</Step>
</Steps>

![The opening scene of a scenario in the course builder, with Dana speaking and two choices that each lead to another scene](/images/suites/learning/learning-scenario-builder.png)

**Starts with** sets the first scene. Choices can lead back to an earlier scene, so learners can try again inside the scenario.

## Before you publish

While you write, the block lists what Publish will ask you to fix:

- a choice that doesn't lead to a scene, or has no text;
- a scene no choice leads to;
- no ending (a scene with no choices);
- a required scenario with no ending that completes it.

## What learners see

Learners read each scene and pick a choice. After a choice with feedback they see what they chose and the feedback, then **Continue**. At an ending, the scenario says whether it's the ending that completes it; at any ending they can **Start again**.

When a required scenario reaches a completing ending, the lesson is complete. Sessionboard checks the learner's choices against the published scenario, so a lesson is only completed by a real path to that ending.

<Tip>
Lesson pages can require one thing for learners to finish them: a video, a course package or a scenario.
</Tip>

<Note>
Ask can write a scenario for you from a description, for example "add a scenario where a customer complains about a late order, with one good ending". Read and edit it in the builder before you publish. See [Build courses with Ask](/sessionboard-learning/ask#build-and-edit-courses).
</Note>
