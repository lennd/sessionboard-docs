The form builder walks down the left side of the screen, one step at a time. This article follows the same order, then covers the question-level tools (customize, question rules, layout elements), multi-language forms, and the actions on the forms list.

<Note>
Events created before the newer forms interface use an older builder with four steps — **Welcome Screen**, **Session Information**, **Speaker Information**, **Form Settings** — and cap out at 20 submission forms. The settings are the same; only where they sit differs. Newer events aren't held to the form limit, though 20 distinct intake paths is a sign the routing belongs in [question rules](#use-question-rules) rather than in separate forms.
</Note>

## Where forms live

Open **Program → Forms**. Every event starts with one form, and you can have several — an abstract call and a session call, for example. Each card shows whether the form is **Open** or **Closed**, what it collects, and how many submissions and drafts came through it. **Add** creates a new form.

![The Submission Forms page listing each form with its status, submission counts, and drafts](/images/kb/forms-list.png)

The ellipsis (⋯) on a form holds everything else:

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Edit** | Open the form builder. |
| **View Submissions** | The sessions that came through this form. |
| **View Drafts** | Submissions started but not completed — see [draft submissions](/sessions/draft-submissions). |
| **View Form** | Open the public form in a new tab. |
| **Copy Link** | Copy the form's public URL for your call for papers email. |
| **Download Form** | Download a blank copy as a PDF or Word document, with every question laid out in order — see [Download a blank copy of a form](/concepts/download-a-blank-form). |
| **Duplicate** | Copy the form — how most teams build next year's form. |
| **Delete** | Remove the form from your event. |

![The ellipsis menu on a form — Edit, View Submissions, View Drafts, View Form, Copy Link, Download Form with its PDF and Word (.docx) choices, Duplicate, Delete](/images/kb/download-form-menu.png)

<Warning>
Duplicating a form copies the *fields*, not the field definitions. Editing a field on the copy — changing its dropdown values, for example — changes it on every other form that uses the same field.
</Warning>

## Build the form, step by step

Choose **Edit** to open the builder. The steps run down the left; **View Form** and **Copy Link** stay in the header so you can check the public form at any point. **Save** writes the step you're on.

### Submission Setup

Two decisions that shape the rest of the form:

- **Abstracts** or **Sessions** — what kind of submissions the form collects. Abstracts are short proposals reviewed before they become sessions; Sessions are full proposals with program details.
- **Participants** — whether the form has a step for speaker details at all. Turn it off for a form that only collects the proposal.

You can change both later by editing the form.

![The Submission Setup step with Abstracts and Sessions cards and the Participants switch](/images/kb/form-builder-setup.png)

### Welcome Screen

The first screen a submitter sees. **Internal Form Name** is what your team sees on the forms list; **External Form Title** is what submitters see. The **Welcome Message** is a rich-text block for what you're looking for, when the call closes, and any terms — **Show message** hides it without deleting it.

![The Welcome Screen step with the internal and external form names and the welcome message editor](/images/kb/form-builder-welcome.png)

### Session Information

The heart of the form. **Section Title** and **Page Heading** name the step — the page heading is the tab submitters see — and **Description & Instructions** sits above the questions.

Under **Form Questions**, **Title** is locked because every session needs one; everything else is yours. Each row has a **Required** switch, and the padlock makes a question view-only, which is useful when you pre-fill something and don't want it edited. Drag rows to reorder them.

![The Session Information step with the section title, instructions, and the list of form questions](/images/kb/form-builder-session-information.png)

**Add Field** opens the field picker. Search the fields that already exist on the event or organization, use **Create Field** to make a new one without leaving the form, or **Add Section Element** for a header, divider, or block of text between questions.

![The Add Field picker with Add Section Element, Create Field, and the searchable field list](/images/kb/form-builder-add-field.png)

<Warning>
If your event uses evaluation plans, the field names you create are visible to evaluators when they review submissions. Name them the way you'd want a reviewer to read them.
</Warning>

When you create a field, two options decide how far it reaches. **Field Description** is internal context for your team and is never shown to submitters. **Field Level** decides whether the field belongs to one event (**Event Field**) or to every event in the organization (**Global Field**). Field level can't be changed after the field is created.

Once fields are on the form, the ellipsis (⋯) on a question opens **Customize question**, **Edit field**, **Use question rules**, and **Remove from form** — each covered under [Question tools](#question-tools) below.

![The question menu on a form field — Customize question, Edit field, Use question rules, Remove from form](/images/kb/form-builder-question-menu.png)

### Participant Information

This step appears when **Participants** is on in Submission Setup. It has its own section title, page heading, and instructions, then two parts:

**Participant roles** — the roles a submitter can add (speaker, chairperson, moderator, and any custom roles from [program settings](/sessions/program-settings#roles)), each with a **Min** and **Max**. **Total across all roles** caps the whole session with **Total min** and **Total max**. When [primary speakers](/events/event-details) are enabled, **Max primary** per role and **Total max primary** cap how many participants can be marked primary; a limit of 0 hides the primary toggle. **Send submission confirmation email** per role decides whether people added in that role receive the confirmation too.

![The Participant Information step with the participant roles table and per-role limits](/images/kb/form-builder-participant-information.png)

<Warning>
Whatever you set here, the public form accepts at most **10** participants per submission.
</Warning>

**Form Questions** — the questions asked about each person. **First Name**, **Last Name**, and **Email** are locked; that's how a participant becomes a contact. Everything else — biography, mobile, headshot — is optional until you switch **Required** on.

**Unique Contact Settings** decide what happens when a submitter adds someone who already exists as a contact:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Allow users to submit new information for existing contacts** | On: the submitter can enter new details for an existing contact. Off: the existing contact must log in to the portal to update their own information. |
| **Notify existing contacts that they have been added to a submission** | Emails the existing contact so they can log in and check their details. |
| **Display existing values for unique contacts (read-only)** | Shows the contact's current values on the form instead of blank fields. |
| **Lock empty participant fields** | Keeps empty fields on an existing contact from being filled in by the submitter. |

### Payments & Fees

Appears on every newer form. Leave **When to Collect Payment** on **Do Not Collect Payment** for a free call. To charge a submission fee, choose **Upon Submission**, pick a **Payment Gateway**, set the **Base Fee**, and add **Pricing Rules** (by field answer or user attribute), **VAT Rules**, and **Promo Codes** as needed. Early-bird pricing works the same way as on awards forms — see [date & time tiers](/awards/awards-pricing-payments-invoices#date--time-tiers-early-bird-pricing).

### Form Settings

![The Form Settings step with deadlines, submission capacity, and the effective behavior summary](/images/kb/form-builder-form-settings.png)

#### Deadlines

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Open Date** | The form won't accept submissions until this date. Leave it empty to open immediately. |
| **Close Date** | Closes the form after this date. New submissions stop, and edits to existing ones are refused. |
| **Send Reminder Email** | Appears once a close date is set. Emails submitters who saved a draft but never completed it, five days and one day before the form closes. |
| **Advanced** | Conditional deadlines — a different close date for certain answers, such as an extra month for posters. |

<Note>
Reminder emails go to **drafts only** — someone who already submitted won't be chased. Edit the wording under **Settings → Email Templates**.

The option is unavailable while conditional deadlines are set, since there's no single close date to count back from.
</Note>

#### Submission capacity

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Set Submission Limit** | Limits how many sessions one user may have on this form. This counts saved drafts as well as submitted sessions — at the limit, submitters can resume a draft but can't start a new one. An event-wide limit from [session settings](/events/event-details) applies when no form-level limit is set. |
| **Allow multiple draft submissions** | Lets one submitter keep more than one draft in progress at a time. Off, the portal's Account step shows **Resume draft** only. |

The **Effective behavior** summary underneath restates what the two settings add up to, so you can read the result before you save.

#### New submissions

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Default status for new submissions** | The status every submission created from this form starts in. Choose one of the standard statuses — **Pending** (the default), **Accept Queue**, **Accepted**, **Decline Queue**, or **Declined** — or any custom status from **Program Settings → Statuses**. Each form has its own default, so a lightning-talk form can land submissions straight in Accept Queue while your main call for papers keeps them Pending. |

If you pick a custom status, whether submitters see its custom name or its standard category label follows that status's **Show custom status name to external users** setting. If the custom status is later archived, new submissions from the form fall back to Pending.

#### After submission

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Auto-redirect to speaker portal** | Sends the submitter to their portal 10 seconds after the confirmation page. With it off, they click **Continue to portal** themselves. |
| **Customize the success page message** | The message shown on the confirmation page after submitting, and after payment where fees apply. |

#### Submission PDF

**Generate a submission PDF** gives submitters a printable copy of their answers. The PDF is created on submit, attached to the confirmation email, and stored on the session.

#### Languages

**Enable multi-language submissions** shows a language selector on the public form. See [Multi-language submissions](#multi-language-submissions) below for what is translated and how.

### Notifications

The last step is who hears about submissions. Pick the admins to notify **when a new submission is received** and **when an existing submission is updated**. Under **Submitter notifications**, the **Submission Confirmation** email can be switched on or off and customized — from name, subject, and body with merge tags — and **Admin notifications** does the same for the emails your team receives.

![The Notifications step with admin recipients and the submitter and admin email templates](/images/kb/form-builder-notifications.png)

<Tip>
**View Form**, top right, opens exactly what your submitters get — welcome, account, submission, participants, review. Walk through it once as a submitter before you send the link, and read every help text out loud. If a question needs explaining on a call, it needs better help text.
</Tip>

## Question tools

### Customize question

Use **Customize question** to change how a field appears to submitters without changing the field itself.

- **Custom Label** — the name they see.
- **Help Text** — extra guidance under the field.
- **Length Limit** (text fields) — set a **Minimum** and/or **Maximum** length and choose whether to **Count** in **Characters** or **Words**. Submitters see a live counter under the field (for example "45 / 100 words"). An answer outside the limits blocks the step it sits on, so a submitter has to fix it before moving forward rather than discovering it at the end on Submit. The message names the field and the limit — "Answer is too long", with "Abstract must be 100 words or fewer (currently 150)" beneath it — and matches what the server says if the answer reaches it. Leave both blank for no limit.
- **Maximum file size** (file fields) — the size advertised on the form, up to 5 GB. Leave blank to inherit the field default (up to 5 GB). Session **Files** settings do not apply to this form.
- **Accepted file types (optional)** (file fields) — tick the formats this question takes, such as PDF only. Leave every box unticked to accept anything. Submitters see the list in the hint under the field, and a file that isn't one of those types is rejected — Sessionboard reads the file's contents, so renaming it doesn't get it through. See [Restrict a file question to certain file types](/faq/what-is-the-maximum-file-size-that-sessionboard-supports#restrict-a-file-question-to-certain-file-types).

On **Headshot** and **Logo**, **Help Text** you write is the only line under the field. Sessionboard does not add a second “Images only (up to 5 GB)” hint beneath it. If **Help Text** is empty, submitters see a type-and-size hint from the field's accepted types and this **Maximum file size** — for example “Images only (up to 2 MB)”, or “Images only (up to 5 GB)” when size is blank.

Other file fields still show the type-and-size hint even when they also have **Help Text**.

The photo editor still enforces [speaker headshot limitations](/events/event-details) (formats, dimensions, and the event file-size cap). Those limits do not change the hint.

### Edit field

**Edit field** changes the field itself — its type, options, description — everywhere it is used, on every form and every record. Use **Customize question** when the change should apply to this form only.

### Use question rules

**Question rules** (conditional logic) show or hide questions based on earlier answers, so one form can take in several session types: *Format is Workshop* shows duration and room setup, *Format is Poster* hides them.

Rules can hang off these field types:

- Checkbox
- Dropdown
- Number
- Country

<Tip>
1. Create and add all fields to your form, then save it, before applying question rules.

2. Open **Use question rules** on either side of the relationship. A **conditional question** only appears based on the answer to a previous question, and you can set that from either field:

   - Open the later question to set **Show this field when** (for example, Member ID shows when “Are you a member?” is Yes).
   - Open the earlier question — if it is a checkbox, dropdown, number, or country field — to set **This question controls** and choose every later question that should appear for a given answer (for example, Session Format is Workshop shows Duration, Room setup, and Max attendees).
</Tip>

Question rules only hide or show questions. To write other field values when the submission is saved, use an Assignment [Field Rule](/settings/field-rules).

### Layout elements

**Add Field → Add Section Element** inserts structure between questions:

- **Section Header** — a heading that groups related questions *(255 characters)*.
- **Divider** — a line between groups.
- **Rich Text** — a block of formatted text, links, or images.

### Event merge tags in form content

Form content can reference the event by name instead of hard-coding it — useful when you duplicate a form for next year's event or copy it between events.

Use **Insert Merge Tag** in the rich-text toolbar (or type the tag directly) anywhere in the form's written content: the welcome screen, page titles and descriptions, section headers, rich text blocks, and the confirmation message.

| Merge tag | Renders as |
| --- | --- |
| &#123;&#123;event_name&#125;&#125; | The event's name |
| &#123;&#123;event_slug&#125;&#125; | The event's URL slug |

Submitters see the resolved value on the public form. Anything that isn't a recognized tag is left exactly as you typed it.

## Multi-language submissions

**Form Settings → Languages** lets submitters switch the form into another language.

### How well each language is translated

Sessionboard ships maintained translations of the form's own wording — buttons, navigation, validation messages — for **English**, **French**, **Spanish**, and **German**. Any other language you enable is **machine-translated when the form loads**. It is not professionally localized and nobody reviews it, so treat that wording as a best effort. Anything with no translation available falls back to English.

The **Auto-translate the following** options decide which of your own content is machine-translated: page headers, titles, descriptions and terms; field labels without a saved translation; placeholders and dropdown values; and the confirmation message.

<Tip>
Where the exact words matter — a legal consent line, an award category, a question whose phrasing was negotiated — write them yourself as a [language variant](/settings/language-translation-variant) instead of leaving them to machine translation. A saved variant always wins.
</Tip>

### Right-to-left languages

Enable a right-to-left language — Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Urdu, Pashto, Kurdish, Divehi, Yiddish — and a submitter who picks it gets the form laid out right to left, not just translated. Text aligns from the right, and the reading order follows the language. Switch back to a left-to-right language and the form returns to normal.

Regional codes resolve to the same direction, so `ar` and `ar-SA` both read right to left. This applies anywhere portal translations are on: the submission form, the portal itself, and follow-up forms.

### Add a translated field to the form

Language variants you have enabled can go on the form itself, next to the default-language field.

In **Add Field**, a field that has variants appears as one collapsed row with a globe icon and a chevron. Click the row to expand it, and the translated versions of that field are listed underneath — click the one you want to add it. A variant you cannot find in the list is usually sitting inside a collapsed parent rather than missing.

Search finds variants as well as parents. Type the translated name and the field surfaces even though its row shows the default-language name, or type a locale code such as `ar` to narrow the list to fields that have an Arabic variant. When your search matches exactly one field through its variant, that row opens expanded, so the entry you searched for is the one you see.

### Replace or unrequire a locked standard field

Some standard fields carry a **Locked** badge, so they cannot be made optional or removed. **Title** is the one organizers hit most often.

The lock lifts as soon as a translated variant of that same field is on the form. Add the Arabic Title beside the English one, and the English Title's **Required** switch becomes editable and **Remove from form** appears. That is the supported way to make Title optional, or to replace the English Title with the translated one. Take the variant back off the form and the field locks again.

Hover the **Locked** badge and it says so, which saves guessing — the explanation appears only when that field actually has a translated variant available to add.

### What a multi-language submission stores

When a submitter fills in only the translated **Title** or **Description** — German only, say — Sessionboard also copies that text into the default-language Title and Description. The session never shows up untitled in lists, the agenda or exports. The translated value is stored as that language's variant as well.

Sessionboard also records the language the form was filled in. The session sidebar shows it next to the form name, for example **Submitted via Call for Papers 2027 · German**, and the Public API returns it too — see [which language a submission came in](/settings/language-translation-variant#which-language-a-submission-came-in). To fill in the other language across your submissions, use the **Update language value** [automation action](/automations/overview#update-language-value).

## Common questions

What event teams ask while building their first call for sessions, with the section or article that answers each.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Should we use a submission form, an application form, or a portal form?">

A **submission form** is for content decisions: it collects the speaker and the session, abstract, or poster they propose, and feeds Submissions, Evaluation, and the agenda. An **application form** is a yes/no about a person or company (scholarships, volunteers, invited-speaker confirmations). A **portal form** collects details from people already in the event. Interest forms source speakers year-round at the organization level. [Which form should I use?](/concepts/which-form-should-i-use) compares all of them.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="One form or several?">

Start with one. **Question rules** ([conditional logic](#use-question-rules)) show different questions by session type or any dropdown answer, so one form can carry general sessions, workshops, and posters. Create a second form when the audiences need different open and close dates, different welcome copy, or a different confirmation flow. Each form has its own dates, submission limit, default status, and notifications, and you can **Duplicate** one from its ellipsis menu.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Which fields should be required, and which can we leave optional?">

**Title** is always required on the session step, and **First Name**, **Last Name**, and **Email** on the participant step; everything else is your choice per field. Make required only what you need to evaluate the proposal (title, abstract, format, track) and collect the rest, such as headshots, full bios, AV needs, and disclosures, as [portal tasks](/portals/portals-101) after acceptance, when the speaker has a reason to finish them. Use **Customize question** to set length limits and help text, and [question rules](#use-question-rules) to show a question only when it applies.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can we change the wording, order, logo, and branding submitters see?">

Yes. The **Welcome Screen** carries your introduction; each step has its own title and instructions; **Customize question** sets a custom label and help text per field; and questions are reordered by dragging them in the builder. The event logo from [Event Details](/events/event-details) appears on the form, portal, and emails (see [event images and dimensions](/settings/branding-assets-dimensions)). Event merge tags let the form text reference the event name without retyping it ([event merge tags in form content](#event-merge-tags-in-form-content)).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can submitters save a draft and come back, or edit after they submit?">

Drafts are built in: a submitter can leave and return to the form, and [draft submissions](/sessions/draft-submissions) are listed under **View Drafts**. Turn on **Send Reminder Email** with a close date and drafts are chased five days and one day before it closes. After submitting, speakers edit their session from their portal for as long as the form is open and the portal allows it; the **Close Date** refuses edits as well as new submissions. See [can end users submit more than one submission?](/faq/can-end-users-submit-more-than-one-submission) for limits.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens to speaker and co-author records — will we get duplicates?">

Every person on a submission becomes a contact, matched on email address. The **Unique Contact Settings** on the Participant Information step decide whether a submitter may enter new information for an existing contact or whether that contact must log in to update it themselves, and whether existing contacts are notified that they were added. If duplicates still appear, [merge duplicate contacts](/contacts/merge-duplicates). Custom participant roles (co-author, moderator) are defined in [program settings](/sessions/program-settings#roles) and offered on the form.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How does the form connect to sessions, the portal, and speakers?">

A completed submission creates the session record (in the form's **Default status for new submissions**) and the participant contacts, links them, and sends the confirmation email with a link to the speaker portal. From there the session moves through [evaluation](/evaluations/evaluation-plans) and [accept or decline](/sessions/accept-decline); accepted sessions appear on the [agenda](/sessions/agenda) and in the portal. Turning **View Session Submission Form from Portal** on in the portal settings lets speakers reopen their submission.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do we share and test the form?">

**Copy Link** from the form's ellipsis menu gives the public URL; **View Form** opens it in a new tab. Submit a test proposal with a personal address before you announce it, then delete the test session and contact. See [how do I share my submission form?](/faq/how-do-i-share-my-submission-form).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who is emailed when a submission arrives?">

The submitter receives the **Submission Confirmation** email from the Notifications step. Admins you list there are emailed when a new submission arrives and when one is edited. Status changes later (accept, decline) are sent through [automated emails](/communications/automated-emails) and [email templates](/settings/email-templates).

</Accordion>
