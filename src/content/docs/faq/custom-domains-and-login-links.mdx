"Can this be on our domain?" has a different answer for email than for the pages people visit, and the two get mixed up — a DNS request from your IT team is almost always about email. Here is what each surface runs on and what you can change.

## Why use it

Organizers who have set up a custom domain will no longer see a confusing "certificate issuing" status that never resolves.

## At a glance

| Surface | Runs on | Your own domain? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emails Sessionboard sends for you | `no-reply@notify.sessionboard.com` | Yes — the [custom email domain add-on](/communications/add-on-custom-email-domain) sends from an address on a domain you own. |
| Portal login link | `app.sessionboard.com/portal-login/<event-slug>/…` for US-hosted organizations; EU-hosted organizations get an EU address | No. Link to it from your own site; the link is the same for every portal on the event. |
| Submission form link | A Sessionboard link | No. The form opens from its link — put a button on your site that points to it. |
| Program Site | `sites.sessionboard.com/s/<your-slug>` | No. The slug at the end is yours to choose; the host is Sessionboard's. See [Program Site](/site/program-site). |
| Agenda and speaker lists on your website | Your website | Yes — [Embeds](/sessions/embeds) are code you paste into a site you already own, so the address is yours. |

## Email: the add-on and its DNS records

The custom email domain is a paid add-on. Once it is on, an admin sets it up in **Settings > Domains**: enter the address, and the page shows the DNS records to add at your domain provider, or sends them to your IT contact. Sessionboard checks every 10 minutes and emails you when the domain verifies; from then on your sends, campaigns and automated notifications go out from your address instead of `no-reply@notify.sessionboard.com`. The domain must be a corporate one — not Gmail, Outlook or Yahoo — and **Change address** switches to another address later.

Plan for it: how long verification takes depends on how quickly the records are added and your DNS provider publishes them, so start before the first invitations go out rather than the day of. If the records are later edited or removed, sends from your address start being rejected; the article explains how to check the domain's authentication status and which record is failing. The whole process is in [Add-on: custom email domain](/communications/add-on-custom-email-domain).

<Note>
Setting up a sending domain does not change the portal or form links, and the portal link does not need any DNS work. They are separate questions.
</Note>

## Portal and form links

Every portal on an event shares one login link, copied from **Program → Portals** (the **⋯** menu on any portal card, then **Copy Link**). Whoever signs in lands in the portal their filters put them in, so you never need a different link per portal — see [Inviting users to the event portal](/portals/inviting-users-to-the-event-portal). The invitation email carries a personal link that signs the person straight in; anyone who comes back later without it signs in with the same email on the login page ([Signing in](/get-started/navigate-your-contact-portal#signing-in)). Copy the link rather than typing it: the address differs for EU-hosted organizations.

There is no single sign-in page across all your events. Each event has its own login link; someone on several events signs in on any one of them and uses **Switch Events** in the portal header menu to move between them ([access your other event portals](/participants/pp-how-to-access-additional-event-portals-you-are-associated-with)).

The submission form is hosted by Sessionboard too and opens from its link; it cannot be embedded in another page. Share it from a button or menu item on your website — [Share your submission form](/faq/how-do-i-share-my-submission-form) covers the link and the open and close dates.

<Tip>
Give the speaker-facing links a short, readable path on your own site — `yourconference.org/speakers` redirecting to the portal login, `yourconference.org/submit` redirecting to the form. People type what the printed program and your emails say, and a redirect you control is the piece you can change later.
</Tip>

## Your website: embeds

When the agenda and speakers should live on a site your marketing team already runs, build an Embed rather than a Site. The embed is a widget or feed you paste into that page, it refreshes on its own as the program changes, and it lives at your address. The outputs, fields and filters are in [Agenda & speaker embeds](/sessions/embeds); choosing between a Site and an Embed is in [Choose a Site, Embed, Poster Gallery or Content Gallery](/site/publishing-surfaces).
