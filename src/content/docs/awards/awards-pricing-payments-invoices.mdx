## When to charge for submissions

Not every awards program requires a submission fee — but for those that do, Sessionboard lets you configure pricing directly within the program setup wizard (Step 4: Pricing) or later by clicking **Edit** on your Program Dashboard and choosing **Pricing** in the sidebar.

The first choice you will make is When to Collect Payment. There are two options:
- Do Not Collect Payment — Submissions are free. The rest of the pricing configuration is skipped entirely. You can always enable payment collection later by editing your program settings.
- Upon Submission — Payment is collected at the time the submitter completes and submits their entry. The submitter must complete payment before the submission is finalized.

![Pricing with Upon Submission selected, showing the payment gateway picker, Base Fee, Date & Time Tiers, Pricing Rules, VAT Rules, and Promo Codes](/images/kb/awards-wizard-pricing.png)

<Tip>
If you are unsure whether to charge, you can leave pricing disabled during initial setup and enable it later. Changing from free to paid will only affect new submissions — existing submissions are not retroactively charged.
</Tip>

## Setting up a payment gateway
Before you can collect payments, you need a payment gateway connected to your organization. This is configured at the organization level, not per program — so once set up, any awards program in your organization can use it.

If no payment gateway is configured yet, the Pricing step will display a prompt with a Manage Payment Settings button. Clicking this takes you to your organization's payment settings, where you can connect a gateway.

Once a gateway is available, select it from the Select Payment Gateway dropdown in the Pricing step. The selected gateway will process all transactions for this program.

### Supported payment gateways

Sessionboard supports over 100 payment gateways. When adding a new gateway, you can choose from the following featured providers:
- Stripe — Cards, wallets, and bank transfers.
- Braintree — PayPal, Venmo, and cards.
- Authorize.net — Cards and eChecks.
- PayPal Commerce — PayPal, Pay Later, and Venmo.

For the full list, see [Awards list of supported payment gateways](/awards/awards-list-of-supported-payment-gateways).

If your preferred gateway is not listed, reach out to the Sessionboard team for assistance connecting it.

### Adding a payment gateway
Open your organization's **Settings → Payment Settings** and click **Add Gateway**. The page lists your gateways with their provider, status, currencies, and last use, and shows how many are active, in test mode, and which currencies they cover.

![The Payment Settings page with the Add Gateway button and the gateway table](/images/kb/awards-payment-settings.png)

Adding a gateway takes three steps:

- **Select Provider** — Choose your payment processor from the provider cards.
- **Configure Gateway** — Enter a **Gateway Nickname** (a friendly name such as "Stripe - Awards"), paste the credentials your provider asks for (for Stripe, a **Restricted API Key**), and turn on **Test Mode** to use sandbox credentials while you test. Expandable **Setup Instructions** explain where to find the key.
- **Currency Support** — Pick the currencies this gateway accepts, click **Test Connection** to confirm the credentials work, then **Add Gateway**.

<Tip>
Use Test Mode during setup to verify that payments process correctly with sandbox credentials before switching to your live key. Invoices from a test gateway carry a **Test** badge.
</Tip>

## Configuring the base fee

The Base Fee is the default fee charged for every submission in this program. It appears at the top of the pricing configuration and is set as a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $50 USD).

To set or change the base fee, enter the amount in the Base Fee field. This fee applies to all submissions regardless of category or other conditions — think of it as the starting price before any pricing rules adjust it.

If your program is free, leave the base fee at $0 (or select "Do Not Collect Payment" in the step above).

## Date & time tiers (early-bird pricing)

Date & Time Tiers let the submission fee change automatically on a schedule — early-bird, regular, and late pricing without anyone editing the program on cutoff day. While a tier is active, its price **replaces the base fee**; pricing rules, VAT rules, and promo codes still apply on top of the tier price.

Pick a mode from the **Mode** dropdown in the Date & Time Tiers section:

- **Off — base fee only** — No date-based pricing. This is the default.
- **Sequential windows** — Each tier has its own start and end date/time. The active tier is the one whose window contains the current moment. Windows cannot overlap; gaps are allowed, and the editor warns you about them (during a gap, the base fee applies).
- **Cutoff dates** — Each tier has a single cutoff. The price steps up after each cutoff passes — classic "prices go up March 1" behavior. Cutoffs must be in increasing order.

For each tier, set a **Label** (submitters see it at checkout, e.g. "Early bird"), a **Price**, and the date/time bounds for your mode. All times are interpreted in your event's time zone, which the editor shows explicitly, and a status line tells you which tier is active right now.

![The Date & Time Tiers editor in Sequential windows mode, with an Early bird and a Regular tier](/images/kb/date-tier-pricing-editor.png)

When a tier is active at checkout, submitters see the tier price along with a note about when the current pricing ends (e.g. "Early bird pricing ends Mar 1, 2026"). If no tier matches — before the first window opens or after the last cutoff passes — the base fee is charged.

<Tip>
Date & Time Tiers are available on awards program pricing and on the **Payments & Fees** step of newer (v2) submission forms. Each completed payment records which tier was applied, so you can reconcile early-bird vs. regular revenue from the invoices export.
</Tip>

<Note>
If you previously simulated early-bird pricing with conditional pricing rules and manual edits on cutoff day, you can move that schedule into Date & Time Tiers and keep your pricing rules for genuinely conditional adjustments (categories, member discounts, and so on).
</Note>

## Pricing rules (conditional fees)

Pricing Rules let you adjust the submission fee based on specific conditions. This is useful for scenarios like category-based pricing, early-bird discounts, or tiered fees based on submission attributes.

To add a pricing rule, click **Add Rule** in the Pricing Rules section. Each rule follows an If / then structure: pick a form field, a condition (**is**, **is not**, or **contains**) and a value — for example "Selected Category is Lifetime Achievement" — then define how the fee changes. The action can be one of three types:
- Set fee to — Overrides the base fee entirely with a specific dollar amount for matching submissions.
- Add — Adds a fixed amount on top of the base fee.
- Subtract — Reduces the base fee by a fixed amount (useful for discounts tied to specific conditions).

Pricing rules are evaluated in order and applied on top of (or in place of) the base fee, depending on how you configure them.

<Tip>
Categories can be linked to pricing rules. If you set up your categories in Step 2 of the wizard, you can reference them here to create category-specific pricing — for example, charging different fees for different award tiers or grant types.
</Tip>

## VAT rules

If your organization needs to collect tax on submission fees, use the VAT Rules section to configure tax rates. VAT rules are applied to the overall total in a user's cart after the base fee and any pricing rules have been calculated.

Click **Add VAT Rule** to create a new rule. Each rule requires a Label (e.g., VAT, GST, Sales Tax), a Type, and an Amount. The type can be either:
- Add additional % — Applies a percentage-based tax on top of the total (e.g., +20%).
- Add flat fee — Adds a fixed amount to the total regardless of the submission fee.

<Note>
VAT rules are optional. If your organization is not required to collect tax on submission fees, you can skip this section entirely.
</Note>

## Promo codes

Promo codes give you the ability to offer discounts to specific submitters. When a promo code is active, applicants can enter it at checkout to receive a reduced fee.

To create a promo code, click **Add Promo Code** in the Promo Codes section at the bottom of the Pricing step. Enter a Code (the text string applicants will type at checkout, e.g., EARLYBIRD), select a Type, and set an Amount. The discount type can be:
- Percentage off — Reduces the submission fee by a percentage (e.g., 20% off).
- Flat amount off — Deducts a fixed dollar amount from the submission fee.

<Tip>
Promo codes are useful for offering complimentary submissions to sponsors, VIP members, or early-bird entrants. Create a unique code for each use case so you can track which discounts are being redeemed.
</Tip>

## Managing invoices
Once your program collects payments, every transaction is tracked as an invoice. You can see them in two places:

- **Program-level:** The **Invoices** tab on the Program Dashboard lists this program's payments, with **Total Collected**, **Pending**, and **Total Refunded** above the table.
- **Organization-level:** From **Program → Awards**, click **Invoices** to see payments across all programs, with four summary cards: **Total Collected**, **Pending**, **Refunded**, and **Failed Payments**.

Each row shows the invoice number, applicant, submission ID, amount, status, payment method, date, and refund date. Use the search box to find an invoice, **Export** to download the list, and the row's **Actions** menu to **View Receipt**. A submission's own **Invoices** tab, in its detail sheet, shows the same payments for that one entry.

![The program-level Invoices tab with totals, search, Export, and the payments table](/images/kb/awards-program-invoices.png)

![The organization-level Invoices view with its four summary cards and the Invoices and Reconciliation tabs](/images/kb/awards-org-invoices.png)

### Refunds

If a submitter needs a refund — a duplicate entry or a withdrawn nomination, for example — contact [support@sessionboard.com](mailto:support@sessionboard.com) with the invoice number. Refunds go back through the gateway that took the original payment, and the invoice then shows a **Refunded** status and a **Refunded At** date, which also feeds the Refunded total.

<Note>
Processing times depend on the gateway and the submitter's payment method.
</Note>

## Reconciling payments with your gateway

Your invoices are Sessionboard's record of what was charged. Your payment gateway holds the money and keeps its own record of every transaction. The **Reconciliation** tab puts the two side by side, one row per payment, so you can see where they disagree — a card the gateway charged that your invoice records as failed, or an invoice marked paid that the gateway never settled.

Open it from **Program → Awards → Invoices** and select the **Reconciliation** tab. Events that collect fees on a submission form have the same tab on the event's **Invoices**.

![The Reconciliation tab on the Awards Invoices page, comparing each invoice's status with the payment gateway's](/images/kb/awards-invoices-reconciliation-tab.png)

Alongside the invoice number, program, and amount, each row shows both sides of the comparison:

- **Sessionboard** — the status on your invoice, such as `completed`, `refunded`, or `failed`.
- **Gateway** — the gateway's own verdict for that transaction, such as `succeeded`.
- **Charge ID** — the transaction identifier from your processor, for example a Stripe `ch_` ID. Click it to copy it, then search for it in your gateway account.
- **Paid** and **Refunded** — the times recorded on the invoice.
- **Reconciliation** — the result of comparing the two. Hover a label to see what it means.

Where your processor provides one, a receipt icon at the end of the row opens the gateway's own hosted receipt for that charge. A **Test** badge next to an invoice number means the payment ran through a gateway configured in Test Mode.

### What each reconciliation result means

| Result | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Matched | Sessionboard and the gateway agree on this charge. |
| No gateway charge | Paid by promo code, $0, or collected outside the gateway, so there is no transaction to compare. |
| Not verified | This charge has not been checked against the gateway yet. |
| Refund not verified | Recorded as refunded here. A refund cannot be confirmed from the original charge, so check it in the export or in your gateway. |
| Mismatch | The two sides disagree about whether the charge settled. Money may have moved without being recorded as revenue. |
| Missing at gateway | Recorded as a card payment, but there is no gateway transaction to verify it. |
| Amount differs | The gateway charged a different amount than the invoice records. |
| Unresolved attempt | The payment attempt never finished. The card may still have been charged — look for a gateway charge matching the amount and time. |
| Not in Sessionboard | The gateway processed this charge, but no invoice here corresponds to it. This result appears in the export. |

The last five results mean someone needs to look. Click **Needs attention** to narrow the table to just those rows; when any appear on the page you are viewing, a banner counts them. Click it again to return to every payment.

<Tip>
Search accepts an invoice number or a charge ID. If your finance team asks about one specific transaction in your gateway account, paste the charge ID straight into the search box to find the invoice it belongs to.
</Tip>

### Exporting the comparison

**Export** produces the same comparison as a **CSV** or **Excel** file, which downloads when it is ready. Each row carries the invoice details, both statuses, the charge ID, and the reconciliation result, so you can send a finance team the whole picture in one file.

The organization-wide Awards export answers one question the table cannot: it also lists charges your gateway processed that have **no** invoice in Sessionboard. Because a gateway is connected to your organization rather than to one program or event, those charges belong to the organization as a whole, which is why they appear in the Awards export.

<Note>
The gateway is checked live as you page through the table. If it cannot be reached, a message names how many rows are affected and those rows show the result last recorded, rather than the table failing to load.
</Note>
