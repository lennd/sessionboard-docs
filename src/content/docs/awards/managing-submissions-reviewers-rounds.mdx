## Why use it

Running judging well means every entry is seen by the right reviewers, nobody is buried while someone else sits idle, and reviewers hear from you when they can actually start. The Review setup card, category matching and the reviewer pool take most of the hand work out of that: seat last year's judges in a couple of clicks, send each entry only to the reviewers who judge its category, and hold assignment emails until the review window opens.

## Tracking submissions

Once your program is published on a Site and taking entries, submissions show up in three places: the Program Dashboard, the program's **Submissions** tab, and the organization-level **Submissions** view across all programs.

### Program Dashboard

The Program Dashboard is the hub for one program. Its header shows the status and completed submission count, and the **Dashboard** tab summarizes where things stand: **Quick Views** (Submissions by Category, by Round, and Unassigned), the **Submission Funnel** (Draft, Submitted, Under Review, Accepted, Rejected), **Review Progress** by round, and a **Category Breakdown**. The tabs beneath the header take you to **Submissions**, **Rounds**, **Reviewers**, **Reviewer Tags**, and **Invoices**.

![The Program Dashboard with Quick Views, the Submission Funnel, and Review Progress](/images/kb/awards-program-dashboard.png)

### The Submissions tab

The program's **Submissions** tab lists every entry with its ID (in the format AWD-YYYY-NNNNN), submitter, email, category, submitted date, status, and whether portal edits are allowed. Use the **View All**, **Submitted**, **Drafts**, and **Similar** tabs to narrow the list, and the **Filters**, **Columns**, and **Sort** controls to shape it. **Import** brings in entries from a spreadsheet; **Export** downloads the current list.

![The Submissions tab of a program, listing entries with ID, submitter, category, submitted date, and status](/images/kb/awards-submissions-tab.png)

### Organization-level Submissions view

For a cross-program view, open **Program → Awards** and click **Submissions**. It lists entries from every awards program with the program name alongside, and four metrics at the top: **Total Applicants**, **Submissions Made**, **Pending Review**, and **Accepted**.

![The organization-level Submissions view with its four metrics and the cross-program table](/images/kb/awards-org-submissions.png)

### Submissions within a round

Open a round from the **Rounds** tab to see its **Submissions in Round** table: ID, Submitter, Submitter Email, Category, **Reviews** (completed reviews), **Avg Score**, and Status. The stats above it show Duration, Submissions, Reviewers, Reviews Done, and overall Review Progress.

![A round's detail page: stats row, the Submissions, Assign, Reviewers, Conflicts, and Reviewer Tags tabs, and the Submissions in Round table](/images/kb/awards-round-detail.png)

## Understanding evaluation rounds

Rounds are the stages submissions move through during review — from a single review round to a multi-stage pipeline with different panels and scoring criteria at each stage.

### Round types

- **Submission + Review** — Accepts new submissions and includes a review period. This is your first round, **Submissions and Review**, which every program starts with.
- **Review Only** — Evaluation only, with no new submissions. Rounds you add after the first are Review Only — use them for entries that advanced from an earlier round, such as a finalist stage.

The round type is fixed once the round is created.

### Round settings

Click **Edit Round** on a round to open its settings:

- **Basic Information** — Round Name (up to 255 characters) and Round Type.
- **Timeline** — When the **Submission Period** and the **Review Period** open and close. Reviewers can only save evaluations while the review period is open, and the submission period is what makes a program count as **Open** on your Site.
- **Reviewer Instructions** — Rich text shown to reviewers working this round.
- **Voting** — Who evaluates, the voting type, and the scorecard (below).
- **Privacy & Conflict of Interest** — Blind Review (below).
- **Abstaining** — **Allow reviewers to abstain**, and optionally **Require a reason when abstaining** as free text or a list of options.
- **Reviewer View Configuration** — Which fields reviewers can filter by, and which fields make up the submission card headline and body in the reviewer portal.

### Managing multiple rounds

The **Rounds** tab lists every round as a card: name, status (for example Active), type badge, start and end dates, reviewer and submission counts, and review progress. Click **Add Round** to add another stage.

![The Rounds tab with the Add Round button and a round card showing dates, reviewers, submissions, and review progress](/images/kb/awards-rounds-tab.png)

<Tip>
Rounds are managed here, not in the setup wizard. The wizard's Rounds step only lists them and links back to this tab.
</Tip>

## Adding reviewers

Reviewers evaluate and score submissions. Add them from the program's **Reviewers** tab, or from a round's **Reviewers** tab.

To recruit a panel — invite a list, import last year's judges, or publish an interest form — use the program's **Call for Reviewers** tab. See [Open a call for awards reviewers](/awards/call-for-reviewers).

### The Add Reviewer dialog

- **First Name**, **Last Name**, and **Email Address** are required.
- **Organization** — Used to flag conflicts when a nomination comes from the same organization.
- **Send email invite** — When on, the reviewer receives the **Review Invitation** email with a link to the review portal. You can customize that template under Notifications.
- **Assign to Rounds** (required) — Pick at least one round.
- **Categories** — Optional; makes it easier to filter and assign submissions to this reviewer.

![The Add Reviewer dialog with name, email, Organization, the Send email invite switch, Assign to Rounds, and Categories](/images/kb/awards-add-reviewer.png)

The Reviewers table shows each reviewer's organization, status, rounds, progress, tags, categories, and last active time. A **Conflicts** tab beside it lists declared and detected conflicts of interest.

### Add from your reviewer pool

To add several people at once, click **Add from reviewer pool** on a round's **Assign** tab (in the **Review setup** card) or in the Rounds step of program setup. The dialog lists people who have reviewed in your organization's other programs and are not in this program yet. Search by name or email, and click **Load more** to see more people.

1. Tick the people you want.
2. Under **Assign to Rounds**, keep or change the rounds. Every round is selected to start.
3. Optionally pick the categories they judge. **Match each submission to reviewers in its category** uses these categories when you assign.
4. Leave **Send invitation email now** off while you set up, or turn it on to email them the Review Invitation right away.
5. Click **Add *N* reviewer(s)**.

People who were already reviewers in this program are not invited again by a bulk add. To email them, use **Resend Invite** on the **Reviewers** tab.

### Reviewer Tags

Reviewer Tags label reviewers with skills or specialties — "Technical Expert," "Industry Veteran" — to simplify filtering and assignment. Manage them on the program's **Reviewer Tags** tab (also reachable from a round). Tags apply across the whole program.

![The Reviewer Tags tab of a program, with the Add Tag button](/images/kb/awards-reviewer-tags.png)

### Organization-level Reviewers view

For a cross-program view, open **Program → Awards** and click **Reviewers**. It shows **Total Reviewers**, **Active**, **Reviews Completed**, and **Avg Completion Rate**, with a table of every reviewer, their programs, progress, and average score.

![The organization-level Reviewers view with its four metrics and the reviewer table](/images/kb/awards-org-reviewers.png)

## Assigning reviewers

The **Assign** tab on a round decides which submissions each reviewer gets. It starts with a **Review setup** card and a **Current state** summary — submissions in the round, how many already have reviewers, existing assignments, and reviews completed — then walks through three steps.

![The Assign tab: Current state, step 1 Which submissions, step 2 How should they be distributed, and the workload constraints](/images/kb/awards-round-assign.png)

### Review setup

The **Review setup** card checks everything the round needs before reviewers can start:

- **Review window** — Whether the round's review period is **Open**, **Upcoming**, or **Closed**, in your organization's time zone. While it is upcoming or closed, reviewers are not emailed about assignments. With no review dates set, reviewing is open as soon as reviewers are assigned. **Edit review dates** opens the round settings.
- **Reviewers** — How many reviewers are in the round, with **Add reviewer** and **Add from reviewer pool**.
- **Reviewers for each category** — Any category with submissions that no reviewer judges, and how many reviewers have no category (they are skipped when you match by category).
- **Assignments** — How many submissions have no reviewer. **Match by category** ticks **Match each submission to reviewers in its category** in the steps below.

### 1. Which submissions?

- **All Submissions** — Every submission in the round.
- **By Category** — Only the categories you pick.
- **Individual Submissions** — Search for and select specific entries.

### 2. How should they be distributed?

Click **Add Reviewer** if the round has no reviewers yet, then choose a distribution:

- **All to All** — Every reviewer gets every filtered submission. Best for small rounds.
- **By Category** — Only reviewers tagged with the categories you pick are used.
- **By Reviewer Tag** — Reviewers with a given tag get the submissions.
- **Individual Reviewer** — Hand-pick one or more reviewers.
- **Match expertise** — Only pair judges with nominations whose category, expertise tags, or searchable fields overlap what they picked on the [Call for Reviewers](/awards/call-for-reviewers). Judges with no picks are not assigned by this rule.

When the program has categories, tick **Match each submission to reviewers in its category** to send each submission only to the round's reviewers who judge its category, spread evenly across them. Reviewers without a category are skipped, and the round's conflict-of-interest rules still apply. A warning names any category nobody in the round judges — those submissions get no reviewer until you give someone that category. The workload constraints below still cap the result.

Two **workload constraints** can cap the result: **Reviewers per submission** (for example, three reviewers on each entry) and **Max submissions per evaluator** (for example, no more than ten each).

### 3. Review and apply

The **Impact Preview** shows exactly what will happen before you commit: **Filtered Submissions**, **Reviewers in this round**, and **New Assignments**. Check the **By submission** and **By reviewer** lists — each row says how many assignments are new and how many are already in place — then click **Assign**. The button counts the evaluations it will create, for example **Assign 12 evaluations**, with the number of submissions and reviewers beneath it.

If you change the assignment and try to leave before assigning — another tab on the round, **View submissions**, the back button in the header, or **Cancel** — Sessionboard asks **You haven't assigned these submissions yet** and offers **Assign now**, **Leave without assigning** or **Cancel**.

![The Impact Preview card, showing filtered submissions, reviewers in the round, and new assignments](/images/kb/awards-round-assign-impact.png)

**When applying** controls what happens to assignments that already exist: **Add to existing** keeps them and adds the new ones, while **Replace not-yet-reviewed** removes assignments nobody has started before applying the new set.

## Scoring & voting configuration

Each round has its own scoring rubric, set up in the round settings under **Voting**.

![Round settings showing who evaluates this round, the Voting Type, and the Scorecard](/images/kb/awards-round-settings-voting.png)

### Who evaluates this round

Each round has one audience:

- **Evaluators** — invited judges score an assigned queue in the reviewer portal.
- **Public voting** — anyone with the Site link scores the same scorecard. Whether they sign in first depends on the round's **Voter sign-in** setting (below). Public voters are not added as invited reviewers; they join when they save a score.

![Evaluators and Public voting tiles, with Public voting marked SELECTED](/images/kb/public-voting-audience.png)

Selecting **Public voting** reveals two more settings under the audience picker:

- **Voter sign-in** — what voters must do before their vote counts:
  - **Inherit Site settings** (default): Voters sign in however your Site allows (magic link and/or SSO).
  - **Magic link**: Voters must confirm their email with a one-time sign-in link before voting, even if the Site also allows SSO.
  - **IP address only**: No sign-in — anyone with the link votes immediately. Each vote records the voter's IP address.
- **Votes per voter** — the maximum number of submissions each voter can vote for in this round. Leave blank for no limit. Voters see their budget on the ballot (for example, "2 of 2 votes used"), and revising a score on a submission they already voted for does not use another vote.

![Voter sign-in options and the Votes per voter limit on a public voting round](/images/kb/public-vote-signin-settings.png)

Below those, two more groups shape the ballot itself — the same controls as on an evaluation plan, described in full under [Community polling](/evaluations/setting-up-round-based-evaluations#ballot-show-all-sessions-or-a-random-subset):

- **Ballot** — **Show all eligible submissions** (default), or **Show a random subset per voter** with a **Submissions per ballot** count. Each voter's subset is dealt the first time they open the ballot and kept across visits; submissions added to the round later are dealt to voters whose ballot is still short. **Votes per voter** cannot exceed the ballot size. **Reset ballots** re-deals everyone until the first vote is saved; after that it gives way to a notice of how many ballots have been dealt and votes cast.
- **Who can vote** — **Anyone who can sign in** (default), or **Only people on the eligible-voter list**. Under **Eligible voters**, **Upload list** takes a CSV or pasted rows of `email, group` where the group is **Ticket holder** or **Community member**; uploading replaces the previous list and **Download list** exports it. Voters whose email is not on the list are told voting is limited. Ticket-holder votes count ×2 and community-member votes ×1, recorded with the voter's first vote.

Random ballots and eligible-voter lists both need a signed-in voter, so **IP address only** is not available while either is on. Save a new round once before uploading an eligible-voter list — the list belongs to the saved round.

On a public voting round, the round's submissions list adds **Weighted avg** and **Distinct voters** columns and a **Voter segment** filter (**All voters / Ticket holders / Community**) next to search. **Avg Score** stays the plain average.

<Note>
With **IP address only**, votes are not tied to a confirmed email. Sessionboard limits repeat voting by IP address on a best-effort basis, but shared networks and VPNs can defeat it. Choose **Magic link** when vote integrity matters more than voter friction.
</Note>

When the round is set to Public voting, a **Public voting** banner includes **Copy link** and **Open**. Share that URL. If the event does not have a Site yet, **Create a Site to share** creates one. The ballot lives on the Program Site. See [Program site](/site/program-site) and [Setting up your Sessionboard site](/awards/setting-up-your-sessionboard-site).

![Public voting banner on an awards round with Copy link and Open](/images/kb/awards-public-voting-copy-link.png)

Pasting the vote URL into Slack, LinkedIn, or similar shows a branded preview of the round. A link to one submission shows that item (title and photo when a photo field is on the submission card). Blind review rounds never put a name or headshot in the preview. Search engines and AI assistants do not index these URLs.

Add a photo or file-upload field to the round's submission card fields so Vote cards (and reviewer cards) show a thumbnail.

![Vote cards in grid view, including a submission thumbnail](/images/kb/public-vote-awards-grid.png)

### Voting type

**Voting Type** defines how reviewers rate submissions. **Score Voting** uses the configurable scorecard below.

### Building a scorecard

The scorecard is the set of questions reviewers answer for each submission. Click **Add Question** and pick a type:

- **1-3 Scale**, **1-5 Scale**, **1-10 Scale** — Rate on that scale.
- **Numeric Score** — Custom minimum and maximum.
- **Custom Dropdown** — Your own options, each with a point value.
- **Free Text** — Comments and feedback (not scored).
- **File Upload** — Reviewers attach a document.
- **Separator** — A visual divider between sections of the scorecard.

![The Add Question menu on a scorecard, listing the scoring, input, and layout question types](/images/kb/awards-scorecard-question-types.png)

**Conditional logic** on a question shows or hides it based on an earlier answer. The scorecard is per round, so different stages can use different criteria.

<Tip>
Design your scorecard before adding reviewers. A clear rubric makes reviews more consistent and reduces back-and-forth with your panel.
</Tip>

## Managing individual submissions

A new entry lands in the status configured for the program — **Submitted** unless you chose **Under review**, **Accepted**, or **Rejected** as the **Default status for new submissions** in the program's Identity step. When fees are due on submit, the entry shows **Pending Payment** first and moves to that status once payment clears.

Click a row on the Submissions tab to open the entry in a side sheet. The header shows the ID, status badge, and whether the portal is locked for edits, with **Edit** and **Preview as submitter in portal** buttons. Tabs switch between **Details** (the form answers), **Reviews** (scores and comments so far), **Invoices** (payments for this entry), and **Audit** (a history of changes).

![A submission's detail sheet with its status, the Details, Reviews, Invoices, and Audit tabs, and the header actions](/images/kb/awards-submission-detail.png)

The menu button in the sheet header offers:

- **Mark as Winner** — Opens the winner tagging flow (see below).
- **Reject Submission** — Marks the entry rejected and removes it from the active review queue.
- **Advance to Next Round** / **Demote to Previous Round** — Move the entry between rounds by hand.
- **Assign Reviewer** — Assign a specific reviewer to this one entry. The reviewer is emailed about the assignment only while the round's review window is open (or has no dates); before it opens or after it closes, the assignment is made without an email.
- **Portal submitter edits** — **Use program default**, **Force allow portal edits**, or **Force block portal edits**, overriding the program's editing policy for this entry.
- **Send Email** — Email the submitter from within the platform.
- **Download files** — Get the entry's uploaded files.
- **Print** — A print-friendly view of the entry.
- **Delete Submission** — Permanently remove the entry.

![The actions menu on a submission, from Mark as Winner through Delete Submission](/images/kb/awards-submission-actions.png)

## Blind review

For anonymous evaluation, turn on **Blind Review** under **Privacy & Conflict of Interest** in the round settings. It hides submitter and nominee identity from reviewers, so evaluations rest on the content alone.

![The Privacy & Conflict of Interest card with the Blind Review switch](/images/kb/awards-round-settings-privacy.png)

<Note>
Blind Review hides identity in the reviewer's interface only. Program administrators still see full submitter details. Turn it on before the review period starts so every reviewer has the same experience.
</Note>

## Winner tags & selection

Winner Tags are the labels you give winning submissions — "Award Recipient," "Runner Up," "Honorable Mention." They are configured in the setup wizard's Winners step and can be edited any time from **Edit** on the Program Dashboard.

### Creating winner tags

On the Winners step, click **Add Winner Tag** and give it a name and an optional description, or use **Quick Add** for the common presets. Create as many as your program needs — a primary winner, runners up, honorable mentions.

![The Winners step of the wizard, with Add Winner Tag and the Quick Add presets](/images/kb/awards-wizard-winners.png)

### Applying winner tags

When evaluation is complete, open a submission and choose **Mark as Winner** from its menu. Pick a winner type from the dropdown and click **Tag as Winner**.

![The Mark as Winner dialog with the winner type dropdown and the Tag as Winner button](/images/kb/awards-mark-as-winner.png)

<Note>
The dropdown lists the Winner Tags configured for the program. If it is empty or missing the tier you need, add the tag on the Winners step first.
</Note>

After tagging, a confirmation reports how many submissions were tagged and reminds you that **winners are not automatically notified**. You can **Send Winner Notification Email** right away, using the template under Notifications, or **Close** and send it later.

<Tip>
Finalize your Winner Notification and Non-Selection Notification templates before you start tagging, since the confirmation offers to send immediately.
</Tip>
