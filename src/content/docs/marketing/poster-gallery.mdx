A poster gallery turns the posters already uploaded to your sessions into a public, searchable page you can hand out as one link. Visitors browse by track or format, open a poster in the browser — PDF, image, video, and audio all render inline — and see it attributed to its session and authors.

<Note>
Poster Gallery is an Early Access **Beta**: request access per event from [Early Access](/get-started/early-access) and our team will switch it on for you. Once enabled, the gallery admin lives under **CMS → Poster Gallery**.
</Note>

## Why use it

A poster session is over in an afternoon; a gallery keeps the work findable for the rest of the year, under one link you can put in the app, the programme and the post-event email. Because it draws from the Poster upload slot speakers already fill, there is nothing to collect twice — a presenter uploads once and the gallery, the coverage view and the portal all agree on which poster is current. Embargo, consent and share-link controls mean you can open it before the meeting without surprising an author or a journal.

## Where posters come from

A gallery does not hold its own uploads. It collects the files speakers upload — or build — into the **upload slot** you point it at, from the sessions you select, so the workflow your speakers already know is what fills the gallery. Only the latest version of each poster shows, and draft or withdrawn sessions are excluded automatically.

That poster ask is defined as an **upload slot** of kind Poster under **Program → Settings → Files**. If your event still uses a single file request for every session, the Poster Gallery page says so and offers to switch:

![The "Posters need upload slots" notice on the Poster Gallery page, with Turn on upload slots and Open Files settings buttons](/images/kb/poster-gallery-upload-slots-banner.png)

**Turn on upload slots** flips the switch for you: your current due date and file types become the first slot, nothing else changes, and you can then add a Poster slot — with **Format is Poster** as its session filter, say — from the Files page. See [Upload slots](/sessions/enable-upload-download-content#upload-slots).

No poster file yet for some sessions? The **Poster Builder** can draft one from the session's abstract — see below.

## Create a gallery

<Steps>

<Step title="Open CMS → Poster Gallery">

The page counts how many posters are available in this event. Click **New Gallery** to create one and open the builder.

</Step>

<Step title="Choose the content">

On the **Content** tab, **Upload slot** decides which files count as posters: the gallery takes whatever speakers upload — or build — into that slot. New galleries pick your Poster slot for you; if you run more than one poster ask, choose the right one here. **Posters by file type (legacy)** instead takes any session file marked as a poster, whatever slot it is in — for events that collected posters before slots existed.

![The Upload slot select on a gallery's Content tab, set to Poster, with the hint that files uploaded or built into this slot are the gallery's posters](/images/kb/poster-gallery-upload-slot.png)

Then pick the **Session formats** and **Tracks** that feed the gallery — leave everything unchecked to include all of them. **Matching posters** previews exactly what a visitor will see, and **Poster coverage** splits the sessions into **Uploaded** and **Missing** so you can chase the gaps from one list. Coverage counts only the sessions the slot applies to, so a slot filtered to **Format is Poster** will not list your keynotes as missing a poster. The same numbers, for every slot at once, are on **Program → Submissions → Files → Slot coverage** — see [Who still owes what](/sessions/enable-upload-download-content#who-still-owes-what-slot-coverage).

</Step>

<Step title="Brand it">

On **Appearance**, set the **Public title** and **Description**, pick a **Theme** and **Accent color**, and add a **Logo** and **Banner** — you can reuse the event's own assets. A **Gallery sponsor** slot (label, logo, link) is there if a sponsor paid for the wall.

</Step>

<Step title="Decide what visitors can do">

**Behavior** holds the visitor-facing toggles — **Search**, **Allow downloads**, **Share actions**, **Bookmarks**, **Add to calendar**, and **Ask the author** — plus how the gallery is grouped (**Track**, **Session**, or none), the **Default sort**, and which **Filter chips shown to visitors** they get.

</Step>

<Step title="Set access, then go Live">

On **Access & Compliance**, choose who can view (see below), then flip the **Live** switch in the header. **Copy public link** gives you the URL to publish; **Preview** shows the gallery as a visitor before you share it.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Tip>
The header's **Share & print** menu has a **Download QR** for the whole gallery and **QR labels for poster boards** — one printable label per poster, so the physical poster hall links back to the online one.
</Tip>

## Who can view it

| Access mode | Who gets in |
| --- | --- |
| **Public** | Anyone with the link. |
| **Share link only** | The public URL is off; only the share links you create work. Each has a name, an optional password, and can be deactivated on its own. |
| **Gated — verified email** | Visitors verify their email first; optionally restrict to **Allowed email domains**. |

## Embargo and author consent

Both live on **Access & Compliance**:

- **Embargo until** holds posters back until a date and time in the event's timezone. **Teaser — show titles with a countdown** keeps the file itself locked; **Hide — show nothing until the embargo lifts** does what it says.
- **Require author consent** publishes only posters whose author accepted your **Consent statement** — presented as a required checkbox when they upload a poster in the portal. The Content tab's coverage warns how many selected sessions still lack consent.

## The visitor experience

Visitors search across posters, authors, and topics, filter by track and format, sort by title, first author, program number, or session time, and keep a browser-local **My list** of bookmarks. Opening a poster shows the file inline with its session, authors, an AI summary and author narration where you added them, a citation, a per-poster link and QR code — and, if enabled, downloads, calendar, and an **Ask the author a question** box. Questions are collected for moderation on the gallery's **Analytics** tab; they are not emailed to the author automatically.

**Analytics** also tracks opens, dwell time, and the searches that found nothing — the fastest read on which topics your audience wanted and could not find.

## Draft posters from abstracts

For sessions with no poster file, the Content tab's **Create drafts from abstracts** (or **Draft with AI**) opens the **Poster Builder**: a block-based editor (sections, key findings, charts, figures, references) with a title, a two- or three-column **Layout**, and an accent color. **Publish to gallery** renders it to PDF, files it in the session's Poster slot and adds it to the same content pool as uploaded posters — **Republish** after edits.

A session has one current poster. Publishing from the builder replaces an uploaded poster as the current one, and a speaker uploading afterwards replaces the built one; the earlier file stays in the session's version history. The gallery, coverage and the speaker's portal all show the same, latest poster.
