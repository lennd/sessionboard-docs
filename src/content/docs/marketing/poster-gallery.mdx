A poster gallery turns the posters already uploaded to your sessions into a public, searchable page you can hand out as one link. Visitors browse by track or format, open a poster in the browser — PDF, image, video and audio all render inline — and see it attributed to its session and authors.

<Note>
Poster Gallery is an Early Access **Beta**: request it per event from [Early Access](/get-started/early-access) and our team switches it on. Once enabled, the gallery builder lives under **Publish → Posters**. On an event without it, **Posters** is still in the sidebar and opens a page explaining what it does, with the request button.
</Note>

## Why use it

A poster session is over in an afternoon; a gallery keeps the work findable for the rest of the year, under one link you can put in the app, the programme and the post-event email. Because it draws from the Poster upload slot your speakers already fill, there is nothing to collect twice — a presenter uploads once, and the gallery, the coverage view and the speaker's portal all agree on which poster is current. Access modes, an embargo and author consent mean you can open it before the meeting without surprising an author or a journal.

## Before you start: collect posters in a Poster slot

A gallery does not hold its own uploads. It collects the files speakers upload — or build — into one **upload slot**, from the sessions you select.

Define that ask under **Program → Settings → Files**. Turn on **Use upload slots**, then add a slot whose **File kind** is **Poster**. The kind is what the gallery reads, so a poster ask must be of kind Poster whatever you name it.

A Poster slot carries its own:

- **Deadline** — the date the ask closes. Past it, the speaker sees the deadline and the reason rather than a drop zone.
- **Accepted file types and size** — refuse the wrong format at upload instead of chasing it later.
- **Sessions it applies to** — add **Session filters** such as *Format is Poster* so your keynotes are never counted as owing a poster.

Full setup, including reminders and the **Slot coverage** view that shows who still owes a file, is in [Enable, upload & download files](/sessions/enable-upload-download-content#upload-slots).

If your event still uses a single file request for every session, the Posters page says so and offers to switch:

![The "Posters need upload slots" notice on the Posters page, with Turn on upload slots and Open Files settings buttons](/images/kb/poster-gallery-upload-slots-banner.png)

**Turn on upload slots** does it for you: your current due date and file types become the first slot, nothing else changes, and you can then add a Poster slot from the Files page.

<Tip>
Give authors something to start from. On the gallery's **Behavior** tab, **ePoster template** takes a branded PowerPoint or PDF that authors download from their portal when they build their poster.

![The ePoster template card on the Behavior tab, describing a branded PowerPoint or PDF template authors download from the speaker portal, with an Upload template button](/images/kb/poster-gallery-eposter-template.png)
</Tip>

## Create a gallery

<Steps>

<Step title="Open Publish → Posters">

The page lists the galleries this event already has and counts the posters available to them. Click **New Gallery** to create one and open the builder.

![The Posters page listing two galleries for the event, each with a Gallery actions menu, above a New Gallery button](/images/kb/posters-list.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Choose which files count as posters">

On the **Content** tab, **Upload slot** decides what the gallery collects: whatever speakers upload — or build — into that slot. New galleries pick your Poster slot for you; if you run more than one poster ask, choose the right one here.

**Posters by file type (legacy)** instead takes any session file marked as a poster, whatever slot it is in — for events that collected posters before slots existed.

![The Upload slot select on the Content tab set to Poster, with the hint that files uploaded or built into this slot are the gallery's posters](/images/kb/poster-gallery-upload-slot.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Pick the sessions that feed it">

**Session formats** and **Tracks** narrow the gallery. Leave everything unchecked to include all of them. Each option shows how many posters it would bring, and **Show N with no posters** reveals the ones that would add nothing.

![The Session formats card with a Poster checkbox showing 16 posters and a "Show 8 with no posters" link](/images/kb/poster-gallery-session-formats.png)

![The Tracks card listing five tracks with their poster counts, from Research & Best Practices with 6 to Policy & Advocacy with 2](/images/kb/poster-gallery-tracks.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Check what you will get, and what is missing">

**Matching posters** previews exactly what a visitor will see — every poster the filters above let through.

![The Matching posters list showing thirteen poster titles and a Generate AI summaries button](/images/kb/poster-gallery-matching-posters.png)

**Poster coverage** splits the sessions in the gallery's scope into **Uploaded** and **Missing**, so you can chase the gaps from one list. It counts only the sessions the slot applies to.

![The Poster coverage card reading 11 of 12 sessions, with Missing (1) and Uploaded (11) tabs and the one session that has no poster yet](/images/kb/poster-gallery-coverage.png)

The same numbers, for every slot at once, are on **Program → Submissions → Files → Slot coverage** — see [Who still owes what](/sessions/enable-upload-download-content#who-still-owes-what-slot-coverage).

</Step>

<Step title="Name it and brand it">

On **Appearance**, two fields do different jobs and sit next to each other:

- **Gallery name** is internal — how you tell this gallery apart from your others. Visitors never see it.
- **Public title** is the heading on the hall. Left empty, it falls back to the gallery name.

![The Gallery name and Public title fields on the Appearance tab, each with help text explaining that the name is internal and the title is what the hall shows](/images/kb/poster-gallery-name-vs-title.png)

Below them set the **Description**, a **Theme** (visitors can still switch it on the page), an **Accent color** and a **Heading font**, and add a **Logo** and **Banner** — **Use the event logo** and **Use the event background image** reuse what the event already has. A **Gallery sponsor** block (label, logo, link) is there if a sponsor paid for the wall; **Choose an event sponsor** reuses a sponsor's logo and website from their account record.

**Public header preview** shows the result as you type.

![The Public header preview showing the hall's title, poster count and description over the gallery's accent colour](/images/kb/poster-gallery-header-preview.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Decide what visitors can do">

**Behavior** holds the visitor-facing switches: **Search**, **Allow downloads**, **Share actions**, **Bookmarks**, **Add to calendar** and **Ask the author**.

![The Behavior tab's visitor switches — Search, Allow downloads, Share actions, Bookmarks, Add to calendar and Ask the author — each with a one-line explanation](/images/kb/poster-gallery-behavior-toggles.png)

**Attendee voting** lets visitors vote for the posters they rate, and **Show vote counts** decides whether they see the running tally. A public hall has no sign-in, so a vote is tied to the visitor's browser — or to their verified email on a gated gallery. Treat the ranking as a popularity signal, not a ballot.

![The Attendee voting and Show vote counts switches, with the note that a vote is tied to the visitor's browser or their verified email on a gated gallery](/images/kb/poster-gallery-voting-switches.png)

Further down, **Group posters by** (Track, Session or no grouping), **Default sort** and **Filter chips shown to visitors** set how the wall is arranged, and **How visitors see it** sums up the result in a sentence.

![The Group posters by and Filter chips shown to visitors settings, with Track, Format and Session chips selected](/images/kb/poster-gallery-grouping-sort.png)

</Step>

<Step title="Set access, then go Live">

On **Access & Compliance**, choose who can view the gallery (see below), then flip the **Live** switch in the header. **Copy public link** gives you the URL to publish; **Preview** opens the gallery as a visitor sees it.

![The gallery builder header with the gallery name, a Live switch, Copy public link, Preview and a Share & print menu](/images/kb/poster-gallery-header-live.png)

</Step>

</Steps>

<Tip>
The header's **Share & print** menu has a **Download QR** for the whole gallery and **QR labels for poster boards** — one printable label per poster, so the physical poster hall links back to the online one.
</Tip>

## Who can view it

**Who can view this gallery** on **Access & Compliance** offers three modes:

| Access mode | Who gets in |
| --- | --- |
| **Public** | Anyone with the link. Share links still work alongside it for scoped or password-protected access. |
| **Share link** | The public URL is off; only the share links you create work. |
| **Gated** | Visitors verify their email first; optionally restrict to allowed email domains. |

![The Access & Compliance card with Who can view this gallery set to Public, an Embargo until field reading No embargo, and the Require author consent switch](/images/kb/poster-gallery-access-modes.png)

**Share links** are scoped, revocable URLs, each with a name and an optional password — useful for press, faculty or society members. Deactivate one without touching the others.

![The Share links card explaining scoped, revocable URLs with a Name field, an optional Password field and a Create button](/images/kb/poster-gallery-share-links.png)

## Hold posters back until you are ready

Two settings on the same tab decide what is visible and when:

- **Embargo until** holds posters back until a date and time in the event's timezone. **Teaser** shows titles with a countdown; **Hide** shows nothing until the embargo lifts.
- **Require author consent** publishes only posters whose author accepted your consent statement — presented as a required checkbox when they upload a poster in the portal. Before you turn it on, the card tells you how many sessions it would hide.

![The Require author consent switch with a warning that turning it on would hide 3 sessions that have no recorded consent yet](/images/kb/poster-gallery-author-consent.png)

## See what visitors did

The **Analytics** tab covers the last 30 days and exports to CSV.

![Analytics stat cards for Poster opens, Avg. time on poster, Zero-result searches, Active days and Attendee votes](/images/kb/poster-gallery-analytics-stats.png)

**Zero-result searches** is the fastest read on which topics your audience wanted and could not find. Under the charts, **Most viewed posters** ranks by opens and dwell time, and **Most voted posters** ranks by votes when voting is on.

![The Most viewed posters panel ranking posters by opens and average time spent](/images/kb/poster-gallery-analytics-most-viewed.png)

![The Most voted posters panel ranking posters by the number of attendee votes](/images/kb/poster-gallery-analytics-most-voted.png)

**Questions for authors** collects everything submitted from the hall. Nothing is emailed automatically — forward them yourself.

![The Questions for authors panel showing one new question with the asker's name, email and the question text](/images/kb/poster-gallery-questions.png)

**Author engagement reports** emails every author whose poster was viewed with their views, average time spent and questions received. **Preview send** shows what one author would get before anything goes out.

![The Author engagement reports card offering to email every author their views, average time spent and questions received, with a Preview send button](/images/kb/poster-gallery-author-reports.png)

## Draft posters from abstracts

For sessions with no poster file, the Content tab can build one from the abstract text already in Sessionboard. You choose the sessions before anything is created.

![The Create poster drafts card explaining that drafts are built from abstract text, with Create poster drafts and Draft with AI buttons](/images/kb/poster-gallery-create-drafts.png)

Both open the poster builder — a block editor with sections, key findings, charts and figures, a two- or three-column layout and an accent colour. Publishing renders the poster to PDF, files it in the session's Poster slot and adds it to the same pool as uploaded posters.

A session has one current poster. Publishing from the builder replaces an uploaded poster as the current one, and a speaker uploading afterwards replaces the built one; the earlier file stays in the session's version history. Your authors can use the same builder themselves — see [Upload, narrate and build your poster](/participants/upload-a-poster).
