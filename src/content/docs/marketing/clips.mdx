## Why use it

A 45-minute session has three moments worth a feed, and finding them by scrubbing is the job nobody gets to. Clips reads the transcript, proposes the moments, and renders them vertical or square with burned-in captions, so the recording you already paid for becomes the week's social content the day after the talk — without a video editor in the loop.

## Create a clip
Clips are cut from a recording, so start in Media.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a recording with a finished transcript">
    Go to **Marketing → Media** and open the item. Clip suggestions need a completed transcript.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the editor">
    Click **Edit & clip**. The recording opens in the Create video editor: the transcript on the left, the player in the middle, and the **Chapters & clips** panel on the right.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate suggestions">
    Once the transcript is ready, chapters and suggested clips are generated automatically and appear in the panel without a reload. Under **Suggested clips**, click **Generate** on a recording that has none (a pasted transcript, for example), or **Regenerate** to redo them. AI proposes around six moments, each with a title, the quote it is built around and a one-line reason. **Copilot** opens the clip copilot in the Ask panel if you would rather describe the clips you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Preview and adjust">
    **Preview** plays the suggested range; **Trim** opens start and end controls so you can move the boundaries. To cut a video by hand instead, use **Edit by transcript** on the left: **Remove** the lines you don't want (or **Remove fillers** for the ums and ahs), then **Export edited video** stitches what is left.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Export">
    Pick the aspect ratio above the list — **Original**, **Vertical · 9:16** or **Square · 1:1** — and the captions: **No captions**, **Clean**, **Bold**, **Boxed**, or one of the **Animated** styles (colored, scaling, background). Then click **Export** on the clip. Rendering runs in the background and the card shows **Rendering…** until it's done.

    On 9:16 and 1:1, captions are sized to the frame and stay in the bottom band, and a long line splits into two-line captions instead of running up the picture. Captions, 9:16 / 1:1 and the animated styles re-encode the video, and a re-encoded clip is capped at **10 minutes** — **Export** is disabled with the reason when a trimmed window is longer. **Original** with **No captions** is a plain trim and has no length limit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Finished clips appear under **Exported clips** at the bottom of the panel, on the recording's **AI artifacts** tab, and in **Media → Clips**.

<Tip>
Use 9:16 with Bold or Karaoke captions for LinkedIn and other feeds. Most people watch with the sound off, so burned-in captions do the work.
</Tip>

## Audiograms

An audio recording has no picture to clip, so it gets an audiogram instead: a waveform video with burned-in captions and the speaker's name over your brand color. Open an audio file you uploaded to Media and click **Export audiogram** under **Actions**. Set the **Start** and **End** (it starts at the playhead and runs 30 seconds; an audiogram can be up to 10 minutes), then pick a **Shape** — **1:1**, **9:16** or **16:9** — a **Look** (**Brand**, **Dark** or **Light**) and a **Captions** style (**Clean**, **Bold** or **Boxed**). **Speaker name** defaults to the tagged speaker, and **Title** names the result. Click **Export audiogram**: it renders in the background and appears in the clips library as **Audiogram · *recording title***, like any other clip.

## The clips library

**Media → Clips** lists your exported clips as cards with a poster frame, their length and title. The tabs filter by origin — **All**, **AI**, **Yours**, **Imported** — and **Search clips…** finds one by title.

The filter bar narrows the list further:

- **Speaker** — clips where that speaker talks, from the speakers tagged on the source recording's transcript or attributed to the clip.
- **Topic** — clips whose recording or session carries that topic: a topic on the recording, one of the session's taxonomy topics, its **Track**, or one of its **Tags**.
- **Aspect** (the **All sizes** menu) — **Landscape (16:9)**, **Vertical (9:16)** or **Square (1:1)**.
- **Created from** and **Created to** — the date range the clip was exported in.

The Speaker and Topic lists only offer values your clips actually have. When the filters leave nothing, the page says **No clips match these filters**; **Clear filters** resets them.

![The clips library filter bar with the All, AI, Yours and Imported tabs, Search clips, and the All speakers, All topics and All sizes menus beside Created from and Created to date pickers](/images/kb/clips-library-filter-bar.png)

A clip appears here the moment you click **Export**: the card reads **Rendering…** until the file is ready and turns playable on its own, no reload needed, and a render that could not finish shows **Export failed** with the reason. Each card names the recording it was cut from and links back to it. A suggestion that has never been exported only exists in the editor's **Suggested clips** list. Opening a clip shows its own page: the player, the **Source recording** it was cut from, the speaker, the part of the transcript inside the clip (click a timestamp to jump there), and its **Shares** and **Clicks**. **Download** saves the MP4, and **Add to gallery** puts it in one of your content galleries. A clip that is still rendering shows **Rendering…** with **Download** unavailable, and becomes playable on its own. To adjust the cut and re-export, go back to the source recording's editor.

The count above the cards is every clip in the library. Clips load 100 at a time, newest first; when there are more, **Load more** at the bottom of the list brings in the next 100 (it shows how many are loaded so far). The tabs and **Search clips…** look through the clips already loaded.

Deleting a clip moves it to the same **Trash** as recordings, under **Media → Library**; **Restore** puts it back in the clips library.

<Tip>
Search by title is the quickest way back to a particular clip. Every clip you export is filed under **Yours**, whether you cut it by hand or started from an AI suggestion.
</Tip>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How many suggestions do I get?">
    Around six per run, and you can generate again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why can't I generate clips on this recording?">
    It has no finished transcript. Suggestions are chosen from transcript content, so transcription has to complete first.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I add my own captions or branding?">
    Caption style is chosen at export. Logo watermarking and clip styling come from your [brand kit](/marketing/brand-kits). In the editor, **Insert** adds an image or logo (**Add image / logo**), a text callout (**Add text / callout**), a B-roll cutaway (**Add video (B-roll)**) and **Add background audio**. Under **B-roll & audio** you set where each B-roll starts (**Starts from**) and each background track's **Volume**. All of them are burned into single-window exports, including the Animated caption styles. A video with cuts (lines removed in the transcript) can't carry these inserts yet: restore the cut lines or remove the inserts before you export. Background audio must be an MP3, M4A, AAC, WAV or OGG file; the editor refuses any other file when you pick it. **Export edited video** and **Clip** on a transcript selection use the captions shown under **Preview options**, including an Animated style. A video with cuts exports with standard captions instead of an animated style, and the editor tells you when that happens. Corrections you make to the transcript in the editor appear in the burned captions and in the caption preview, and the **Clean**, **Bold** and **Boxed** styles use the speaker names you tagged.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How long does export take?">
    It renders in the background — minutes for a short clip. The card shows **Rendering…** until it's ready.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why was my clip rejected or marked failed?">
    A clip's time range must fall inside the recording — a range past the end of the source is rejected with "Clip range is outside the source duration" before anything renders. A render that would produce an empty file fails with a reason on the clip card instead of appearing ready with a broken download.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is there a maximum clip length?">
    Only when the clip has to be re-encoded. With captions, a 9:16 or 1:1 crop, an animated style or overlays, a clip can be up to **10 minutes**; the Export button tells you when a window is longer. A plain trim — **Original** aspect with **No captions** — reuses the source video and can be any length, up to the whole recording. The exception is a WebM recording: an MP4 can't hold its video format, so even a plain trim of a WebM is converted to MP4 and the 10-minute cap applies to it (exporting the whole recording unedited still works at any length).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
