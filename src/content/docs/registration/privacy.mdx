**Registration → Settings → Privacy** controls how the event handles registrants' personal data. It covers the consent registrants give on the form, the record of that consent, requests to see or delete a person's data, and how long the event keeps registrants' details.

## Why use it

Data-protection laws such as the GDPR expect you to show what each person agreed to and when. They also expect you to answer a request for a copy of someone's data, remove it when asked, and not keep it longer than you need. With Privacy, those answers come from the registration itself, so you don't have to rebuild them from emails and spreadsheets.

## Choose a privacy mode

Each event has one of two modes:

![The Privacy panel set to Full GDPR, with the Data Protection Officer's name and email, the data-processing consent text, a required consent question and the Anonymise registrants switch](/images/kb/registration-privacy-settings.png)

- **Basic** (the default): the form asks what it asks today. You can still add consent questions.
- **Full GDPR**: also adds:
  - an explicit, unticked consent to the processing of the registrant's data, which they must tick to register;
  - your Data Protection Officer on the form and in registration emails;
  - the consent log;
  - data exports and anonymisation;
  - retention.

<Steps>
1. Go to **Registration → Settings** and scroll to **Privacy**.
2. Under **Privacy mode**, choose **Full GDPR**.
3. Fill in the **Data Protection Officer**: a name and an email at least, plus a phone and postal address if you like.
4. Write the **Data-processing consent**, for example: *I agree that the organizer processes my registration data as described in the privacy notice.*
5. Click **Save privacy settings**.
</Steps>

You need the Registration permission **Can manage registration settings** to change these settings. See [Registration roles and permissions](/registration/roles-and-permissions).

## Add consent questions

Consent questions are extra checkboxes on every registration form, for example *I agree to the code of conduct* or *Share my badge scan with sponsors*.

<Steps>
1. Under **Consent questions**, click **Add consent question** and write the wording.
2. Tick **Required** if registrants must agree before they can register. Leave it unticked for an optional question.
3. Click **Save privacy settings**.
</Steps>

Every time you change a question's wording, it becomes a new version (**v2**, **v3** and so on). The consent log keeps the exact wording each registrant saw, so a change never rewrites what someone agreed to earlier. If you remove a question, it disappears from the form but stays in the log.

<Note>
The person registering answers the consent questions for the colleagues they register in a group, in the same way they tick the main consent box for them.
</Note>

## The consent log

In full GDPR mode, the **Consent log** under **Privacy** lists every consent given, declined or withdrawn. Each row shows:

- when it happened;
- who it was;
- which consent it was, and the exact wording version;
- the answer;
- where it came from: the registration form, the signed-in Site, the attendee portal, staff (**added by staff**) or an import, with the IP address.

The log records only what the person was actually asked:

- **Personalised recommendations** is on by default and the form does not ask about it, so it has no row unless they turn it off. Their consents show it as **On by default (not asked)**.
- When staff add a registration with **Add registration**, nobody asked the registrant, so each consent is logged as **Not asked (added by staff)** with the staff member's name. It is never logged as given.

The log also holds the consents registration already collected before this page existed: the main consent box, the marketing opt-in and the SMS opt-in. Registrations made earlier show a **backfill** row for each.

Open a registrant in **Registration → Registrants** to see their own consents under **Consent**.

## Registrants can withdraw consent

Registrants see a **Your privacy** section in two places:

![The Consent section of a registrant's drawer, with processing of registration data given, marketing emails not given and personalised recommendations On by default (not asked)](/images/kb/registration-registrant-consent.png)

- the **Change or cancel your registration** page, from the link in every registration email;
- the attendee portal on the Site, on **Home** and on **Edit registration**.

It lists each consent with the date it was given and its wording version. Next to each consent that is still given, a **Withdraw** button takes it back once they confirm. For personalised recommendations, which are on by default, the button is **Turn off**.

- **Marketing emails**: they stop being opted in to marketing.
- **Registration text messages**: they are no longer texted.
- **Processing of my registration data**: their data stops being used for recommendations and attendee insights.
- **A consent question**: the withdrawal is logged.

Withdrawing does not cancel the registration. Each withdrawal appears in the consent log as **Withdrawn**, with where it came from. In full GDPR mode the section also shows your Data Protection Officer.

When you use **View as registrant**, you see the same section, read-only.

## Export a person's data

When someone asks for a copy of their data (a data subject access request):

<Steps>
1. Under **Privacy → A person's data**, enter their email.
2. Click **Export data**.
</Steps>

You get one JSON file containing:

- their registrations, including voided and merged ones;
- their answers;
- the consent log;
- their orders, with line items, payments, invoices and stored payment methods;
- staff notes;
- text messages;
- check-ins, badge prints, housing and seating.

It also includes orders that the email bought for other people. Card numbers and payment tokens are never included.

To export a single registration, open it in **Registrants** and click **Export data** under **Consent**.

## Anonymise registrants

Anonymising removes a person's personal data and keeps what your finance records need. It cannot be undone.

<Steps>
1. Under **Privacy → A person's data**, enter their email and click **Anonymise…**. You first see exactly what will change, and nothing is removed yet.
2. Click **Anonymise** and confirm.
</Steps>

To anonymise one registration, open it in **Registrants** and click **Anonymise** under **Consent**. To anonymise several at once, select them in **Registrants** and choose **More → Anonymise…**. You see the dry run first, then type **ANONYMISE** to confirm.

| Removed | Kept |
|---|---|
| Name, email, company, job title and badge ID | The registration itself: its ticket, registrant type, status and check-in |
| Answers to the form's questions | Every order amount, line item, payment, refund and invoice amount |
| Staff notes, text messages and the registrant's links | Tax and VAT on orders and invoices |
| The purchaser's name, email, phone and street address on orders and invoices | The purchaser's company, VAT number, PO number and country, kept as tax evidence |
| Stored cards and bank accounts (also removed from the payment gateway) | Credit balances |
| Housing deliveries, and the person's event app profile and saved items | Check-ins, badge print counts and seating |
| IP addresses in the consent and activity logs | The consent log rows, as proof of what was agreed |

An anonymised registration shows **Anonymised** in the registrant drawer and in reports, so totals still add up. Its **Edit** button is unavailable, because the personal details cannot be added back. Voided and merged registrations are included. Onsite, an anonymised registration is not found at the desk, in the Attend app's staff mode or on a kiosk, and it cannot be checked in or given a badge. A batch badge print leaves it out.

<Tip>
Deleting a person in **Attendees** and an erasure request sent through the API anonymise their registrations in the same way. Their orders and payments are kept.
</Tip>

## Anonymise everyone after the event (retention)

In full GDPR mode you can let Sessionboard anonymise every registrant once you no longer need their details.

<Steps>
1. Under **Privacy**, turn on **Anonymise registrants** and enter the number of months after the event ends, for example **12**.
2. Click **Save privacy settings**.
3. Under **Retention**, click **Preview (dry run)**. You see the date the period ends and what it will change, for example "Will anonymise 12 registrations". Nothing is removed by the preview.
</Steps>

Once the period has passed, Sessionboard anonymises the event's remaining registrants overnight. It runs once a day and never repeats work. Each run, including each preview, is listed under **History** and in the event's **History**. To start it right away once it is due, click **Run now**. Until it is due, **Run now** is unavailable and says from which date it can be used. If today's run has already happened, **Run now** tells you so, with how many registrations it anonymised, and starts nothing.

## Who can do what

| Task | Permission |
|---|---|
| Change the privacy mode, Data Protection Officer, consent questions and retention | **Can manage registration settings** |
| Export a person's data, anonymise, and run retention | **Can export and anonymise registrant data** |
| Read the consent log | Either of the two |

Every role that can manage registration settings starts with **Can export and anonymise registrant data**. To keep these tools with your Data Protection Officer, turn the permission off for other roles in **Event Team → Permissions → Registration**.
