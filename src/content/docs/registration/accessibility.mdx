Sessionboard builds the public registration pages to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, level AA. These pages are the registration form, the confirmation page, the attendee portal, the exhibit page and the exhibitor portal. Some choices you make for an event still affect how accessible its registration is. This page covers what the product does for you and what to check for your event.

## Why use it

Sessionboard's registration forms and attendee portals are built to work for every registrant, including those who use screen readers, keyboard-only navigation or high-contrast display settings. Labels, errors and help text are programmatically linked to their controls so assistive technology reads the right context. Focus order, visible focus rings and sufficient colour contrast are maintained even when an event applies a light or white theme.

## What registrants get

![The first step of a public registration form in the event's accent colour, with the numbered steps, a language picker and the Member, Non-member and Trainee prices](/images/kb/registration-form-welcome-prices.png)

- **Keyboard use.** Every step of the form works with Tab, Shift+Tab, Space and Enter. When a step changes, focus moves to the new step's heading. When you press **Continue** with a required answer missing, focus moves to that question.
- **Screen readers.** Each question reads its label, its help text, its error message and whether it is required. The step list, prices, upload progress and promo-code results are announced.
- **Your Site colours, kept readable.** If your Site's primary or accent colour is very light (white, off-white or a bright yellow), selected options, the current step and the keyboard focus ring are drawn in a darker shade of the same colour, so they stay visible on the white form.
- **Phones and zoom.** The form fits a 320-pixel-wide screen and 400% browser zoom without scrolling sideways.
- **Languages.** The form sets the page language, so screen readers pronounce it correctly, and Arabic reads right to left.
- **Sign-in codes.** Emailed codes can be pasted, or filled in automatically by the phone.

## What to check for your event

- **Write questions as plain labels.** Put the question in the label and any instructions in the help text. Don't put the only instructions in placeholder text, which disappears when someone starts typing.
- **Give hold windows enough time.** When a waitlist offer or a booth hold has a deadline, people who use assistive technology may need longer. A hold of 20 hours or more avoids the problem.
- **Offer more than one kiosk lookup.** On a [self check-in kiosk](/onsite/kiosk), turn on scanning the wallet pass, or the emailed six-digit code, alongside last name and confirmation number, so that nobody has to copy a number from memory.
- **Use meaningful images.** Your Site logo is read aloud by its organization name. Don't put essential information only in the hero image.
